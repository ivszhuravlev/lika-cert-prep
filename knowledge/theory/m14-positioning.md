# Module 14. Patient Positioning

Original notes for MDCB objective II.2.

## Patient Positioning

**MDCB:** II.2, II.3

**What the exam expects**

You choose a reproducible position for the site, you know why arms, bladder, and prone versus supine are in the setup note, and you can tell a reference tattoo from an isocenter. Indexing and laterality are part of positioning. Motion management hardware is the next module; the position it attaches to is this one.

**Core facts**

The position is a prescription. The scan, the plan, and every fraction use the same one. “Close” is not the same position if an arm is in a tangent or the bladder is empty.

Supine, head-first, is the default for head and neck, thorax, abdomen, and pelvis because it is stable and the lasers see the anterior and lateral marks. Feet-first is a different DICOM orientation and is used when the gantry cannot clear the patient’s head or the immobilization for a lower-extremity site. Write which one it is.

Prone is chosen for a reason, not for variety. Prone breast can drop the breast through a board and move the chest wall. Prone pelvis with a belly board lets small bowel fall out of a posterior field. The belly board is not a supine device. A prone plan treated supine puts the belly board’s opening in the wrong place and puts the beams on the wrong surface.

Arms. Thorax, breast, lung, and upper abdomen: arms up, out of the oblique and lateral beams, usually on a wing board. Arms on the chest sit in the tangent. Head and neck: arms at the sides, shoulders pulled down so the neck beams are not treating the shoulder, mask on a headrest. A mask scanned with the chin extended is not the same neck as a mask with the chin flexed.

Pelvis. Knee sponge and a foot band reduce rotation. Prostate and many gynecologic protocols scan with a full bladder and an empty rectum so bowel is displaced and the prostate is in a known place. The protocol in the chart is the protocol. A full-bladder plan treated with an empty bladder moves small bowel into the high dose and moves the target. Some rectal protocols want a different filling. Match the note.

Perineum, vulva, and anus may be supine frog-leg or prone, depending on the clinic’s technique, so that skin folds are opened and the anatomy is accessible. Skin folds self-bolus. Dose to the skin inside a fold is higher than the skin dose on a flat phantom. Tape or a position that opens the fold is a dosimetry decision.

Breast board angle, headrest letter, vac-lok, and alpha cradle are indexed to the couch. The same notch every day is the reproducibility you can actually check. A device that slides is not immobilization.

Marks. Tattoos or BBs are a reference point the lasers can find. They are not automatically the isocenter. The isocenter is often shifted from that reference. Module 16 is the arithmetic. Positioning’s job is to make the reference point reachable and stable: not on a sloping skin surface if you can avoid it, not under a fold, documented anterior and lateral.

Laterality. The patient’s right is the patient’s right in the bunker and on the scan. Heart is on the patient’s left. A flipped image treats the wrong breast or the wrong lung. Say left and right in patient language. “Table left” depends on which end of the couch you are standing at.

Couch coordinates and patient coordinates differ by a sign when you move the couch to bring a point to isocenter. That sign is a shift issue. The positioning issue is to record the position in words a second person can reproduce: supine, head-first, arms up, wing board notch 3, headrest B, knee sponge, full bladder.

Bolus and the position interact. Bolus that does not touch the skin is an air gap. The contour correction you think you applied is then wrong, and the skin dose is not the bolus dose. Build the position so the bolus can sit in contact if the plan requires contact.

**Traps**

Belly board used supine. Arms down for a tangential breast plan. Tattoos treated as the isocenter with no shift checked. Prone simulation and supine treatment. Full-bladder instructions left off the setup note. Left and right taken from the screen orientation after a flip. An indexed device locked in a different notch because “it still fits.”

**Sources**

Khan and Gibbons, *The Physics of Radiation Therapy*, setup geometry chapters. AAPM TG-66 for simulation position and indexing. Public blueprint II.2.

## Patient Positioning Mini-Quiz

This quiz checks supine versus prone indications, arm position for breast and thorax, bladder and rectum instructions as part of the position, indexing of the immobilization, and the difference between a reference tattoo and the isocenter. The traps are a belly board in a supine plan, arms in the tangent, treating the tattoo as isocenter, a left-right flip, and a full-bladder scan delivered empty.

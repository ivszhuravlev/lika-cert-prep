# Module 22. Cross-Sectional Anatomy

The exam shows an axial slice, or describes one, and expects the structure’s name. Practice until you can name these landmarks with no picture in front of you. Contouring atlases settle border disputes. Dose limits are not in this module.

## Anatomy List

**MDCB objectives:** III.4, III.2

**What the exam expects**

You can identify, on a described axial slice, the carina, the four cardiac chambers, liver versus spleen, the kidneys, aorta versus IVC, prostate, rectum, bladder, femoral heads, parotid, and larynx, and you can place the brachial plexus region. You can also place the lens, optic nerves, chiasm, pituitary, brainstem, cochlea, and hippocampus; the esophagus at its cervical, left-atrial, and diaphragmatic levels; the trachea and the lung lobes; the conus and the cauda equina; the penile bulb; small bowel versus sigmoid; and the breast on the chest wall. You use radiologic orientation: head-first supine, axial image viewed from the feet, the patient’s right on the image left, and anterior at the top.

**Core facts**

**Carina.** The trachea splits into the right and left main bronchi at about the T4–T5 level, behind the sternal angle. On an axial slice at that level the airway changes from one round black lumen to two. The aortic arch is just above this. The pulmonary arteries cross near it. The esophagus is posterior, usually slightly left, between the airway and the vertebral body. Below the carina a mediastinal node or a tumor can sit in the subcarinal space between the bronchi.

**Heart, on an axial slice through the chambers.** Name each chamber from where it sits.

- The most anterior chamber, immediately behind the sternum, is the right ventricle.
- The patient’s right heart border is the right atrium. The superior and inferior vena cava enter it.
- The patient’s left border and the apex are the left ventricle, with the thick wall.
- The most posterior chamber is the left atrium. The pulmonary veins enter it. The esophagus and the descending aorta lie behind it, so a hot spot “next to the esophagus at the left atrium” is a posterior-heart problem, not a right-ventricle problem.
- The left anterior descending coronary artery runs in the anterior interventricular groove, on the front of the heart. Left breast tangents approach this region. A numeric heart or LAD limit is protocol-specific; confirm it in the named cardiac constraint.

**Liver and spleen.** On an upper-abdominal slice the liver is the large homogeneous organ in the patient’s right upper quadrant, under the right hemidiaphragm. The intrahepatic IVC is embedded in the posterior liver. The spleen is the smaller organ in the patient’s left upper quadrant, posterolateral, against the left hemidiaphragm and the ribs, lateral and posterior to the stomach. Stomach air sits medial and anterior to the spleen. The spleen is superior and lateral to the left kidney. If only one of the two is in view and it fills the right upper quadrant, it is liver.

**Kidneys.** Both are retroperitoneal, against the psoas, from about T12 to L3. The hilum faces medially. The right kidney usually sits lower than the left because the liver occupies the space above it. The left kidney sits closer to the spleen and the stomach. Adrenal glands cap the upper poles anteromedially. A para-aortic nodal volume at the renal hilum is medial to the kidneys, not inside them.

**Aorta and IVC.** Side of the spine and wall thickness separate them.

- The abdominal aorta is the patient’s left of midline: round, thick-walled, just in front of the vertebral body. It bifurcates into the common iliac arteries at about L4.
- The IVC is the patient’s right of midline: oval, thinner wall, and more anterior than the aorta at renal levels. It is formed from the common iliac veins at about L5 and, higher up, runs in a groove through the posterior liver.
- In the chest, the ascending aorta is central and anterior, the arch passes from right-anterior to left-posterior above the carina, and the descending aorta is a round structure on the patient’s left of the spine. The esophagus is anterior and usually slightly right of the descending aorta as they pass behind the heart.

A vessel inside the liver is the IVC or a hepatic vein, not the aorta. A round thick vessel hard against the left side of the lumbar spine is the aorta.

**Prostate, rectum, bladder, femoral heads.** On a male pelvic slice:

- The bladder is anterior and midline. Urine is fluid density. Filling changes the dome and moves the bowel.
- The prostate is inferior to the bladder, midline, surrounding the prostatic urethra, anterior to the rectum and posterior to the pubic symphysis.
- The seminal vesicles sit just above the prostate, posterior to the bladder and anterior to the rectum, often as a bow-tie on the axial slice.
- The rectum is posterior midline, often containing air or stool, wrapped by mesorectal fat. It is the organ immediately behind the prostate.
- The femoral heads are the round bones lateral and slightly posterior, seated in the acetabulum, at and below the level of the prostate. They are the bony OAR on a prostate or rectal plan. The pubic symphysis is the anterior midline bone. On a pelvic contour, the femoral head starts at the top of the head and stops at the bottom of the lesser trochanter. The ischial tuberosities are a lower bony landmark. They are not the inferior border of the femoral head.

**Parotid.** At about C1, the parotid is the soft tissue lateral and posterior to the mandibular ramus, in front of the mastoid and the ear, wrapping the posterior ramus. It is subcutaneous on the lateral face. The facial nerve runs through it and is not seen as a line on CT; the retromandibular vein is the imaging landmark used to discuss superficial versus deep lobe. The submandibular gland is a different gland: more anterior and inferior, under the body of the mandible, above the hyoid, in nodal level IB.

**Larynx.** An air-filled structure in the anterior midline neck, roughly C3 to C6.

- The epiglottis is the highest, a thin soft-tissue curve at the base of the tongue.
- The thyroid cartilage is the shield, open posteriorly, at the level of the true vocal cords. The cords are soft tissue at the edge of the airway slit, with the arytenoids posterior.
- The cricoid cartilage is immediately below the thyroid cartilage and is the only complete ring. On an axial slice the posterior lamina is broad, like a signet.
- The pyriform sinuses are lateral air pockets beside the larynx and belong to the hypopharynx, not to the glottis.
- False cords sit above the ventricles; true cords sit at the thyroid-cartilage level. A “cord” contour drawn at the hyoid is too high.

**Brachial plexus region.** Roots leave the neural foramina from C5 through T1, pass between the anterior and middle scalene muscles, cross above the first rib with the subclavian artery, and form cords around the axillary artery. On an axial slice through the supraclavicular fossa, the plexus is the soft-tissue bundle between the scalenes, posterior to the clavicle and the sternocleidomastoid, above the lung apex. A low-neck or apical-lung field can cross it. The contour borders used for planning are the RTOG brachial plexus atlas. Confirm borders there. A numeric plexus limit is protocol-specific (conventional versus stereotactic); confirm it in the named source.

**Intracranial OARs, head-first supine, viewed from the feet.** Anterior is at the top. The patient’s right is image left.

- **Lens.** The small biconvex structure in the anterior globe, behind the cornea and in front of the vitreous. The patient’s right lens is image left.
- **Optic nerves.** Each nerve leaves the posterior pole of the globe, runs through the orbital fat to the orbital apex and the optic canal, then runs posteromedially to the chiasm. On an orbital slice the nerve is the soft-tissue cord behind the globe. The chiasm is intracranial.
- **Chiasm.** The midline band in the suprasellar cistern, superior to the pituitary, anterior to the midbrain. The nerves enter from anterolateral. The tracts leave posterolateral.
- **Pituitary.** Midline soft tissue in the sella. The sphenoid sinus is anterior. The cavernous sinuses, each with a cavernous internal carotid artery, are lateral. The dorsum sellae, the basilar artery, and the pons are posterior. The chiasm is on the next slice up.
- **Brainstem.** Midline posterior fossa. The midbrain, with the cerebral peduncles, is at the suprasellar level, and the interpeduncular cistern is anterior to it. The pons is lower and bulkier, posterior to the clivus and the basilar artery and anterior to the fourth ventricle. The cerebellum is posterior to the fourth ventricle. The medulla is smaller, at the foramen magnum, and continues as the spinal cord.
- **Cochlea.** The spiral in the petrous temporal bone, lateral to the brainstem, anterior to the vestibule and the semicircular canals, medial to the middle-ear cavity, and lateral to the internal auditory canal. The patient’s right cochlea is image left. It is bone-window anatomy, not a node and not part of the pons.
- **Hippocampus.** Paired medial temporal lobe, in the floor of the temporal horn, lateral to the ambient cistern and the midbrain. The amygdala is the more anterior medial temporal gray matter. The patient’s left hippocampus is image right.

**Esophagus.** It begins at the lower border of the cricoid, about C6. In the neck it lies immediately posterior to the trachea, usually slightly left of midline, anterior to the vertebral body. In the chest the aortic arch and the left main bronchus cross it at about T4–T5. It then passes behind the left atrium. There it is anterior to the descending aorta, usually a little to the patient’s right of that aorta, while remaining slightly left of the midline. It leaves the thorax through the esophageal hiatus at about T10, slightly left of midline, and has a short abdominal segment to the cardia. A contour that starts at the hyoid, about C3, is too high.

**Trachea and the lung lobes.** The trachea begins at the lower border of the cricoid, about C6, and ends at the carina, about T4–T5. It is anterior to the esophagus. The right main bronchus is shorter, wider, and more vertical. The left main bronchus is longer and more horizontal and passes under the aortic arch.

The right lung has three lobes. The horizontal fissure, at about the right hilum, separates the right upper lobe above from the right middle lobe below. Below that fissure, the lung against the right heart border is the right middle lobe. Posterior to the oblique fissure is the right lower lobe. The left lung has two lobes, separated by an oblique fissure. The lingula is the anteroinferior part of the left upper lobe, against the left heart border. It is not a middle lobe. Posterior to the left oblique fissure is the left lower lobe. Above the clavicles, the apical lung on each side is upper lobe.

**Spinal cord and cauda equina.** In an adult the cord ends as the conus medullaris at about L1–L2. Below the conus, the lumbar and sacral nerve roots continue inside the thecal sac as the cauda equina. The thecal sac ends at about S2. On a thoracic slice the cord is the single soft-tissue structure in the canal, surrounded by CSF. On a lumbar slice below L2 the canal contains the cauda equina. The brachial plexus has already left the cervical foramina and is not this lumbar sac.

**Vertebral landmarks.** These levels name a slice when the stem gives a landmark instead of a picture. A skin dermatome is not a vertebral number. The umbilicus moves with habitus, so the iliac crests are the landmark used for L4.

| Landmark | Vertebral level |
| --- | --- |
| Hyoid | C3 |
| Thyroid cartilage, at true-cord level | C4–C5 |
| Cricoid; trachea and esophagus begin | C6 |
| Suprasternal (jugular) notch | about T2–T3 |
| Sternal angle; carina behind it | T4–T5 disc |
| IVC through the diaphragm | T8 |
| Esophagus through the diaphragm | T10 |
| Aorta through the diaphragm; celiac trunk | T12, celiac region T12–L1 |
| Conus medullaris in the adult | about L1–L2 |
| Umbilicus | about the L3–L4 disc in a slim adult; dermatome T10 is not this vertebra |
| Iliac crests; aortic bifurcation | about L4 |
| Common iliac veins join to form the IVC | about L5 |

**Penile bulb.** On a slice inferior to the prostate apex and the urogenital diaphragm, the penile bulb is the midline expansion of the corpus spongiosum. The urethra runs through it. The crura of the corpora cavernosa lie lateral along the ischiopubic rami and do not contain the urethra. The bulb is anterior to the anal canal and inferior to the pubic symphysis. The seminal vesicles stay on the slice above the prostate. The bulb is not the prostate, the rectum, or a femoral head.

**Small bowel and sigmoid.** Jejunum and ileum are mobile mesenteric loops in the central abdomen and pelvis. Their folds, the valvulae conniventes, cross the full lumen, and the loops move with filling and from day to day. They often lie above the bladder. The duodenum is the exception in position: a C-shaped, mostly retroperitoneal loop around the head of the pancreas, on the patient’s right. The sigmoid is the larger-caliber colon of the left iliac fossa. It has a mesentery, and its haustra do not cross the full lumen. It leads into the rectum. The rectum is the fixed posterior midline structure in the sacral hollow, directly behind the prostate on a male slice.

**Breast and chest wall.** On a head-first supine axial slice viewed from the feet, the patient’s left breast is image right. Breast tissue lies anterior to pectoralis major, from about the second rib to the sixth, from the lateral sternal edge toward the mid-axillary line. The axillary tail extends superolaterally toward the axilla. Pectoralis major is the muscle immediately behind the breast, from the clavicle, sternum, and ribs toward the humerus. Pectoralis minor lies deep to it. Deep to those muscles are the ribs and intercostal muscles, then the lung. Serratus anterior is the lateral chest-wall muscle. The chest wall is the muscle and the ribs. The lung is deep to the ribs. The left breast overlies the region of the left ventricle. Internal mammary nodes lie just lateral to the sternum and are not inside the breast mound.

**Slices to rehearse with no image.** Name each one with the picture covered.

1. **Carina.** Two bronchi, esophagus posterior, descending aorta on the patient’s left, pulmonary vessels, no liver yet or only the dome. The heart chambers appear as you move inferiorly: right ventricle anterior, left atrium posterior.
2. **Upper abdomen.** Liver on the patient’s right with the IVC in it, spleen posterolateral on the patient’s left, stomach air between them, aorta round on the left of the spine, right kidney lower than the left as you scroll down.
3. **Male prostate level.** Bladder anterior if you are slightly high, prostate midline, rectum posterior, seminal vesicles on the slice just above the prostate, femoral heads lateral, pubis anterior.
4. **Orbits, sella, and temporal bone.** Lens in the anterior globe. Optic nerve from the posterior globe through the orbital fat. Chiasm midline above the sella and anterior to the midbrain. Pituitary in the sella with the cavernous carotids lateral. Cochlea in the petrous bone, lateral to the pons. Hippocampus in the medial temporal lobe beside the midbrain. Image left is the patient’s right.
5. **Esophagus and the lobes.** Esophagus posterior to the trachea from C6, behind the left atrium, through the diaphragm at T10. On the right, the middle lobe is anterior against the heart below the horizontal fissure, and the lower lobe is posterior to the oblique fissure. On the left, the lingula is anterior and belongs to the upper lobe. The lower lobe is posterior.
6. **Lumbar canal and the male perineum.** The conus ends about L1–L2. Below it the canal holds the cauda equina. At L4 the aorta has bifurcated. Below the prostate apex the penile bulb is midline between the crura and anterior to the anal canal. Central loops with full-lumen folds are small bowel. The fixed posterior midline bowel is the rectum.
7. **Breast, axilla, and caudal neck.** Breast tissue is anterior to pectoralis major. Axillary level I is lateral to pectoralis minor, level II is behind it, and level III is medial to it, still below the clavicle. Internal mammary nodes are just lateral to the sternum. The fossa above the clavicle is the supraclavicular caudal neck. The plexus in that fossa is the nerve bundle between the scalenes.

**Traps**

- Calling the most posterior chamber the right ventricle. The posterior chamber is the left atrium. The anterior chamber is the right ventricle.
- Swapping the great vessels. Aorta is patient’s left and round. IVC is patient’s right and oval.
- Placing the right kidney higher than the left as a rule. The right kidney is usually the lower one.
- Calling the submandibular gland the parotid because both are lateral. The parotid is at the ramus and the ear. The submandibular gland is under the mandibular body.
- Drawing femoral heads as pelvic nodes. Nodes follow vessels. Femoral heads are bone.
- Putting the brachial plexus inside the spinal canal. It has already left the foramina and sits in the scalene gap and the supraclavicular fossa.
- Naming the lens as the optic nerve. The lens is anterior in the globe. The nerve leaves the posterior pole.
- Putting the chiasm in the sella. The pituitary is in the sella. The chiasm is superior to it, in the suprasellar cistern, anterior to the midbrain.
- Placing the hippocampus on the midline. It is medial temporal lobe, lateral to the midbrain. The patient’s left hippocampus is image right.
- Starting the esophagus at the hyoid. It starts at the cricoid, about C6, and it crosses the diaphragm at about T10.
- Calling the lingula a third lobe. The left lung has two lobes. The lingula belongs to the left upper lobe.
- Drawing the spinal cord through the lower lumbar canal. In an adult the cord ends about L1–L2. Below that is the cauda equina.
- Reading dermatome T10 as the vertebral level of the umbilicus. Vertebral T10 is the esophageal hiatus. The umbilicus is about L3–L4, and the iliac crests mark L4.
- Calling the penile bulb the prostate. The bulb is inferior to the apex, between the crura, anterior to the anal canal.
- Calling every central pelvic loop the rectum. Folds that cross the full lumen are small bowel. The rectum is the fixed posterior midline structure in the sacral hollow.

**Sources**

- Standard cross-sectional atlases used for radiotherapy contouring
- Consensus head-and-neck OAR atlases, for the borders of chiasm, cochlea, and hippocampus
- RTOG brachial plexus contouring atlas, for the borders of that OAR
- ICRU 50 and 62, for the difference between the organ you just named and the PRV drawn around it

## Lymph Node Anatomy (Only if you have time)

**MDCB objectives:** III.4, III.2

**What the exam expects**

You can place neck levels I through V and the pelvic nodal chains from a description of the vessels and the muscles around them. Level VI is the trap that is not part of I–V. Inguinal nodes are not a routine prostate chain. You can also separate axillary levels I–III from the internal mammary chain, retropharyngeal nodes from level II, the supraclavicular fossa from the superior mediastinum, hilar nodes from subcarinal nodes, and celiac nodes from porta hepatis.

**Core facts**

Neck levels follow the Robbins classification and the consensus contouring atlases. Borders below are the ones used to recognize a level, not a substitute for the atlas on a real contour.

- **Level IA**, submental: midline, between the anterior bellies of the digastric muscles, from the mandible down to the hyoid.
- **Level IB**, submandibular: lateral to IA, around the submandibular gland, from the mandible to the hyoid, anterior to the posterior edge of that gland.
- **Level II**, upper jugular: from the skull base down to the hyoid, around the upper internal jugular vein. **IIA** is anterior and medial to the spinal accessory nerve. **IIB** is posterior to that nerve, toward the sternocleidomastoid. The posterior edge of II is not the trapezius; that is level V.
- **Level III**, middle jugular: hyoid down to the bottom of the cricoid, still along the internal jugular vein, deep to the sternocleidomastoid.
- **Level IV**, lower jugular: cricoid down to the clavicle, along the lower internal jugular vein.
- **Level V**, posterior triangle: posterior to the sternocleidomastoid and anterior to the trapezius. **VA** is from the skull base to the cricoid. **VB** is from the cricoid to the clavicle.

**Level VI** is the anterior compartment between the carotid arteries, from the hyoid to the suprasternal notch: prelaryngeal (Delphian), pretracheal, and paratracheal nodes. It is not level I, II, III, IV, or V. A stem that says “midline, between the carotids, in front of the trachea” is VI.

Superior mediastinal nodes below the sternal notch are sometimes called level VII. Do not pull them up into level IV just because they are near the clavicle.

Pelvic and abdominal chains follow the vessels:

- **Para-aortic**: beside the abdominal aorta, from about the renal vessels down to the aortic bifurcation. Relevant for cervix, endometrium, and seminoma when the protocol includes them.
- **Common iliac**: along the common iliac vessels, from the bifurcation to the split into internal and external iliac.
- **External iliac**: anterolateral pelvis, along the external iliac vessels, toward the inguinal ligament.
- **Internal iliac** (hypogastric): posterior and medial, along the internal iliac vessels on the pelvic sidewall.
- **Obturator**: low on the sidewall, medial to the obturator internus, included with the internal and external chains in a typical pelvic nodal CTV.
- **Presacral**: midline, anterior to the sacrum, between the common iliacs.
- **Inguinal**: in the groin, below the inguinal ligament, along the femoral vessels. Include them for vulva, distal vagina, anal margin, and some penile cases. They are not part of a standard prostate nodal volume.

A prostate nodal CTV, when nodes are treated, follows the obturator and the external, internal, and common iliac vessels, plus presacral nodes when the protocol asks. It does not follow the femoral heads and it does not drop into the groin.

**Axilla and internal mammary nodes.** Breast nodal levels use pectoralis minor. They are not neck levels I–V.

- **Axillary level I** is lateral to the lateral border of pectoralis minor, along the axillary vessels in the low axilla, out toward the latissimus dorsi.
- **Axillary level II** lies behind pectoralis minor, between its medial and lateral borders. Interpectoral (Rotter) nodes, between pectoralis major and minor, are often grouped with level II.
- **Axillary level III** is medial to the medial border of pectoralis minor. This is the infraclavicular, or apical, group. It is still below the clavicle.
- **Internal mammary nodes** follow the internal thoracic vessels, mostly in the first three intercostal spaces, just lateral to the sternum, deep to the anterior chest wall and anterior to the pleura. They are not in the axilla.

**Retropharyngeal nodes.** The lateral retropharyngeal nodes lie behind the pharyngeal wall and in front of the longus colli, from the skull base toward the hyoid. They are medial to the internal carotid artery. The node of Rouvière is the high lateral retropharyngeal node at about C1. Level II is lateral to that carotid border and surrounds the internal jugular vein. A node medial to the carotid at C1 is not level IIA. Medial retropharyngeal nodes sit closer to the midline and are inconstant.

**Supraclavicular nodes.** These are the caudal neck, in the fossa above the clavicle, over the lung apex. In the neck atlas, levels IV and VB reach this fossa and stop at the clavicle. In breast planning that same fossa is the supraclavicular region. It lies superior to the clavicle, so it is not axillary level III. Superior mediastinal nodes below the sternal notch are not supraclavicular and are not level IV. The brachial plexus crosses this fossa between the scalenes. That bundle is nerve, not a node.

**Mediastinal and hilar nodes.** Mediastinal nodes are central, between the lungs, around the trachea, the carina, the esophagus, and the great vessels. Subcarinal nodes, the usual station 7, sit between the main bronchi under the carina. Aortopulmonary-window nodes, the usual station 5, sit on the patient’s left, under the aortic arch and above the left pulmonary artery. Hilar nodes, the usual station 10, sit at the lung root, where the main bronchus and the pulmonary vessels enter the lung, lateral to the subcarinal space. A node along a lobar bronchus inside the lung is intrapulmonary. The numbered IASLC map is the reference when a station border is in dispute.

**Celiac nodes and porta hepatis.** Celiac nodes cluster anterior to the aorta at the celiac trunk, about T12, around the left gastric, common hepatic, and splenic arteries. The superior mesenteric artery arises just below, at about L1. This celiac cluster is superior to the para-aortic chain that runs from the renal vessels to the bifurcation. Porta hepatis nodes follow the portal vein, the hepatic artery, and the bile duct in the hepatoduodenal ligament, at the liver hilum on the patient’s right. At the porta the portal vein is posterior, the bile duct is anterior and to the patient’s right, and the proper hepatic artery is anterior and to the patient’s left. The portal vein is formed by the splenic vein and the superior mesenteric vein behind the neck of the pancreas, then enters the liver at the hilum. The vessel embedded in the posterior liver is the IVC.

**Traps**

- Numbering the posterior triangle as level IIB all the way to the trapezius. IIB stops at the posterior sternocleidomastoid. Behind that muscle is level V.
- Calling every low-neck node level IV. Posterior low neck is VB. Anterior tracheal midline is VI.
- Including inguinal nodes on a routine prostate plan.
- Contouring pelvic nodes on the bone instead of on the vessels.
- Swapping left and right when the question says “image left.” Image left is the patient’s right.
- Calling a node medial to the internal carotid artery at C1 level II. That is the lateral retropharyngeal node. At the skull base it is the node of Rouvière.
- Numbering the axilla with neck levels. Axillary levels I–III follow the borders of pectoralis minor. Internal mammary nodes are parasternal, along the internal thoracic vessels.
- Calling axillary level III the supraclavicular fossa. Level III is infraclavicular, below the clavicle. The fossa above the clavicle is the caudal neck.
- Extending that fossa below the sternal notch. Below the notch is superior mediastinum, not level IV and not supraclavicular.
- Swapping subcarinal and hilar. Subcarinal is the space between the bronchi under the carina. Hilar is the lung root.
- Calling the porta hepatis the posterior liver vessel. The portal vein enters at the hilum. The IVC is the vessel in the posterior liver. Celiac nodes are on the aorta at about T12.

**Sources**

- Robbins classification of neck levels, and the consensus head-and-neck contouring atlases, including the retropharyngeal nodes
- Consensus breast nodal contouring atlases, for axillary levels I–III and internal mammary nodes
- IASLC lymph-node map, for mediastinal and hilar station borders
- RTOG pelvic lymph-node contouring atlas
- RTOG brachial plexus atlas, so the plexus is not contoured as a node chain

## Anatomy Quiz

**MDCB objectives:** III.4

**Skills this quiz trains**

- Name carina, cardiac chambers, liver, spleen, kidneys, aorta, IVC, prostate, seminal vesicles, rectum, bladder, and femoral heads from position words alone.
- Separate parotid from submandibular gland, and true-cord level from epiglottis and cricoid.
- Place the brachial plexus between the scalenes and in the supraclavicular fossa.
- Place lens, optic nerve, chiasm, pituitary, brainstem, cochlea, and hippocampus on a head-first supine slice, and keep image left as the patient’s right.
- Place the esophagus at C6, behind the left atrium, and at T10, and separate the right middle lobe from the lingula and the lower lobes.
- Stop the adult cord at about L1–L2 and call the lower lumbar sac the cauda equina.
- Use the vertebral landmark table, and keep dermatome T10 off the vertebral number of the umbilicus.
- Separate the penile bulb from the prostate, and small bowel from sigmoid and rectum.
- Separate breast from chest wall, axillary levels I–III, internal mammary nodes, the node of Rouvière, the supraclavicular fossa, hilar nodes from subcarinal nodes, and celiac nodes from porta hepatis.
- Assign neck levels I–V from cranial and posterior borders, and refuse to call level VI one of those five.
- Assign pelvic nodes to the correct vessel, and keep inguinal nodes off a standard prostate volume.

**Traps**

- Left atrium versus right ventricle.
- Aorta on the patient’s left versus IVC on the patient’s right.
- Right kidney usually lower than the left.
- Image left equals patient right.
- Level V versus level II, and level VI versus level III.
- Femoral heads described as nodes.
- Lens called the optic nerve, or the chiasm placed inside the sella.
- Hippocampus placed on the midline, or the patient’s left hippocampus placed on image left.
- Esophagus started at the hyoid, or the lingula called a middle lobe.
- Spinal cord contoured through the lower lumbar canal.
- Umbilicus called vertebral T10 because the dermatome is T10.
- Penile bulb called prostate, or central small-bowel loops called rectum.
- Internal mammary nodes placed in the axilla, Rouvière called level II, or supraclavicular nodes placed below the sternal notch.

**Check yourself**

Without a picture, list what must be true of an axial slice that contains the prostate and not the seminal vesicles, and name the organ directly posterior to the prostate and the bones directly lateral to it.

Walk-through: The slice is below the bladder neck and below the seminal vesicles. The rectum is directly posterior. The femoral heads are the round bones laterally, in the acetabula. The pubic symphysis is anterior. A round vessel just left of the lumbar spine would already have bifurcated this low; the iliac vessels, not the abdominal aorta, are the nodal landmarks here.

On a head-first supine slice viewed from the feet, name the structure anterior in the globe, the structure superior to the pituitary, and the patient’s left medial temporal structure beside the midbrain, and say which edge of the image that temporal structure is on.

Walk-through: The lens is anterior in the globe. The chiasm is superior to the pituitary and anterior to the midbrain. The left hippocampus is the medial temporal structure beside the midbrain, and it lies on image right.

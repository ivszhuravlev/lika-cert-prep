# Module 43. Memory lane

Original notes for MDCB CMD prep. Objective III.8: OAR constraints from QUANTEC, TG-101, and related summaries. These are 2010-era planning estimates for conventional fractionation unless a row is explicitly stereotactic. They are not personal prescriptions. A physician’s constraint in the chart overrides a memory number for that patient. On an exam item, the number in the stem is the one to apply, and these are the numbers to use when the stem says QUANTEC or TG-101 and prints no other figure.

Confirm every TG-101 cell in Table III of the AAPM report before clinical use. The study table below is the commonly examined point-maximum and critical-volume pattern, including optic pathway, brainstem, and brachial plexus.

## QUANTEC

**Objectives:** III.8

**What the exam expects:** Apply a QUANTEC summary estimate to a stated DVH point, conventional fractionation. Know that a rectum-style constraint is a set: one passing bin does not rescue a failing bin. Know the TG-101 study table well enough to score 1, 3, and 5 fractions without swapping one organ’s point maximum onto another organ.

The estimates below are the 2010 QUANTEC summary (International Journal of Radiation Oncology, Biology, Physics), conventional fractionation, for the endpoint named. They are not prescriptions.

| Organ | Summary estimate | Endpoint the summary is about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spinal cord | Dmax 50 Gy | about 0.2% myelopathy |
| Brain | Dmax < 60 Gy | symptomatic necrosis under about 3%. The same summary places 72 Gy near 5% and 90 Gy near 10% |
| Brainstem | whole organ < 54 Gy | necrosis / neuropathy |
| Optic nerves and chiasm | < 55 Gy | optic neuropathy |
| Cochlea | mean ≤ 45 Gy | sensory neural hearing loss under about 30% |
| Parotid | mean < 25 Gy to both glands, or spare one gland to mean < 20 Gy | long-term salivary function |
| Larynx | mean < 44 Gy or V50 < 27% | laryngeal edema, each under about 20%. Different endpoints in the same summary: Dmax < 66 Gy for vocal dysfunction, and mean < 50 Gy for aspiration, both described with chemotherapy |
| Lung | V20 ≤ 30% and mean lung dose 20 Gy | about 20% symptomatic pneumonitis |
| Heart, pericardium | mean < 26 Gy and V30 < 46% | pericarditis, each under about 15%. V25 < 10% is a different endpoint: long-term cardiac mortality, about <1% |
| Esophagus | mean < 34 Gy; V35 < 50%, V50 < 40%, V70 < 20% | mean: grade 3 or higher acute esophagitis, about 5–20%. The three volume figures: grade 2 or higher acute esophagitis, each under about 30% |
| Rectum | the set V50 < 50%, V60 < 35%, V65 < 25%, V70 < 20%, V75 < 15% | late rectal toxicity; use the set together |
| Bladder, prostate treatment | the set V65 ≤ 50%, V70 ≤ 35%, V75 ≤ 25%, V80 ≤ 15% | grade 3 or higher late toxicity; use the set together. For a bladder primary, the separate correlate is Dmax < 65 Gy, about <6% |
| Penile bulb | mean dose to 95% of the gland < 50 Gy, recorded in this bank as D95 < 50 Gy; also D90 < 50 Gy | severe erectile dysfunction, each under about 35%. A further summary correlate is D60–D70 < 70 Gy, under about 55% |
| Kidney | mean < 15–18 Gy; combined kidneys V12 < 55% | renal function. V12 < 55% is a separate combined-kidney volume correlate, not a substitute for the mean |
| Liver | mean < 30–32 Gy | radiation liver injury, excluding HCC and Child-Pugh liver disease |
| Stomach | whole-organ D100 < 45 Gy | ulceration under about 7% |
| Small bowel | V15 < 120 cm³ for individual loops, or V45 < 195 cm³ for the peritoneal space | late toxicity |

Lung is a pair: V20 and mean dose. Mean lung dose of 20 Gy is about the 20% symptomatic pneumonitis region, and V20 ≤ 30% is the paired constraint. Quoting only the mean misses a failing V20. The same summary curve places a mean of 7 Gy near 5%, 13 Gy near 10%, and 24 Gy near 30%. Those points do not replace the V20 row. Rectum is a five-point set. Bladder for prostate treatment is a four-point set. Esophagus is not that kind of set: the mean is the grade 3 parameter, and V35, V50, and V70 are alternate grade 2 parameters. A stem that names one of them is scored on that one. Larynx mean < 44 Gy and V50 < 27% are alternate edema parameters in the same way. Small bowel has two definitions; the stem must say loops or peritoneal space. Liver’s 30–32 Gy mean is not the number for a hepatocellular carcinoma or a Child-Pugh abnormal liver; those cases use a different, more restrictive approach that this memory row does not cover.

The penile-bulb correlate used in this bank follows the QUANTEC summary wording: mean dose to 95% of the gland < 50 Gy. Teaching cards shorten that first parameter to D95 < 50 Gy, and this bank uses that name for it. D90 < 50 Gy is the separate summary parameter, not a second name for the same dose. The QUANTEC summary does not give a separate conventional-fractionation duodenum row. Do not borrow stomach D100 or the small-bowel volumes for a duodenum line. Stereotactic duodenum limits are in the TG-101 table.

**TG-101 serial organs**, threshold dose to the critical volume and point maximum. The point is 0.035 cm³ or less. For a serial organ, the critical volume should not sit above the threshold dose, and the point dose should not exceed the point maximum. Confirm these cells in TG-101 Table III before using them clinically. Optic pathway, brainstem, and brachial plexus are in this table. The one-fraction optic point maximum is 10 Gy, the three-fraction brainstem point maximum is 23.1 Gy, and the five-fraction brachial-plexus point maximum is 30.5 Gy.

A dash means Table III does not give that cell. The 1.2 cm³ cord row and the 10 cm³ duodenum row have threshold doses only. Do not copy the point maximum from the other row of the same organ onto the blank. The cord subvolume 5–6 mm above and below the treated level uses the same doses as the 0.35 cm³ row, applied to <10% of that subvolume. Esophagus, trachea and large bronchus, duodenum, and jejunum/ileum: avoid circumferential irradiation.

**TG-101 parallel organs**, critical-volume idea. There is no point maximum. At least the stated volume should stay at or below the threshold dose. A smaller spared volume fails even when the dose inside that smaller volume is under the threshold.

| Organ | Minimum volume at or below the threshold | 1 fx | 5 fx | Endpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lung, both | 1500 cm³ | 7 Gy | 12.5 Gy | basic lung function |
| Lung, both | 1000 cm³ | 7.4 Gy | 13.5 Gy | pneumonitis |
| Liver | 700 cm³ | 9.1 Gy | 21 Gy | basic liver function |
| Renal cortex, both | 200 cm³ | 8.4 Gy | 17.5 Gy | basic renal function |

The three-fraction parallel-tissue thresholds are omitted here. Use Table III for a three-fraction lung, liver, or kidney critical volume rather than filling that column from memory.

SBRT cord limits and the QUANTEC 50 Gy cord limit answer different fractionations. Do not compare a 14 Gy single-fraction point maximum with the 50 Gy conventional Dmax as if they were the same constraint. Do not score a one-fraction optic point dose of 10 Gy against the three-fraction brainstem point maximum of 23.1 Gy, or a five-fraction plexus point dose against the five-fraction cord point maximum of 30 Gy.

### Worked example

**Given.** Conventional fractionation prostate plan. The rectum DVH reads V50 = 48%, V60 = 36%, V65 = 22%, V70 = 18%, V75 = 12%. Judge it against the QUANTEC rectum set above. These are summary estimates, not a prescription written for this patient.

**Arithmetic, one bin at a time.**

- V50: \(48 < 50\), inside the estimate
- V60: \(36 < 35\) is false; 36 is 1 percentage point over 35
- V65: \(22 < 25\), inside
- V70: \(18 < 20\), inside
- V75: \(12 < 15\), inside

**Check.** \(36 - 35 = 1\). The other four inequalities hold. One failure is enough for the set.

**Result means.** The plan does not meet the QUANTEC rectum set, because V60 is 36% against an estimate of less than 35%. A note that says “four of five passed” still describes a set that failed. The next planning step is to reduce the volume at 60 Gy, then re-read every bin. This comparison does not change the physician’s prescription.

**Wrong turns**

- Passing the plan on V70 alone.
- Applying the conventional 50 Gy cord limit to a 3-fraction SBRT cord dose of 18 Gy and calling it a QUANTEC pass. Different fractionation, different table.
- Using the liver 30–32 Gy mean for an HCC case the row explicitly excludes.
- Treating a parotid mean of 24 Gy to both glands as a failure against the “spare one gland to 20 Gy” branch. The bilateral mean under 25 Gy is the branch that applies when both glands are irradiated. The spare-one-gland branch is the alternative.
- Scoring optic pathway, brainstem, or brachial plexus on the cord point maximum, or using a 1-fraction cell on a 3- or 5-fraction plan.
- Treating a passing esophagus V35 as if it cleared a mean over 34 Gy. Those are different parameters.
- Calling combined-kidney V12 the same limit as the bilateral mean of 15–18 Gy.

**Traps.** Write the fractionation (conventional versus 1, 3, or 5 fractions) on the first line. Then use either the QUANTEC table or the TG-101 table, not a blend. Read the volume on the row: optic pathway is 0.2 cm³, brainstem is 0.5 cm³, cord is 0.35 cm³, and brachial plexus is 3 cm³.

**Sources.** QUANTEC, IJROBP 2010, the organ papers and the summary tables. AAPM TG-101 Table III for stereotactic serial-organ and critical-volume doses. Marks et al. is the QUANTEC overview paper if you need the citation trail back to a single organ chapter.

## QUANTEC Mini-Quiz

**Skills this quiz checks**

- Apply one QUANTEC estimate to a stated DVH point, and apply the rectum and prostate-bladder numbers as sets.
- Keep lung as V20 ≤ 30% and mean dose 20 Gy together. Mean lung dose 20 Gy is about the 20% pneumonitis region.
- Keep the liver mean off HCC and Child-Pugh cases.
- Separate conventional cord Dmax 50 Gy (about 0.2% myelopathy) from the TG-101 1-, 3-, and 5-fraction cord row.

## Physical Density of Materials

**Objectives:** IV.4, I.9, III.8

**What the exam expects:** Tissue rows match the module 37 recall list and stay at the values already in this lesson. Phantom and shielding materials are added for identification. Contrast is not on this list. The same numbers are ready to use in a water-equivalent-thickness product.

| Material | Physical density (g/cm³) |
| --- | --- |
| Air | about 0.001 |
| Lung | about 0.25–0.30 |
| Fat | 0.92 |
| Water | 1.00 |
| Muscle | about 1.04 |
| Bone | about 1.85 |
| Polystyrene | 1.05 |
| PMMA | 1.19 |
| Concrete | about 2.35 |
| Aluminum | 2.7 |
| Titanium | 4.5 |
| Steel | about 7.8 |
| Copper | 8.96 |
| Cerrobend | about 9.4 |
| Lead | 11.3 |
| Tungsten | 19.3 |

Muscle stays about 1.04 and bone stays about 1.85. Those values were already in this lesson, so they are not replaced. Water stays 1.00. Fat, which was missing, is 0.92.

### Worked example

**Given.** A beam crosses 5.0 cm of bone. Use density 1.85. The surrounding calculation had been water (density 1.00) through that same 5.0 cm.

**Formula.** \(d_w = d \times \rho\). Extra water-equivalent thickness \(= d(\rho - 1)\).

**Substitute.** \(d_w = 5.0 \times 1.85 = 9.25\) cm. Extra \(= 5.0 \times 0.85 = 4.25\) cm.

**Check.** \(9.25 - 5.00 = 4.25\). \(5 \times 1.85 = 9.25\).

**Result means.** The bone slab is 9.25 cm of water equivalent, 4.25 cm more than the homogeneous water path. A hand calculation that leaves the 5 cm as water under-attenuates. Air through the same 5.0 cm physical gap is \(5.0 \times 0.001 = 0.005\) cm of water equivalent, which is why an air cavity barely attenuates and why it must not be assigned lung density (0.25–0.30) or bone.

**Wrong turns**

- Bone at 1.04 (that is muscle) or lung at 1.00.
- Air at 0.25 (that is the low end of lung). Fat at 0.25 is the same miss: fat is 0.92.
- A hip prosthesis assigned “bone” at 1.85. Metal is an override to the alloy. Titanium is 4.5 and steel is about 7.8.
- Cerrobend (about 9.4) called lead (11.3), or lead called tungsten (19.3).
- Relative electron density and physical density swapped for metal. They diverge for high-Z materials. This table is physical density.

**Traps.** If the stem says “lung” and gives no number, use about 0.25–0.30 and show which end you picked. If the stem gives 0.26, use 0.26. If the stem says Cerrobend and gives no number, use about 9.4.

**Sources.** Standard physical-density tables used with CT-to-density commissioning (module 37). QUANTEC is not a density reference; it shares this module only because both are memory items.

## Constraint rows added from review

**Objectives:** III.8

**What the exam expects:** A DVH value appears in the stem and you say pass or fail, or several organs appear and you name the one that is limiting. The QUANTEC lesson above covers the 2010 summary rows. The rows below are the ones that turn up on department templates and MDCB-style items but were not in that lesson: three are QUANTEC rows that the summary table omitted, the rest are RTOG-protocol or common-practice numbers. Each row says where its figure comes from. The stem's own number, when it prints one, always wins over this list. These are conventional-fractionation (about 2 Gy per fraction) rows except the last one.

**Core facts, one row at a time**

Spinal cord PRV. The cord contour is expanded by 5 mm in every direction to a planning organ-at-risk volume (PRV), and the planning limit of Dmax 50 Gy is applied to the PRV, not only to the cord itself; some protocols write cord 45 Gy and PRV 50 Gy, and some expand 7–10 mm where the field arrangement makes it feasible. Source: RTOG head-and-neck protocols and common planning practice; QUANTEC's 50 Gy row is written for the cord contour and does not define a PRV.

Pharyngeal constrictors. Mean dose below 50 Gy, endpoint symptomatic dysphagia and aspiration; this is a separate QUANTEC row from the larynx rows (mean below 44 Gy for edema, mean below 50 Gy for aspiration, Dmax below 66 Gy for vocal dysfunction), and a plan can pass every larynx row and still fail the constrictors. Source: QUANTEC larynx-and-pharynx paper.

Kidneys, bilateral. The full QUANTEC set is mean below 15–18 Gy, V12 below 55%, V20 below 32%, V23 below 30%, and V28 below 20%; it is scored as a set, so one volume bin over its figure fails the kidneys even when the mean passes. Source: QUANTEC kidney paper (the summary lesson above carried only the mean and V12).

Mandible. The planning goal is Dmax below 70 Gy, with the usual protocol reading that no 1 cm³ of mandible exceeds 70 Gy (a few protocols allow 1 cm³ up to 75 Gy); the endpoint is osteoradionecrosis. Source: RTOG head-and-neck protocols; this bank scores Dmax 70 Gy as the goal and the 1 cm³ reading as the acceptable-variation line.

Brachial plexus, conventional fractionation. Dmax at or below 66 Gy, endpoint brachial plexopathy; the Emami TD5/5 figure is 60 Gy and some templates still use it. Source: RTOG head-and-neck and lung protocols; the TG-101 plexus cells (17.5, 24, 30.5 Gy point maximum for 1, 3, 5 fractions) are stereotactic only and must not be used on a 30-fraction plan.

Femoral heads. V50 below 5%, with Dmax below 50 Gy as the stricter alternative some templates write instead; the endpoint is femoral head necrosis and fracture. Source: common planning practice and department templates; the RTOG prostate protocols (0126, 0415 style) print V50 below 10%, so apply whichever figure the stem gives and use V50 below 5% when it is silent.

Lens. Dmax below 7–10 Gy depending on the source: Emami TD5/5 is 10 Gy, most IMRT templates write 5–7 Gy, and QUANTEC-era teaching cards often say 6 Gy; the detectable-opacity threshold taught for radiation protection is about 0.5 Gy (ICRP 2011; older texts said 2 Gy acute, 5 Gy fractionated), which is not a planning constraint. Source: Emami and common planning practice, not QUANTEC; this bank uses Dmax below 7 Gy when a stem is silent and accepts any figure inside 5–10 Gy that a stem prints.

Eye, globe and retina. Retina Dmax below 45 Gy and globe Dmax below 50 Gy, endpoint retinopathy and blindness. Source: Emami retina TD5/5 of 45 Gy and RTOG head-and-neck template rows of 45–50 Gy; not a QUANTEC row.

Breast and chest-wall tangents, ipsilateral lung. Ipsilateral lung V20 below 15–20% for a whole-breast tangent plan (RTOG 1005 writes V20 at or below 15%, V10 at or below 35%, V5 at or below 50%); chest-wall and regional-nodal plans run up to about 25%. Source: RTOG breast protocols; the bilateral-lung V20 at or below 30% row is the thoracic-radiotherapy row and is the wrong one for a tangent plan, because tangents treat one lung only.

Breast, heart (left-sided). Heart V25 below 10% is the QUANTEC long-term cardiac-mortality row, and mean heart dose below 4–5 Gy is the breast-protocol row (RTOG 1005 writes mean below 4 Gy and V20 below 5%); the Darby data behind the mean-dose row is about a 7% relative increase in major coronary events per gray of mean heart dose. Source: QUANTEC for V25, RTOG breast protocols and common practice for the mean.

Spinal cord, stereotactic, QUANTEC line. QUANTEC's cord paper places less than 1% myelopathy at a maximum cord dose of 13 Gy in a single fraction or 20 Gy in three fractions; TG-101 Table III prints a different pair for the same organ, a point maximum (0.035 cm³) of 14 Gy in one fraction, 21.9 Gy in three, and 30 Gy in five, with 10, 18, and 23 Gy to 0.35 cm³. Source: QUANTEC spinal cord paper and TG-101; a stem that names QUANTEC is scored on 13 and 20 Gy, a stem that names TG-101 on 14 and 21.9 Gy, and a stem that gives neither is a point-maximum question that the printed options will usually settle.

### Worked example

**Given.** Conventional head-and-neck IMRT, 70 Gy in 35 fractions. The DVH reads cord Dmax 44 Gy, cord PRV (cord plus 5 mm) Dmax 51 Gy, pharyngeal constrictor mean 48 Gy, mandible Dmax 71 Gy with 0.3 cm³ above 70 Gy, bilateral parotid mean 24 Gy, brachial plexus Dmax 63 Gy. Name the failing rows.

**Compare, one row at a time.**

- Cord 44 Gy against 50 Gy: inside. Cord PRV 51 Gy against 50 Gy: over by 1 Gy. Fails.
- Constrictor mean 48 Gy against 50 Gy: inside, by 2 Gy.
- Mandible Dmax 71 Gy against the 70 Gy goal: over by 1 Gy, so the goal is missed. Volume above 70 Gy is 0.3 cm³ against the 1 cm³ variation line: 0.3 < 1, so the plan is inside the acceptable variation. Record it as a variation, not a pass.
- Parotid mean 24 Gy against 25 Gy bilateral: inside.
- Plexus 63 Gy against 66 Gy: inside; against the Emami 60 Gy figure it would be over, which is why the stem must say which figure applies.

**Check.** 51 − 50 = 1 Gy over on the PRV; 71 − 70 = 1 Gy over on the mandible goal with 0.7 cm³ of room under the variation line; every other row is inside its figure.

**Result means.** The limiting structure is the cord PRV, the only row with no allowance left; the mandible is a recorded variation. Reducing the PRV maximum by about 1 Gy, then re-reading every row, is the next planning step. The constrictor mean at 48 Gy has only 2 Gy of room; a re-optimization that fixes the PRV must not push the constrictors past 50 Gy.

**Wrong turns**

- Passing the plan because the cord itself is 44 Gy; the limit was written on the PRV.
- Scoring the constrictors on the larynx 44 Gy edema row (they would then look like a failure) or dropping them because "larynx passed."
- Calling 63 Gy a plexus failure against the TG-101 five-fraction point maximum of 30.5 Gy; that cell is for stereotactic plans.
- Scoring a left-breast tangent plan's ipsilateral lung V20 of 22% as a pass against the bilateral 30% row.
- Reading mean heart dose 5.5 Gy as a pass because V25 is only 6%; the two rows are different endpoints and the breast template row is the mean.
- Comparing a single-fraction cord point of 13.5 Gy with the conventional 50 Gy and calling it a pass, or scoring it against 14 Gy when the stem said QUANTEC (13 Gy).
- Taking a kidney mean of 13 Gy as a pass when V28 is 24%; the V28 bin fails the set.

**Traps.** Write the fractionation and the source the stem names on the first line. The three QUANTEC rows here are constrictors, the kidney volume bins, and the stereotactic cord line; every other row is an RTOG protocol or template figure, and when a stem prints a different template number, the stem wins. "Ipsilateral" and "bilateral" lung are different rows with different figures.

**Sources.** QUANTEC organ papers (larynx and pharynx, kidney, spinal cord), IJROBP 2010. RTOG 1005 and the RTOG head-and-neck protocol family for the mandible, plexus, PRV, and breast template rows. Emami et al. 1991 for the lens and retina TD5/5 figures. Darby et al. 2013 for the mean-heart-dose relation. AAPM TG-101 Table III for the stereotactic cord cells quoted for comparison. The femoral head V50 below 5% is a department-template figure; RTOG prostate protocols print 10%.

## Tissue Thresholds and Stereotactic Rows: Gonads, Ribs, Brainstem, Lung, Lens, Cord

**Objectives:** III.8

**What the exam expects:** Two kinds of number sit side by side on this module and items mix them deliberately. Tissue-reaction thresholds (the dose at which an effect such as sterility or cataract begins to appear) come from radiobiology and ICRP; planning constraints (the DVH line a protocol scores) come from QUANTEC, TG-101, and RTOG protocols. The rows below add the thresholds and stereotactic rows that the earlier lessons left out, and each says which document owns its figure, because a stem that names QUANTEC cannot be answered with a TG-101 cell or an RTOG line.

**Gonads (thresholds, not planning rows).** Testes: temporary oligospermia or azoospermia begins at about 0.15 Gy, and doses up to about 1 Gy give temporary sterility with recovery that takes months to a few years; permanent sterility follows about 3.5 to 6 Gy, and fractionation lowers rather than raises that threshold because spermatogonial stem cells are killed cumulatively. Leydig (hormone) cells need about 15 to 20 Gy or more, so a man can be sterile and hormonally intact. Ovaries: oocytes are a fixed, non-renewing pool, so permanent failure is age dependent, taught as roughly 5 to 10 Gy fractionated in younger women and falling to 2 to 3 Gy near menopause; a single dose of a few gray can do it at any adult age, and about 20 Gy sterilises essentially everyone. Source: Hall and Giaccia, ICRP 118; these are thresholds for counselling and scatter estimates, not DVH rows.

**Ribs and chest wall.** Conventional fractionation: rib fracture is uncommon below about 50 Gy and rises above it, a few percent in the 50 to 60 Gy region where tangents or an electron boost put full dose on bone. SBRT: the RTOG 0813 and 0915 lung protocols write chest wall V30 below 30 cc, scored on the volume above 30 Gy; the same 30 Gy label on a conventional plan is below any fracture threshold. Example: V30 of 42 cc fails by 12 cc regardless of the rib maximum. Source: RTOG 0813 and 0915 for the SBRT row; the breast and chest-wall late-effects literature for the 50 Gy figure.

**Brainstem, single-fraction radiosurgery.** TG-101 Table III: point maximum (0.035 cc) 15 Gy, with 10 Gy to 0.5 cc, brainstem excluding medulla. QUANTEC brainstem paper: a radiosurgery maximum of 12.5 Gy for under about 5 percent risk, which many programs and most teaching cards use as the conservative figure. A point dose of 13.5 Gy is therefore over the QUANTEC figure by 1.0 Gy and under the TG-101 point maximum by 1.5 Gy; the stem's attribution decides. This bank keys 15 Gy when the stem says TG-101 and 12.5 Gy when it says QUANTEC or "conservative."

**Lung V20 across fractionation.** Three rows carry the same label and very different biology. Conventional (about 2 Gy per fraction): QUANTEC total-lung V20 at or below 30 percent for about 20 percent pneumonitis; the RTOG 0617 dose-escalation protocol allowed up to 37 percent. SBRT (three to five fractions): RTOG 0813 and 0915 write total-lung V20 below 10 percent, alongside the TG-101 critical-volume cells (1,500 cc under 12.5 Gy and 1,000 cc under 13.5 Gy in five fractions). Example: V20 of 12 percent fails an SBRT plan by 2 points and would pass a conventional plan with 18 points to spare. Ranked most permissive to most restrictive: 0617 at 37, QUANTEC at 30, SBRT at 10.

**Lens: threshold versus tolerance versus constraint.** ICRP 2011 cataract threshold: about 0.5 Gy, acute or protracted. Legacy teaching pair: 2 Gy acute and 5 Gy fractionated, and the 5 Gy figure is what older items key as the lens "tolerance"; treat it as historical. Planning constraint: Dmax below 7 Gy in this bank when a stem is silent, inside the 5 to 10 Gy range of Emami and department templates (the earlier lesson has the detail). A lens at 4 Gy can pass a plan while sitting far above the cataract threshold; the two numbers answer different questions.

**Spinal cord, stereotactic: who owns which number.** QUANTEC's cord paper gives 13 Gy in one fraction and 20 Gy in three fractions (under about 1 percent myelopathy) and stops there; it has no five-fraction line. TG-101 Table III gives point maxima (0.035 cc) of 14, 21.9, and 30 Gy for one, three, and five fractions, with 10, 18, and 23 Gy to 0.35 cc. A stem that says "according to QUANTEC" and asks about five fractions is mis-attributed; the only five-fraction cord row is TG-101's 30 Gy, and a 28 Gy point passes it by 2 Gy. The earlier constraint lesson has the one- and three-fraction comparison worked.

**Wrong turns**

- Reading 0.3 Gy to the testes as permanent sterility; permanent needs 3.5 to 6 Gy.
- Giving one ovarian threshold for every age.
- Scoring an SBRT chest wall on the conventional 50 Gy fracture figure, or forbidding any 30 Gy to chest wall.
- Keying brainstem radiosurgery at 12.5 Gy when the stem says TG-101, or at 15 Gy when it says QUANTEC.
- Passing an SBRT lung V20 of 12 percent against the QUANTEC 30 percent row.
- Reporting the legacy 5 Gy fractionated lens figure as the current cataract threshold.
- Citing a QUANTEC five-fraction cord line; there is none.

**Traps.** First line: is this a tissue-reaction threshold or a planning constraint? Second line: what fractionation? Third line: which document does the stem name? Only then compare the number.

**Sources.** Hall and Giaccia, Radiobiology for the Radiologist, and ICRP Publication 118 for gonadal and lens thresholds; ICRP 2011 statement on tissue reactions; QUANTEC brainstem and spinal-cord papers (IJROBP 2010); AAPM TG-101 Table III; RTOG 0813, 0915, and 0617 for the lung and chest-wall rows; Emami et al. 1991 for the lens TD5/5.

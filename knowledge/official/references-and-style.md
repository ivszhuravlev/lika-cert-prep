## Reports and literature item writers use

Item writers use reports current to dosimetry practice from AAPM, ABS, ASTRO, ICRU, ICRP, and NCRP, plus published RTOG trials and NCCN guidelines. The board also expects familiarity with recent practice-changing papers and with new delivery and imaging equipment. The page says an exhaustive reference list is in the applicant handbook. The handbook text that was read gives the four books above and these two examples, then the style guide:

- QUANTEC special issue. *International Journal of Radiation Oncology Biology Physics*. Volume 76, Issue 3, Supplement, S1–S160, March 2010.
- Miften et al. Management of radiotherapy patients with implanted cardiac pacemakers and defibrillators: A report of AAPM TG-203. *Medical Physics* 2019; 46(1): e757–788.

## AAPM reports named for this bank

Summaries are from the public landing pages, read 2026-10-10, except TG-43. Report PDFs are not in this repo.

**TG-43.** Landing page: https://www.aapm.org/pubs/reports/detail.asp?docid=84 . The AAPM reports index lists Report 084 (2004) as the update of Task Group 43: a revised protocol for brachytherapy dose calculation. The index also lists a 2007 supplement and a 2017 Supplement 2 (AAPM and GEC-ESTRO). The docid=84 abstract page was not retrieved, so the summary stops at that index title.

**TG-51.** https://aapm.org/pubs/reports/detail.asp?docid=66 . Report 067 (1999): the AAPM protocol for clinical reference dosimetry of high-energy photon and electron beams. It replaces the need, under TG-21, for the user to carry large tables of stopping-power ratios and mass-energy absorption coefficients and to calculate theoretical dosimetry factors. Worksheets and an equipment list are included. *Medical Physics* 26: 1847–1870 (1999). The landing page says the journal printing has an incorrect formula on page 16, item 8A, and that the AAPM file has the correction. An addendum, Report 067.A1, is linked from the same page.

**TG-101.** https://aapm.org/pubs/reports/detail.asp?docid=102 . Report 101 (2010): stereotactic body radiation therapy. Best-practice guidance for physicists, physicians, and therapists: clinical literature, program setup (protocols, equipment, resources, QA), and consistent prescribing, reporting, and recording. *Medical Physics* 37: 4078–4101 (2010).

**TG-142.** https://www.aapm.org/pubs/reports/detail.asp?docid=125 . 2009 report on quality assurance of medical accelerators. It updates linear-accelerator QA in TG-40. Tolerances depend on use: non-IMRT, IMRT, and stereotactic delivery, including MLC tests for IMRT and imaging checks. Tables cover daily, monthly, and annual tests, plus wedges, MLC, and imaging. It also covers how a physicist sets up the QA program. *Medical Physics* 36: 4197–4212 (2009).

**TG-203.** https://aapm.org/pubs/reports/detail.asp?docid=191 . Report 203 (2019): management of radiotherapy patients with implanted cardiac pacemakers and defibrillators. It follows TG-34 (1994) and covers failure modes of modern devices and management through the radiotherapy process. *Medical Physics*, https://doi.org/10.1002/mp.13838 .

## Style facts that change a written answer

From the September 2026 handbook style guide, plus the image rule printed with the outline. The full acronym list is omitted.

- `dmax` means depth of maximum dose. `Dmax` means maximum dose.
- Elements: the handbook’s example is iodine written with the mass number on the symbol. The text extract reads “I 125,” which is the superscript form (¹²⁵I), not a hyphenated word.
- Images are head-first supine unless the item says otherwise.
- In a stem, spell “degree.” In options, use the degree symbol.
- In a stem, spell “percentage.” In options, use %.
- In an option, the word “not” is lower case and underlined.
- Dates use an ordinal: “January 1st,” not “January 1.”
- Temperatures are centigrade only.
- Noun: “X ray.” Adjective: “X-ray.” Verb: “x-ray.”
- “Ultrasound” is written in full.
- TNM categories are subscripted, as in T1N0M0.
- “4D CT” has a space between 4D and CT.
- “field-in-field” is hyphenated when it modifies another word.
- “Cobalt-60” is hyphenated.
- Spell “mold,” not the British spelling.
- A named report is cited in parentheses in the form NCRP# 100 or ICRU# 100.
- Stems that depend on a report are framed as “According to QUANTEC guidelines,” “According to NRC guidelines,” or “According to NCRP (AAPM, etc.) Report #…”
- `EQD` is defined there as equivalent dose in two fractions. The “2” is the subscript that the extract split onto the following line.
- Proper names are capitalized (Compton, Hounsfield, and the other names in that entry).
- `RO-ILS` is printed with the registration mark.

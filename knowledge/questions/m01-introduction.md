# Module 1 — Introduction

Original items written for this bank. They are not retired MDCB questions and not a transcription of another course.

## Hello

### Q1
Stem: Which count is the number of questions on the CMD examination?
A. 100
B. 135
C. 155
D. 200
Answer: C
Objective: I.1
Why: The exam handbook states that the CMD exam contains 155 questions. The other counts are common guesses from older or shorter certification tests.

### Q2
Stem: How long is the candidate given to finish the CMD exam?
A. 2 hours 30 minutes
B. 3 hours
C. 3 hours 50 minutes
D. 5 hours
Answer: C
Objective: I.1
Why: The allotted time is 3 hours 50 minutes (230 minutes) for the full 155-item exam. Shorter times would not match the published administration, and 5 hours is not the limit.

### Q3
Stem: Which statement matches the design of the CMD exam?
A. It is offered in several languages and tests worldwide practice patterns equally.
B. It is delivered in English and is written for U.S.-based practice.
C. It is an oral exam with a written calculation add-on.
D. It is open-book and uses the department’s planning-system manuals.
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: The handbook states the exam is delivered in English only and was designed to test dosimetry knowledge for U.S.-based practice. It is a closed-book computer exam, not an oral or open-book test.

### Q4
Stem: A few items on the form do not look like the usual multiple-choice physics questions, and the stem sends the candidate to a patient data set. What are those items for?
A. Unscored pilot items that can be skipped
B. A separate take-home contouring assignment
C. Performance-based items answered from a plan in ProKnow
D. Replacement questions used only if the candidate fails a domain
Answer: C
Objective: I.1
Why: A small number of performance-based items use ProKnow so the candidate can evaluate a plan, isodose display, DVH, anatomy, beam arrangement, or contour. Pilot items are a different feature: they are mixed into the exam and are unscored, but they are not identified and are not the ProKnow set.

### Q5
Stem: An axial image is shown and the stem does not state the patient orientation. How should the image be read?
A. Feet-first prone
B. Head-first supine
C. Head-first prone
D. Whatever orientation the candidate’s clinic uses most often
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: The handbook states that all images on test questions are head-first supine unless otherwise noted. A prone or feet-first setup will be stated. Assuming the clinic’s most common setup, or prone, will swap anterior-posterior or superior-inferior anatomy.

## Medical Dosimetrist Certification Board Content Breakdown

### Q1
Stem: Which content domain is 42% of the scored exam?
A. Radiation Physics
B. Dose Calculation Methods
C. Treatment Planning
D. Quality Assurance and Standard of Care
Answer: C
Objective: I.1
Why: On the 2023 test-specification matrix, domain III, Treatment Planning, is weighted 42%. Radiation Physics is 14%, Dose Calculation Methods is 13%, and Quality Assurance and Standard of Care is 9%.

### Q2
Stem: What is the weight of Radiation Physics?
A. 5%
B. 8%
C. 14%
D. 42%
Answer: C
Objective: I.1
Why: Domain I, Radiation Physics, is 14%. Five percent is brachytherapy, 8% is localization, and 42% is treatment planning.

### Q3
Stem: Which domain has the smallest weight on the 2023 matrix?
A. Localization, 8%
B. Brachytherapy, 5%
C. Radiation Protection, 9%
D. Dose Calculation Methods, 13%
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Brachytherapy is 5%. Localization is 8%, radiation protection is 9%, and dose calculation methods are 13%. The smaller weight does not make the domain optional.

### Q4
Stem: Localization and Dose Calculation Methods are weighted at which pair?
A. Localization 14% and dose calculation 8%
B. Localization 8% and dose calculation 13%
C. Localization 9% and dose calculation 9%
D. Localization 13% and dose calculation 42%
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Domain II, Localization, is 8%, and domain IV, Dose Calculation Methods, is 13%. Swapping those two weights, or assigning both the 9% protection/QA weight, is the usual mix-up.

### Q5
Stem: Radiation Protection and Quality Assurance and Standard of Care are weighted as:
A. 5% and 8%
B. 9% each
C. 13% and 14%
D. 14% and 42%
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Domain VI and domain VII are each 9%. They are easy to trade with physics (14%), dose calculation (13%), or brachytherapy (5%).

### Q6
Stem: Which task belongs to the ProKnow performance-based items?
A. Reciting the seven domain weights from memory while the screen is blank
B. Evaluating isodose, DVH, anatomy, beam arrangement, or a contour on a data set
C. Writing a new optimization objective into the clinic’s production planning system
D. Calculating a vault tenth-value layer on a scratch formula card supplied by the center
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: ProKnow items test plan evaluation, isodose distributions, dose to critical structures, DVHs, anatomical structures, beam arrangements, and contouring. Domain weights are ordinary knowledge items. The test center does not supply a formula card.

### Q7
Stem: In the ProKnow dose display used for the exam, dose is shown as:
A. Relative percent, normalized to 100% at isocenter
B. Monitor units
C. Absolute dose in Gy
D. Exposure in roentgen
Answer: C
Objective: I.10
Why: The handbook knowledge-skills list states that all dose is displayed in absolute dose (Gy). Reading the color wash as a percent, as MU, or as exposure will make a constraint comparison wrong.

### Q8
Stem: A CT in the ProKnow window opens completely black. What does the handbook tell the candidate to check first?
A. Window and level
B. That the patient was scanned feet-first
C. That the item is an unscored pilot and can be left blank
D. That the structure set was deleted and cannot be restored
Answer: A
Objective: I.8
Why: A black or missing-looking CT is not intentional. The window/level settings need to be checked and can be reset. Orientation, pilot status, and a deleted contour are separate problems and are not the stated cause of a black image.

### Q9
Stem: Which statement about scoring is the one published for this exam?
A. The candidate must pass every domain on its own.
B. The result is curved so that a fixed fraction of the room passes.
C. One cut is applied to the total number of scored items correct. A domain does not have its own pass hurdle.
D. Unscored pilot items are counted in the raw score so that guessing is penalized.
Answer: C
Objective: I.1
Why: Scores are not curved. Each candidate is compared with one cut score from the standard-setting study, using the total number of scored questions correct. A passing result is not required in each of the seven domains. Embedded unscored items are not included in the score.

## MDCB Formula Sheet

### Q1
Stem: What may the candidate use for arithmetic on exam day?
A. A programmable alpha calculator and a prewritten formula card
B. A silent, non-programmable scientific calculator, plus the blank note board the center provides
C. The calculator the test vendor keeps at the desk
D. A phone calculator, because only the camera is restricted
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: The candidate must bring a silent hand-held solar or battery scientific calculator. Alpha, programmable, and paper-tape calculators are not allowed, and the vendor does not provide one. Notes and books stay outside. The center may issue a blank erasable note board for scratch work; it cannot be brought in already filled with formulas.

### Q2
Stem: Which expression is the decay constant?
A. 0.693 × T½
B. 0.693 / T½
C. 1.44 × T½
D. T½ / 0.693
Answer: B
Objective: I.7
Why: λ = 0.693 / T½. Option A multiplies and has the wrong units. Option C is the mean life (1.44 × T½). Option D is also the mean life, written as T½ / 0.693.

### Q3
Stem: A permanent-implant total-dose estimate multiplies the initial dose rate by which time?
A. The half-life
B. The mean life, 1.44 × half-life
C. The decay constant
D. One year, regardless of the nuclide
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: Total decays from a source that is left in place scale with the mean life, τ = 1.44 × T½, not with the half-life alone. The decay constant is a rate, and a fixed year ignores the nuclide.

### Q4
Stem: On a radiograph, magnification is which ratio?
A. Source-to-object / source-to-film
B. Source-to-film / source-to-object
C. Object size × film size
D. Source-to-film − source-to-object
Answer: B
Objective: I.6
Why: Magnification = image size / object size = source-to-film distance / source-to-object distance. Inverting the distances, multiplying the two sizes, or subtracting the distances are the usual geometry errors.

### Q5
Stem: Intensity from a point source in air, with no attenuation, changes with distance by:
A. I2 = I1 × (d1 / d2)^2
B. I2 = I1 × (d2 / d1)^2
C. I2 = I1 × (d1 / d2)
D. I2 = I1 × (d2 − d1)
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Inverse square: the farther distance goes in the denominator, and the ratio is squared. Option B inverts the ratio and makes intensity rise as you move away. Option C forgets the square. Option D treats the falloff as a subtraction.

### Q6
Stem: A beam is 4 half-value layers thick. The narrow-beam transmission is:
A. 1/2
B. 1/4
C. 1/8
D. 1/16
Answer: D
Objective: I.10
Why: Each HVL transmits one half. Four HVLs transmit (1/2)^4 = 1/16. The other choices stop one, two, or three layers early.

## Studying Tips

### Q1
Stem: A candidate blocks equal hours for every domain. Which change best matches the blueprint?
A. Give Treatment Planning the largest block, because that domain is 42%.
B. Give Brachytherapy the largest block, because it is the most mathematical.
C. Skip Localization, because 8% cannot affect a pass decision.
D. Study only Radiation Physics until every formula is memorized.
Answer: A
Objective: I.1
Why: Treatment Planning is 42% of the scored exam, so it should get the largest share of practice. Brachytherapy is 5%. An 8% domain still counts toward the single total score. Physics is 14%, not the whole test.

### Q2
Stem: A calculation stem is ambiguous until the candidate notices one phrase. Which phrase decides whether a percent-depth-dose table or a TMR table is the natural tool?
A. Whether the plan is SSD or isocentric SAD
B. Whether the patient is male or female
C. Whether the dose is written in cGy or Gy
D. Whether the nuclide is cesium or iridium
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Fixed-SSD setups are read from PDD, with an inverse-square correction if the SSD is not the table SSD. Isocentric setups are read from TMR or TPR. Units and the brachytherapy nuclide do not choose the external-beam depth table.

### Q3
Stem: Which error is a power-of-ten mistake rather than a concept mistake?
A. Using the mean life where the half-life belongs
B. Reporting 3.5 Gy as 35 cGy
C. Treating a Compton event as a photoelectric event
D. Shifting a prone patient with supine signs
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: 3.5 Gy = 350 cGy, not 35 cGy. That is a factor-of-ten slip on the centi prefix. Mean life, interaction type, and patient orientation are concept errors, not decimal errors.

### Q4
Stem: How should the candidate treat items that might be unscored pilots?
A. Leave any unfamiliar item blank so it cannot lower the score.
B. Answer every item. Pilots are not marked, and a blank scored item is simply wrong.
C. Spend the last hour hunting for the pilot items to delete them.
D. Pilot items are the only items that use images.
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Unscored items are embedded and are not identified. They are not in the score, but a scored item left blank does not earn the point. Images are not a marker of pilot status.

### Q5
Stem: Spread evenly, 155 items in 230 minutes is about how long per item?
A. About 0.5 minute
B. About 1.5 minutes
C. About 4 minutes
D. About 6 minutes
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: 3 hours 50 minutes is 230 minutes. 230 / 155 = 1.48 minutes, about 1.5 minutes per item if time is split evenly. Performance-based items take longer than that average, so routine items have to be faster. Four and six minutes would overrun the session.

## How I Passed (A Note from our Founder)

### Q1
Stem: A study note says the author “just did a lot of questions.” Which use of that advice actually matches this exam?
A. Repeat one decay problem until the arithmetic is memorized, then scale the same numbers.
B. Practice many setups, because the exam changes the numbers, the nuclide, and whether the stem wants half-life or mean life.
C. Stop calculations after one perfect worksheet. The formula card at the center removes the need.
D. Skip plan-evaluation practice. The computer-based items are only definitions.
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: The same relationship is tested with new numbers and with traps such as mean life used in place of half-life. There is no formula card to rely on. Plan-evaluation items in ProKnow are part of the exam, not a side quiz of definitions.

### Q2
Stem: The highest-yield statement in a “how I passed” note is which one?
A. Treatment planning is 42%, so isodose, DVH, and planning judgment cannot be left until the last night.
B. Brachytherapy is half the exam, so seeds come before everything else.
C. A review course’s advertised pass rate is the MDCB pass standard.
D. Domain diagnostic scores from a practice form can be ignored if physics feels strong, because physics is 42%.
Answer: A
Objective: I.1
Why: Domain III is 42%. Brachytherapy is 5%, not half. The MDCB does not endorse pass rates guaranteed by a review course. Physics is 14%, not 42%.

### Q3
Stem: Why do candidates lose points on a radiograph even when the magnification arithmetic is easy?
A. The image is assumed head-first supine unless the stem says otherwise, and the measured size still has to be divided by the magnification.
B. Films on the exam are always life size, so magnification is never used.
C. Magnification is source-to-object divided by source-to-film, so the image is always smaller than the anatomy.
D. ProKnow replaces every film question, so radiograph geometry is no longer tested.
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Default orientation is head-first supine. Magnification greater than 1 means the image is larger than the object, so the true size is the image size divided by source-to-film/source-to-object. Film geometry remains on the blueprint under geometric characteristics.

### Q4
Stem: A candidate plans to learn the ProKnow window during the exam because the tools are “obvious.” What is the actual constraint?
A. Tutorial access on the MDCB ProKnow site is turned off 72 hours before the first exam day.
B. ProKnow opens only after the candidate passes the multiple-choice section.
C. Contouring items are graded by a physicist after the 6-week score report.
D. The dose display can be switched to rem, so Gy practice is unnecessary.
Answer: A
Objective: I.1
Why: Practice access is shut off 72 hours before the first day of that exam administration, and logging in after that can invalidate a score. Dose on those items is in Gy. Contouring is submitted during the exam, including committing the structure.

### Q5
Stem: Which exam-day habit matches a note that says “slow down on the setup line”?
A. Circle whether the calculation is SSD or SAD, and whether the distance in a decay problem is a half-life count or a mean life, before touching the calculator.
B. Convert every dose to rem first, because the prescription is a dose equivalent.
C. Use TMR for every depth, including a fixed-SSD extended-distance setup.
D. Multiply every film measurement by the magnification to get the patient size.
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Most arithmetic misses start by grabbing the wrong table or the wrong lifetime. Prescription dose is absorbed dose, not rem. A fixed-SSD setup wants PDD, not a reflex TMR. Patient size from a magnified film is the image size divided by the magnification.

## How to Navigate this Course

### Q1
Stem: In this bank, modules 2 through 12 are the block aimed at which domain?
A. Domain I, Radiation Physics, 14%
B. Domain III, Treatment Planning, 42%
C. Domain V, Brachytherapy, 5%
D. Domain VII, Quality Assurance, 9%
Answer: A
Objective: I.1
Why: The public curriculum maps modules 2–12 to Radiation Physics. Treatment planning, brachytherapy, and quality assurance are later modules and different weights.

### Q2
Stem: Modules 13 through 16 (patient data, positioning, motion, and simulation) are aimed at which domain?
A. Domain I only
B. Domain II, Localization, 8%
C. Domain IV only
D. Domain VI, Radiation Protection
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Those lessons are the localization sequence: manage patient data, positioning, immobilization and motion, and simulation and TPS localization. Inhomogeneity corrections inside the patient-data module also touch dose calculation, but the module’s home on the map is localization.

### Q3
Stem: A lesson title ends in “Quiz” or “Mini-Quiz.” How should it be used?
A. Skip it. Only the tutorial lessons are real content.
B. Treat it as another lesson. The objectives in that quiz are still on the blueprint.
C. Memorize the answer letters in order.
D. Assume quiz items are the unscored pilots and will not be scored on the real exam.
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Quiz lessons are part of the module. Their topics (decay modes, tube design, TAR/TMR, positioning) are tested on the exam. Answer letters in this bank are not a pattern to memorize, and a course quiz is not an embedded pilot item.

### Q4
Stem: Which lessons in the physics block are the ones that require extra numeric practice, with the arithmetic written out?
A. Hello, and How to Navigate this Course
B. The radioactivity calculation lessons, the wavelength tutorial, the magnification tutorial, and the unit-conversion tutorial
C. Only the lessons that mention ProKnow
D. Only module 16
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: Those are the calculation lessons in modules 1–16. Exam structure and simulation concepts are not the numeric drills. Later modules add MU, gap, and brachytherapy arithmetic.

### Q5
Stem: The “Objective” line on an item, such as I.7 or II.2, refers to what?
A. A page number in a review book
B. The 2023 MDCB test-specification objective that the item trains
C. The number of minutes to spend on the item
D. The percent weight of that single item
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: I.7 is “recall half-lives,” II.2 is patient positioning, and the other codes are the rest of the seven-domain matrix. They are not page numbers, timers, or item weights. Domain weights apply to the whole domain, not to one question.

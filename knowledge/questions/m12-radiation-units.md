# Module 12 — Radiation Units (Activity, Exposure, Absorbed Dose, Dose Equivalent)

Original items. Conversions used here: 1 Gy = 100 cGy = 100 rad, 1 Sv = 100 rem, 1 mCi = 37 MBq, 1 Ci = 3.7 × 10^10 Bq, 1 R = 2.58 × 10^(−4) C/kg. Alpha particles use wR = 20.

## Units

### Q1
Stem: Absorbed dose is energy imparted per unit mass. Its SI unit is:
A. The gray (J/kg)
B. The becquerel
C. The roentgen
D. The sievert, with no weighting factor allowed in the definition
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1 Gy = 1 J/kg of energy absorbed. The becquerel is activity. The roentgen is exposure. The sievert is equivalent dose or effective dose after a radiation weighting factor, not the raw absorbed dose.

### Q2
Stem: A prescription of 2 Gy per fraction should be written as absorbed dose. Writing it as 2 Sv implies:
A. A radiation weighting factor has already been applied, which is not how external photon prescriptions are stated
B. The same quantity, because gray and sievert are interchangeable names
C. An activity of 2 Bq
D. An exposure of 2 R
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Photon wR is 1, so the numeric equivalent dose would match the absorbed dose, but the prescription is still stated in gray. Using sievert suggests a protection quantity. It is not an activity and it is not an exposure.

### Q3
Stem: 1 cGy and 1 rad are related by:
A. 1 cGy = 1 rad
B. 1 cGy = 100 rad
C. 1 cGy = 0.01 rad
D. 1 cGy = 1 rem for alpha particles only
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1 Gy = 100 rad, so 1 cGy = 1 rad. 100 rad would be 1 Gy. 0.01 rad would be 0.1 mGy. The rem is dose equivalent, and for alphas it is not numerically equal to the rad.

### Q4
Stem: Activity, exposure, and absorbed dose are three different quantities. Which set of units matches that order?
A. Bq, R, Gy
B. Gy, Bq, R
C. Sv, R, Bq
D. R, Gy, Bq
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Activity is becquerel (or curie). Exposure is roentgen (or C/kg). Absorbed dose is gray (or rad). The sievert does not belong in the activity slot, and the order in the other options is scrambled.

### Q5
Stem: Equivalent dose uses a radiation weighting factor. Effective dose goes one step further and also uses:
A. Tissue weighting factors
B. The decay constant only
C. Magnification
D. Hounsfield units
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Effective dose sums equivalent dose in each organ times a tissue weighting factor wT. It is a protection quantity, not a prescription dose. Decay constant, magnification, and HU are unrelated conversions.

## Unit Conversion Tutorial

### Q1
Stem: A point receives 3.5 Gy. What is that dose in cGy?
A. 350 cGy
B. 35 cGy
C. 3.5 cGy
D. 3500 cGy
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1 Gy = 100 cGy, so 3.5 × 100 = 350 cGy. 35 cGy drops a factor of ten. 3.5 cGy forgets the prefix entirely. 3500 cGy multiplies by 1000.

### Q2
Stem: A source is 80 mCi. Using 1 mCi = 37 MBq, what is the activity in MBq?
A. 2960 MBq
B. 2.16 MBq
C. 296 MBq
D. 29600 MBq
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 80 × 37 = 2960 MBq. 2.16 MBq divides 80/37. 296 MBq uses 3.7 instead of 37 (a factor-of-ten error). 29600 MBq uses 370 instead of 37.

### Q3
Stem: An HDR source is listed as 7.4 GBq. What is that activity in mCi? Use 1 mCi = 37 MBq.
A. 200 mCi
B. 274 mCi
C. 20 mCi
D. 2000 mCi
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 7.4 GBq = 7400 MBq. 7400 / 37 = 200 mCi. Check: 200 × 37 = 7400 MBq. 274 mCi multiplies 7.4 × 37 and ignores the giga-to-mega step. 20 mCi drops a factor of ten on the 7400. 2000 mCi divides by 3.7 instead of 37.

### Q4
Stem: An older text quotes 25 rad at a point. The absorbed dose in gray is:
A. 0.25 Gy
B. 25 Gy
C. 2.5 Gy
D. 250 Gy
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1 Gy = 100 rad, so 25 rad = 25/100 = 0.25 Gy. That is also 25 cGy. 25 Gy treats the rad as a gray. 2.5 Gy divides by 10. 250 Gy multiplies by 10.

### Q5
Stem: A worker’s effective dose is reported as 40 mSv. In rem that is:
A. 4.0 rem
B. 40 rem
C. 400 rem
D. 0.40 rem
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1 Sv = 100 rem, so 1 mSv = 0.1 rem. 40 × 0.1 = 4.0 rem. Equivalently, 40 mSv = 0.040 Sv and 0.040 × 100 = 4.0 rem. 40 rem forgets the milli. 400 rem multiplies by 10. 0.40 rem divides by an extra ten.

### Q6
Stem: An alpha-particle absorbed dose is 0.050 Gy. Using wR = 20, the equivalent dose is:
A. 1.0 Sv
B. 0.050 Sv
C. 0.0025 Sv
D. 20 Sv
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: H = wR × D = 20 × 0.050 = 1.0 Sv. 0.050 Sv uses the photon weighting factor of 1. 0.0025 Sv divides by 20. 20 Sv reports the weighting factor and drops the absorbed dose.

### Q7
Stem: A film badge reads 5000 mrem. What is that dose equivalent in mSv?
A. 50 mSv
B. 5000 mSv
C. 5 mSv
D. 500 mSv
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1000 mrem = 1 rem = 10 mSv, so 5000 mrem = 5 rem = 50 mSv. 5000 mSv treats the milli prefix as already in sievert. 5 mSv drops a factor of ten. 500 mSv moves the decimal the other way (confusing 1 rem with 100 mSv instead of 10 mSv).

### Q8
Stem: A seed assay is 1.85 MBq. What is that activity in μCi? Use 1 mCi = 37 MBq.
A. 50 μCi
B. 1.85 μCi
C. 68.5 μCi
D. 500 μCi
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1.85 / 37 = 0.050 mCi. 0.050 mCi = 50 μCi. Check: 50 × 10^(−3) mCi × 37 MBq/mCi = 1.85 MBq. 1.85 μCi ignores the conversion. 68.5 μCi multiplies 1.85 × 37. 500 μCi is 0.50 mCi, ten times the correct activity.

## Units Quiz

### Q1
Stem: 2.0 Gy equals:
A. 200 cGy
B. 20 cGy
C. 2000 cGy
D. 2.0 cGy
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 2.0 × 100 = 200 cGy. 20 cGy and 2.0 cGy drop one or two factors of ten. 2000 cGy multiplies by 1000.

### Q2
Stem: 370 MBq equals how many mCi? Use 1 mCi = 37 MBq.
A. 10 mCi
B. 37 mCi
C. 100 mCi
D. 1.0 mCi
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 370 / 37 = 10 mCi. 37 mCi stops at the conversion constant. 100 mCi divides by 3.7. 1.0 mCi divides by 370.

### Q3
Stem: 6.0 rem equals:
A. 60 mSv
B. 6.0 mSv
C. 600 mSv
D. 0.60 mSv
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 1 rem = 10 mSv, so 6.0 × 10 = 60 mSv. 6.0 mSv equates rem with mSv. 600 mSv uses 1 rem = 100 mSv. 0.60 mSv moves the decimal two places too far.

### Q4
Stem: 100 R of exposure equals how many C/kg? Use 1 R = 2.58 × 10^(−4) C/kg.
A. 2.58 × 10^(−2) C/kg
B. 2.58 × 10^(−4) C/kg
C. 2.58 × 10^(−6) C/kg
D. 2.58 × 10^2 C/kg
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 100 × 2.58 × 10^(−4) = 2.58 × 10^(−2) C/kg. 2.58 × 10^(−4) forgets the factor of 100. 2.58 × 10^(−6) divides by 100. 2.58 × 10^2 inverts the conversion and still mishandles the prefix.

### Q5
Stem: A calculation ends as 150 cGy. A coworker records 150 Gy. The recorded value is wrong by a factor of:
A. 100
B. 10
C. 1.44
D. 2
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: 150 cGy = 1.50 Gy, not 150 Gy. The ratio 150 / 1.50 = 100, which is the centi prefix. A factor of 10 would be a single decimal slip in the other direction. 1.44 is the mean-life factor and does not convert gray to centigray.

### Q20
Stem: A survey reads 10.0 R in air under equilibrium. Absorbed dose in air, using 0.876 rad/R, is:
A. 0.876 cGy
B. 8.76 cGy
C. 10.0 cGy
D. 87.6 cGy
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: 10.0 × 0.876 = 8.76 rad, and 1 rad = 1 cGy, so the air dose is 8.76 cGy. 0.876 cGy drops a factor of 10. 10.0 cGy treats the roentgen as a centigray. 87.6 cGy moves the decimal the other way.

### Q30
Stem: Gray, sievert, and roentgen name which quantities?
A. Gray is exposure, sievert is activity, and roentgen is absorbed dose in tissue for any particle
B. Gray is absorbed dose, sievert is equivalent or effective dose after weighting, and roentgen is photon exposure in air
C. All three equal 1 J/kg, so a prescription may be written in any of them
D. Roentgen is the SI unit that replaced the gray
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: Answer B. Absorbed dose is energy imparted per unit mass, in gray. Equivalent dose and effective dose are weighted protection quantities, in sievert. Exposure is ionization in air by photons, in roentgen or C/kg. A prescription is absorbed dose. It is not renamed into roentgen, and the three units are not one quantity.

### Q31
Stem: A photon absorbed dose is 3.60 Gy and w_R = 1. Which report keeps gray, sievert, and roentgen apart?
A. 3.60 R of exposure and 3.60 rem
B. 360 Sv
C. 360 cGy of absorbed dose, and 3.60 Sv (360 rem) if the quantity is equivalent dose. It is not 3.60 R
D. 0.0360 Sv
Answer: C
Objective: I.10
Why: Answer C. 3.60 × 100 = 360 cGy = 360 rad. For photons w_R = 1, so the equivalent dose is 3.60 Sv, and 3.60 × 100 = 360 rem. 360 Sv multiplies the gray by 100 and then calls the result a sievert. 0.0360 Sv divides by 100. 3.60 R would be an exposure, and 3.60 rem is a hundred times smaller than 360 rem.

### Q32
Stem: A survey reads 4.00 R in air under equilibrium. Using 0.876 rad/R, the absorbed dose in air is:
A. 4.00 cGy
B. 0.3504 cGy
C. 35.04 cGy
D. 3.504 cGy
Answer: D
Objective: I.10
Why: Answer D. 4.00 × 0.876 = 3.504 rad, and 1 rad = 1 cGy, so the air dose is 3.504 cGy. 4.00 cGy treats the roentgen as a rad. 0.3504 cGy drops a factor of 10. 35.04 cGy moves the decimal the other way. The result is absorbed dose in air. It is not still 4.00 R, and it is not a sievert unless the quantity asked for is equivalent dose.

### Q33
Stem: An alpha absorbed dose is 0.250 Gy with w_R = 20. The equivalent dose is:
A. 0.250 Sv
B. 5.00 Sv
C. 0.250 R
D. 20.0 R
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: Answer B. H = 20 × 0.250 = 5.00 Sv, which is 5.00 × 100 = 500 rem. The same 0.250 Gy of photons, w_R = 1, would remain 0.250 Sv, or 25.0 rem. 0.250 R and 20.0 R are exposures. The roentgen is not obtained by multiplying gray by w_R.

### Q34
Stem: Convert 25.0 R of exposure to C/kg. Use 1 R = 2.58 × 10^(−4) C/kg. Do not report the result as gray or sievert.
A. 2.58 × 10^(−4) C/kg
B. 2.58 × 10^(−2) C/kg
C. 6.45 × 10^(−3) C/kg
D. 6.45 × 10^(−1) C/kg
Answer: C
Objective: I.10
Why: Answer C. 25.0 × 2.58 × 10^(−4) = 6.45 × 10^(−3) C/kg. That is exposure. It is not 6.45 × 10^(−3) Gy and it is not 6.45 × 10^(−3) Sv. 2.58 × 10^(−4) forgets the factor 25.0. 2.58 × 10^(−2) is 100 R. 6.45 × 10^(−1) is 25.0 × 2.58 × 10^(−2), a factor of 100 too large.

### Q35
Stem: A 6 MV prescription is 250 cGy to water. A coworker multiplies by 0.876 and records roentgen, then also calls the prescription a sievert. The consistent report is:
A. 219 R
B. 250 Sv
C. 2.50 R
D. 2.50 Gy of absorbed dose, equal to 250 rad. The factor 0.876 starts from the roentgen and does not apply to a dose already in cGy. For these photons, w_R = 1, so an equivalent dose would be 2.50 Sv only if that quantity was requested
Answer: D
Objective: I.10
Why: Answer D. 250 cGy = 2.50 Gy = 250 rad. 250 × 0.876 = 219, which is the air f-factor applied to a number that is already centigray, and the unit roentgen is still the wrong quantity. 250 Sv treats every centigray as a sievert. 2.50 R renames the gray as exposure. The prescription remains absorbed dose in gray.

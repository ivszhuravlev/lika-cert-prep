# Module 34. Effects of beam modifying devices

Original items. Physical, motorized, and dynamic wedges; dynamic-wedge factor behavior and limits; electron skin collimation.

## Wedge Taxonomy and Electron Skin Collimation

### Q1
Stem: A linac has a single 60 degree wedge permanently housed inside the treatment head and driven into the beam by a motor. Intermediate effective wedge angles are produced by delivering part of the monitor units open and the rest through the wedge. What is this device called?
A. An individual physical wedge
B. A universal, or motorized, wedge
C. A dynamic wedge
D. A missing-tissue compensator
Answer: B
Objective: IV.2
Why: The universal or motorized (upper) wedge is one 60 degree wedge inside the head whose effective angle is set by weighting open and wedged monitor units in the planning system. An individual physical wedge is one external wedge per nominal angle, mounted on the tray by the therapist. A dynamic wedge uses a moving collimator jaw and has no metal in the beam. A compensator replaces missing tissue and is milled per patient.

### Q2
Stem: Which property belongs to a dynamic (or virtual) wedge and not to a physical wedge?
A. It hardens the beam as the thick end attenuates the low-energy photons
B. It must be mounted on the tray by the therapist for every field
C. Its effective angle is limited to 15, 30, 45, or 60 degrees
D. Any angle up to 60 degrees is available by software, the beam is not hardened, and scatter outside the field is lower because no metal is in the beam
Answer: D
Objective: IV.2
Why: A dynamic wedge produces its tilt by sweeping one collimator jaw during beam-on, so the angle is a software setting, the spectrum is unchanged, and the peripheral scatter that comes from a metal wedge is absent. Beam hardening and therapist mounting are properties of physical wedges. A fixed set of nominal angles describes the physical wedge set, not the dynamic wedge.

### Q3
Stem: A 60 degree enhanced dynamic wedge is measured on a 10 by 10 cm field and again on a 20 by 20 cm field, with 20 cm along the wedge direction. How does the wedge factor change, and how does that compare with a physical wedge?
A. It falls substantially on the larger field, whereas a physical wedge factor changes little, rising slightly with field size
B. It rises substantially on the larger field, whereas a physical wedge factor falls
C. It does not change with field size, the same as a physical wedge
D. It is 1.0 at both field sizes because no metal is in the beam
Answer: A
Objective: IV.2
Why: On a longer field the jaw must sweep farther and the central axis is covered for a larger share of the monitor units, so the central-axis dose per monitor unit, which is the wedge factor, drops markedly; a physical wedge factor moves the other way and by only a few percent because more wedge scatter reaches the axis on a large field. The bank uses this direction because it follows from the sweep mechanism and matches vendor tables; a statement that the dynamic-wedge factor rises with field size reverses the mechanism. The factor is well below 1.0 because the axis is covered for part of the delivery.

### Q4
Stem: Which is a limitation of a dynamic wedge compared with a physical wedge?
A. It cannot produce wedge angles greater than 15 degrees
B. It cannot be used together with MLC field shaping
C. There is a minimum field size in the wedge direction, a minimum number of monitor units per field, and the wedge direction is fixed to the moving-jaw axis
D. It increases the scattered dose outside the field
Answer: C
Objective: IV.2
Why: The moving jaw needs room to sweep and must stop short of the fixed jaw, the sweep must be spread over enough monitor units to control it, and the gradient can only lie along the jaw's travel direction, so the collimator is rotated to orient the wedge. Dynamic wedges reach 60 degrees. MLC shaping is used with dynamic wedges routinely, with the usual rule that leaves do not sit where the moving jaw must travel. Scatter outside the field is lower than with a physical wedge, not higher.

### Q5
Stem: A plan calls for an effective 30 degree wedge on a machine that has only a universal 60 degree motorized wedge. How is the field delivered?
A. Half of the field is covered by the wedge and half is left open
B. The 60 degree wedge is rotated 30 degrees about the central axis
C. Two fields are treated, each with a 30 degree physical wedge from the tray
D. Part of the monitor units are delivered open and the rest through the 60 degree wedge, with the planning system setting the two weights so the combined isodose tilt is 30 degrees
Answer: D
Objective: IV.2
Why: The motorized wedge produces intermediate angles by combining an open segment and a fully wedged segment of the same field in a dose-weighted ratio; the planning system computes the weights and the two segments are delivered in sequence. The wedge is either fully in or fully out, not half-covering. Rotating the wedge changes its orientation, not its angle. The machine in the stem has no physical wedge set.

### Q6
Stem: An electron field around the nose uses a cutout in the applicator, but the applicator end sits several centimeters above the irregular surface and the physician wants a sharp field edge at the skin to protect the eyes. What is the appropriate device?
A. Thicker bolus over the whole field
B. Skin collimation, a lead or Cerrobend mask shaped to the field outline and placed directly on the skin, with lead about E/2 millimeters thick for the energy in MeV
C. A physical wedge inserted in the applicator
D. A larger applicator at an extended SSD
Answer: B
Objective: III.7
Why: The electron penumbra widens across an air gap because electrons scatter laterally in air, so defining the edge at the skin surface with a shaped lead mask restores a sharp edge and shields the adjacent lens and eye; the thickness rule is about E/2 mm of lead (Cerrobend about 1.2 times thicker). Bolus shifts dmax and does not sharpen the edge. Wedges are photon devices in this context. A larger applicator or longer SSD widens the penumbra further.

### Q7
Stem: An electron applicator is used at 110 cm SSD instead of the standard 100 cm, and no skin collimation is added. What happens to the penumbra at the skin?
A. It widens, because electrons scatter laterally in the extra air between the cutout and the skin
B. It narrows, because the beam divergence is smaller at the longer distance
C. It is unchanged, because the cutout defines the edge regardless of distance
D. It disappears, because the air absorbs the edge electrons
Answer: A
Objective: III.7
Why: The cutout defines the edge at the applicator, and every centimeter of air beyond it lets edge electrons scatter sideways, so the 80 to 20 percent penumbra grows with the gap; skin collimation moves the edge definition to the surface and removes that growth. Divergence is a geometric effect that does not sharpen an electron edge. The cutout does not define the edge at the skin when there is a gap. Air broadens the beam; it does not remove the penumbra.

# Module 5. Characteristics of X-Rays and Particle Beams

Original items. Gaps identified in the RM Review port: light-field rule, penumbra definitions, FFF beam numbers.

## Light Field and Penumbra Definitions

### Q1
Stem: The edge of the light field on a properly adjusted linac corresponds to which isodose line of the radiation beam at the depth of maximum dose?
A. 90%
B. 80%
C. 50%
D. 20%
Answer: C
Objective: I.3
Why: Field size is defined at the 50% isodose line at dmax, and the light field is aligned to that line at commissioning. A structure placed at the light edge therefore receives only about half the central-axis dose, which is why the 90% or 95% line is used for coverage and a margin is added. 80% and 20% are the bounds of the physical penumbra, not the field edge.

### Q2
Stem: The physical penumbra of a photon beam profile is measured as the lateral distance between which two isodose values?
A. 100% and 50%
B. 80% and 20%
C. 95% and 5%
D. 50% and 0%
Answer: B
Objective: I.3
Why: The standard definition is the distance between the 80% and 20% lines on a profile at a stated depth. Some texts use 90% to 10%, but 80 to 20 is the convention this bank follows. 100 to 50 would be half the field width and would include the flat region. 95 to 5 and 50 to 0 are not recognized penumbra definitions.

### Q3
Stem: A Co-60 unit has a 2.0 cm diameter source and a collimator whose distal face is 45 cm from the source. SSD is 80 cm. What is the geometric penumbra at a depth of 10 cm?
A. 1.6 cm
B. 2.0 cm
C. 0.9 cm
D. 4.0 cm
Answer: B
Objective: I.3
Why: Geometric penumbra equals source diameter times (SSD plus depth minus SCD) divided by SCD: 2.0 times (80 plus 10 minus 45) divided by 45 equals 2.0 times 45 divided by 45, which is 2.0 cm. Check: 45 over 45 is 1, so the penumbra equals the source size. 1.6 cm drops the depth and uses 80 minus 45. 0.9 cm inverts the ratio. 4.0 cm doubles the source size by treating diameter as radius.

### Q4
Stem: Which single change reduces the geometric penumbra of a photon beam?
A. Moving the collimating device closer to the patient
B. Increasing the SSD
C. Treating at a greater depth
D. Switching from a linac to a Co-60 source
Answer: A
Objective: I.3
Why: Bringing the collimator closer to the patient increases the source-to-collimator distance, which is the denominator in the penumbra formula, so the penumbra shrinks; that is the reason electron applicators and SRS cones end near the skin. Longer SSD and greater depth both increase the source-to-point distance and widen the penumbra. A Co-60 source is one to two centimeters across, far larger than a linac focal spot, and widens the penumbra.

### Q5
Stem: Dose at the field edge that comes from photons passing through the tapered tip of a collimator jaw or block is called:
A. Geometric penumbra
B. Scatter penumbra
C. Transmission penumbra
D. Physical penumbra
Answer: C
Objective: I.3
Why: Transmission penumbra is the leakage through the thin edge of the collimating material; a focused, divergent jaw face minimizes it. Geometric penumbra comes from the finite source size viewed from the edge. Scatter from the patient broadens the edge further. The physical penumbra is the measured 80 to 20% distance that contains all three contributions.

## Flattening-Filter-Free Beam Facts

### Q1
Stem: A 6 MV flattened beam has a percent depth dose of about 67% at 10 cm for a 10 by 10 field. The 6 MV flattening-filter-free beam on the same machine has a percent depth dose at 10 cm of approximately:
A. 64%
B. 67%
C. 70%
D. 73%
Answer: A
Objective: I.3
Why: The filter hardened the beam; without it the spectrum is softer and PDD at 10 cm falls by about 3 percentage points, to roughly 64%. 67% would mean no change, which ignores the spectral shift. 70% has the direction wrong. 73% is the flattened 10 MV value, not a 6 MV FFF value.

### Q2
Stem: The maximum dose rate commonly commissioned for a 10 MV flattening-filter-free beam is about:
A. 400 MU/min
B. 600 MU/min
C. 1400 MU/min
D. 2400 MU/min
Answer: D
Objective: I.5
Why: With the filter removed the central-axis fluence is no longer attenuated, so 10 MV FFF runs at about 2400 MU/min, roughly four times a flattened beam. 600 MU/min is the usual flattened maximum. 1400 MU/min is the 6 MV FFF figure. 400 MU/min is a typical older flattened rate. The higher dose per pulse also increases chamber recombination during measurement.

### Q3
Stem: Compared with the flattened beam of the same nominal energy, the surface dose of a flattening-filter-free beam is:
A. Lower, because the beam is harder
B. Higher, because the spectrum is softer and more low-energy photons and contaminant electrons reach the skin
C. Identical, because surface dose depends only on nominal energy
D. Lower, because the dose rate is higher
Answer: B
Objective: I.3
Why: Removing the filter removes the absorber that hardened the beam and stopped low-energy photons and electrons from the target and primary collimator, so the buildup-region dose rises by several percent. The FFF beam is softer, not harder. Surface dose depends on the spectrum, field size, and head design, not on nominal energy alone. Dose rate changes how fast the dose arrives, not the depth-dose shape.

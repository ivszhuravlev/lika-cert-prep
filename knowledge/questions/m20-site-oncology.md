# Module 20 — Site Specific Clinical Oncology

Original items. Totals below are typical regimens for recognition. The signed prescription is the patient’s dose. When a stem prints a constraint, apply that constraint.

## Advice for Oncology Section

### Q1
Stem: A list says 40 Gy in 15 fractions and names no disease site. What can you assign?
A. Whole-breast hypofractionation only
B. A short glioblastoma course only
C. No site yet; 40 Gy in 15 is used for both breast and a short glioblastoma course
D. Peripheral lung stereotactic treatment, because 15 fractions is more than 3
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Forty gray in fifteen fractions is a standard hypofractionated breast course and also a short course used for some elderly or frail patients with glioblastoma. The fraction size is a clue. The organ or diagnosis decides which regimen it is, and this stem gives neither. Peripheral lung stereotactic courses sit near 50–54 Gy in 3 to 5 fractions.

### Q2
Stem: A painful bone metastasis is prescribed 8 Gy in one fraction. How is that schedule classified?
A. Single-fraction stereotactic body radiotherapy
B. Palliative treatment for pain
C. Definitive high-70s prostate treatment
D. Preoperative long-course rectal radiotherapy
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Eight gray once, 20 Gy in 5, and 30 Gy in 10 are bone-pain schedules. A single 8 Gy fraction is ordinary palliation. Lung stereotactic schedules use a higher course dose in a few fractions. A definitive prostate course is in the high 70s at 1.8–2 Gy per fraction, or a hypofractionated prostate schedule such as 60 Gy in 20. Long-course rectum is 50.4 Gy in 28.

### Q3
Stem: The question is whether the spinal cord is safe, and the only cord dose offered is a mean dose. What is wrong with using that metric here?
A. Nothing; every organ is scored by mean dose
B. The cord is serial, so a small high-dose region is the relevant end of the dose distribution
C. The cord is parallel, so only a lung-style V20 answers it
D. Mean dose is the definition of D2%
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Cord, brainstem, and optic pathway fail when a small segment is taken to a high dose. The metric is a maximum or a near-maximum. Lung, liver, kidney, and parotid are parallel, so mean dose or a volume above a threshold is the usual score. A mean does not answer a cord-maximum question, and D2% is a near-maximum dose, not the mean.

### Q4
Stem: The only constraint printed in the stem is spinal cord maximum dose ≤ 45 Gy. The cord maximum is 44 Gy. Lung V20 Gy is 40%, and no lung limit is printed. Which conclusion follows from the stem?
A. The printed cord constraint is met. A lung limit was not given, so 40% cannot be failed against an unstated number.
B. The plan fails a universal lung V20 of 30%
C. The cord fails because 44 Gy is near 45 Gy
D. V20 Gy and maximum dose are the same quantity
Answer: A
Objective: III.8
Why: 44 Gy is below the printed maximum of 45 Gy, so that cord line is met. A lung percent remembered from a different guideline is not a constraint in this stem. V20 Gy is the volume receiving at least 20 Gy. It is a different metric from a maximum dose in gray.

### Q5
Stem: Two prostate courses are both labeled 60 Gy. One is 60 Gy in 30 fractions. The other is 60 Gy in 20 fractions. What is true?
A. They are the same treatment because the total dose matches
B. They are different treatments: 2 Gy per fraction versus 3 Gy per fraction
C. Both are whole-brain palliation of 30 Gy in 10
D. The 20-fraction course is 8 Gy bone palliation
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Dose per fraction is 60/30 = 2 Gy in the first course and 60/20 = 3 Gy in the second. The same total dose does not make the schedules biologically identical. Sixty gray in 20 fractions is moderate prostate hypofractionation. It is a different regimen from 30 Gy in 10 and from 8 Gy in one fraction.

## Overview (Notes)

### Q1
Stem: Thirty gray in 10 fractions covers the whole brain, and the field edge is kept off the lenses when the technique allows. What regimen is this?
A. Definitive glioblastoma, 60 Gy in 30 fractions, to a partial brain volume
B. Typical whole-brain palliation
C. Hypofractionated whole breast, 40 Gy in 15
D. Peripheral lung treatment, 54 Gy in 3
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Thirty gray in ten fractions is the usual palliative whole-brain course. A shorter course such as 20 Gy in 5 is sometimes used for poor performance status. Sixty gray in 30 to a partial brain volume is definitive glioblastoma treatment. The breast and lung schedules use different totals and different organs. Scalp and lenses are the whole-brain field issues when the technique can respect the lenses.

### Q2
Stem: An elderly patient with glioblastoma near the brainstem receives 40 Gy in 15 fractions to a partial brain volume. Which pairing fits the stem?
A. Breast hypofractionation, limited by heart and ipsilateral lung
B. A short glioblastoma course, limited by brainstem and optic structures when the tumor is next to them
C. Peripheral lung stereotactic radiotherapy
D. Sixty gray in 30 fractions, because every brain dose is 60 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Forty gray in fifteen fractions can be breast hypofractionation or a short glioblastoma course. This stem names glioblastoma and the brainstem, so the site is brain. Brainstem, chiasm, optic nerves, and eyes limit a target in that location. The prescribed course is 40 Gy in 15, which is 40/15 = 2.67 Gy per fraction, not 60 Gy in 30 fractions of 2 Gy.

### Q3
Stem: Gross head-and-neck disease is prescribed 69.96 Gy in 33 fractions of 2.12 Gy. With α/β = 10 Gy, what is the EQD2?
A. 60.0 Gy
B. 69.96 Gy
C. 70.7 Gy
D. 80.0 Gy
Answer: C
Objective: III.3
Why: EQD2 = 69.96 × (2.12 + 10) / (2 + 10) = 69.96 × 12.12 / 12 = 69.96 × 1.01 = 70.66 Gy, which is 70.7 Gy to one decimal. That is why 69.96 Gy in 33 fractions sits with 70 Gy in 35 fractions in the same “about 70 Gy” recognition bin for a tumor α/β of 10. Sixty gray is a typical postoperative dose, not this product. 69.96 Gy is the physical dose. The product does not reach 80 Gy.

### Q4
Stem: Whole breast is prescribed 40 Gy in 15 fractions. For late effects, α/β = 3 Gy. What is the EQD2?
A. 40.0 Gy
B. 42.2 Gy
C. 45.3 Gy
D. 50.4 Gy
Answer: C
Objective: III.3
Why: d = 40/15 = 8/3 Gy. EQD2 = 40 × (8/3 + 3) / (2 + 3) = 40 × (17/3) / 5 = 40 × 17 / 15 = 680/15 = 45.3 Gy. With α/β = 10, EQD2 = 40 × (8/3 + 10) / 12 = 40 × (38/3) / 12 = 40 × 38 / 36 = 42.2 Gy. That 42.2 Gy figure is the generic early or tumor conversion, not the late one asked here. Forty gray is the physical dose. 50.4 Gy is a conventional course total, not this EQD2.

### Q5
Stem: A prostate receives 60 Gy in 20 fractions. The stem sets α/β = 1.5 Gy. What is the EQD2?
A. 65 Gy, the result of using α/β = 10
B. 77.1 Gy
C. 60 Gy, because EQD2 equals physical dose for every fraction size
D. 54 Gy, a 3-fraction lung total copied onto the prostate
Answer: B
Objective: III.3
Why: d = 60/20 = 3 Gy. EQD2 = 60 × (3 + 1.5) / (2 + 1.5) = 60 × 4.5 / 3.5 = 60 × 9/7 = 77.1 Gy. That sits in the same high-70s bin as a conventional prostate course. Using α/β = 10 gives 60 × (3 + 10) / (2 + 10) = 60 × 13/12 = 65 Gy and makes the course look low. The stem’s α/β is 1.5 Gy, so the EQD2 to report is 77.1 Gy. Rectum and bladder are the organs that usually limit the plan.

### Q6
Stem: Cervix treatment is recorded as 45 Gy external beam to the pelvis, and the tally stops there. What has been left out?
A. Nothing; 45 Gy external beam is the full tumor dose
B. Brachytherapy, which is what brings the high-risk CTV to a high EQD2
C. A whole-brain course of 30 Gy in 10 added to every pelvis
D. Replacement of the brachytherapy by 20 Gy in 10, as for stage I seminoma
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Typical cervix external beam is about 45 Gy to the pelvis. Image-guided brachytherapy is part of the course. ABS and EMBRACE discussions place the high-risk CTV near the mid-to-high 80s Gy EQD2 at α/β = 10. Confirm the goal and the rectal, sigmoid, and bladder EQD2 limits in the named protocol. External beam alone is not the whole treatment. Whole-brain and seminoma schedules belong to other sites.

### Q7
Stem: Two preoperative rectal regimens are prescribed in the same clinic: 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions and 25 Gy in 5 fractions. Which statement is correct?
A. They are biologically equal because both are given before surgery
B. 25 Gy in 5 is a peripheral lung stereotactic course
C. Both are standard rectal regimens, and they are not equal; 25 Gy in 5 is not lung stereotactic treatment
D. 25 Gy in 5 is the same prescription as 54 Gy in 3
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Long course is 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions of 1.8 Gy, usually with chemotherapy. Short course is 25 Gy in 5 fractions of 5 Gy. For α/β = 10, short-course EQD2 = 25 × (5 + 10) / (2 + 10) = 25 × 15/12 = 31.25 Gy, while long course is about 50 Gy EQD2. Small bowel often limits the high dose. Peripheral lung stereotactic totals cluster near 50–54 Gy in 3 to 5 fractions, so 25 Gy in 5 is a different regimen.

### Q8
Stem: A peripheral lung lesion is treated to 54 Gy in 3 fractions. The same 54 Gy in 3 is then copied onto an ultracentral lesion because the numbers were familiar. What is the mistake?
A. There is no mistake; one stereotactic schedule covers peripheral and ultracentral lung
B. An ultracentral target beside the bronchial tree, esophagus, or great vessels needs the milder schedule in the named protocol
C. 54 Gy in 3 is the standard whole-brain dose
D. The ultracentral lesion should be changed to 8 Gy in one fraction to the bronchus
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Schedules to recognize for peripheral lung include 54 Gy in 3, 50 Gy in 5, and 48 Gy in 4. Central and ultracentral targets use a milder dose specified by the protocol. Confirm that dose in the named protocol. Whole-brain palliation is 30 Gy in 10. Eight gray once is bone palliation. The familiar peripheral number is the wrong number for the ultracentral geometry.

## Oncology Quiz

### Q1
Stem: Late tissue is assigned α/β = 3 Gy. A painful rib receives 8 Gy in 1 fraction. What is the late EQD2, and what is the intent?
A. 17.6 Gy, single-fraction bone palliation
B. 12 Gy, and that 12 Gy is the late result
C. 36 Gy, the same late EQD2 as 30 Gy in 10
D. 54 Gy in 3 fractions to the rib, because one fraction is stereotactic
Answer: A
Objective: III.3
Why: EQD2 = 8 × (8 + 3) / (2 + 3) = 8 × 11/5 = 17.6 Gy. With α/β = 10 the same physical dose gives 8 × (8 + 10) / 12 = 8 × 18/12 = 12 Gy, which is the generic early or tumor figure. Thirty gray in 10 at α/β = 3 is 30 × (3 + 3) / 5 = 30 × 6/5 = 36 Gy EQD2, a different schedule. The intent of 8 Gy once to a painful rib is pain relief.

### Q2
Stem: Stage I seminoma is treated with 20 Gy in 10 fractions to a para-aortic volume. Which description fits?
A. Palliative coverage of a lumbar bone metastasis
B. A low definitive para-aortic dose for seminoma
C. Peripheral lung stereotactic radiotherapy of 50 Gy in 5
D. Whole-brain palliation of 30 Gy in 10
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Twenty gray in ten fractions is a typical stage I seminoma para-aortic course. Dose per fraction is 20/10 = 2 Gy. A low abdominal dose is not automatically 8 Gy once, 20 Gy in 5, or 30 Gy in 10 for bone pain. It is not a lung stereotactic total and it is not whole-brain treatment. The kidneys and bowel are the abdominal organs around that volume.

### Q3
Stem: A mid-esophageal tumor receives 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions with chemotherapy. Which organs usually shape that plan?
A. Heart, lungs, and spinal cord
B. Rectum and bladder, because the numbers match long-course rectum
C. Lenses and scalp
D. The femoral heads alone
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions of 1.8 Gy is also a long-course rectal regimen, but this stem is esophagus. Heart, lungs, and cord are the organs that limit an esophageal plan. Rectum and bladder belong to the pelvic course that shares the numbers. Lenses belong to whole brain. Femoral heads are a pelvic bone on a prostate or rectal plan.

### Q4
Stem: The printed constraint is both-lungs V20 Gy ≤ 30%. The plan shows right-lung V20 = 22%, left-lung V20 = 28%, and both-lungs V20 = 34%. What follows?
A. The constraint is met because each lung alone is under 30%
B. The printed both-lungs constraint fails, because 34% is above 30%
C. V20 is a maximum dose in gray, so the percent has no meaning
D. The cord maximum has been exceeded
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: V20 Gy is the volume receiving at least 20 Gy. The stem applied it to both lungs. 34% is greater than 30%, so that printed line fails. A single lung under 30% does not meet a both-lungs metric. No cord dose appears in the stem, and a percent volume is not a maximum dose in gray.

### Q5
Stem: Tangents for a left-sided breast cancer are compared with tangents for a right-sided breast cancer. What is the cardiac distinction?
A. Both sides deliver the same dose to the heart and to the left anterior descending region
B. The left-sided tangents are the cardiac case, including the left anterior descending region
C. The right breast is the side that always encloses the left ventricle
D. Breast fractionation removes heart dose, so laterality can be ignored
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Left-sided tangents approach the heart and the left anterior descending territory. A right-sided pair does not see that same heart volume. Ipsilateral lung and contralateral breast are the other usual breast limits. Deep-inspiration breath hold is a technique for the left-sided geometry. It does not change which side the heart is on.

### Q6
Stem: A spine field of 30 Gy in 10 fractions is proposed. The cord in that field was treated before. What happens to the familiar palliative number?
A. It is used unchanged, because palliative schedules ignore prior dose
B. The prior cord dose has to be reconciled before the schedule is accepted
C. It is replaced by 54 Gy in 3, because every spine field is peripheral lung stereotactic treatment
D. An extra 8 Gy is added on top of the 30 Gy because both are palliative
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Thirty gray in ten fractions is a standard bone-pain regimen when the cord budget allows it. Prior cord dose can make that familiar number the wrong number. It is not converted into 54 Gy in 3, and it is not stacked with a second palliative schedule by default. The cord is serial, so the high-dose end of the combined course is the quantity to check.

### Q7
Stem: One plan is 60 Gy in 30 fractions to a partial brain volume. Another is 30 Gy in 10 fractions to the whole brain. Which one is typical definitive glioblastoma treatment?
A. 30 Gy in 10 fractions
B. 60 Gy in 30 fractions
C. Both, because both volumes contain brain
D. Neither; both totals are hypofractionated breast doses
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Sixty gray in 30 fractions of 2 Gy to the protocol volume is the typical definitive glioblastoma course. Nearby limits are brainstem, optic structures, eyes, and uninvolved brain when the tumor approaches them. Thirty gray in 10 is whole-brain palliation. Sharing the word brain does not make the courses interchangeable. The breast total that overlaps a short glioblastoma course is 40 Gy in 15, which is neither of these two.

### Q8
Stem: A lung stereotactic plan starts from a familiar peripheral dose. The lesion is ultracentral, and the patient has a pacemaker. Why might that familiar tumor dose be the wrong number to copy?
A. It is always right; one peripheral dose covers every lung target and every implanted device
B. Ultracentral geometry needs the protocol’s central schedule, and the device limit comes from device guidance rather than from the tumor dose
C. The pacemaker requires 18 MV photons
D. An ultracentral lesion is treated with total skin electrons
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Peripheral schedules such as 54 Gy in 3 are a different problem from an ultracentral target next to bronchus, esophagus, or great vessels. The central dose is the one in the named protocol. A pacemaker limit is device-specific. It is not the tumor prescription, and it is not a reason to choose 18 MV. Total skin electrons are a different procedure. Chest wall, cord, and great vessels remain the organs to read against the stereotactic table the protocol names.

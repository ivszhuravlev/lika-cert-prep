# Module 20 — Site-specific clinical oncology

Original practice items for CMD preparation. Not retired MDCB items and not a commercial quiz. Doses below are the common regimens used to recognize fractionation on a planning exam, not a prescription for a specific patient.

## Advice for Oncology Section

### Q1
Stem: A planning exam asks whether a written dose is conventional, palliative, moderately hypofractionated, or stereotactic. Which set of fractions is classified correctly?
A. 1.8 to 2.0 Gy per fraction is conventional; 8 Gy in one fraction is a standard palliative bone dose; 7 Gy and above per fraction, in a short course to a small volume, is in stereotactic territory
B. 8 Gy in one fraction is myeloablative total-body irradiation; 2 Gy per fraction is stereotactic by definition
C. 3 Gy per fraction is always stereotactic body radiotherapy, regardless of the number of fractions and the volume
D. Conventional fractionation is 12 Gy in a single fraction
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer A. The exam expects the planner to recognize the class of a prescription before arguing about technique. Conventional radical fractionation sits near 1.8 to 2.0 Gy per fraction, five days a week. A single 8 Gy fraction, 20 Gy in 5, and 30 Gy in 10 are the familiar palliative bone schedules. Stereotactic radiosurgery and stereotactic body radiotherapy use large doses per fraction, often about 7 to 20 Gy, in one to five fractions, on a small, tightly localized target. Three Gy times ten is a common palliative course, not stereotactic body radiotherapy. Twelve Gy as a single fraction is not conventional fractionation; a fractionated 12 Gy total-body course is a different regimen given in smaller fractions.

### Q2
Stem: A T1 squamous cell carcinoma is confined to one true vocal cord. The cord moves normally and there are no involved nodes. Which nodal decision matches the disease?
A. Elective irradiation of both sides of the neck to 50 Gy, because every head-and-neck primary drains bilaterally
B. No elective neck, because the true glottis has very sparse lymphatics and a T1 lesion is treated with small opposed lateral fields on the larynx
C. Elective inguinal irradiation, because laryngeal lymphatics drain to the groin
D. Para-aortic irradiation to 20 Gy, as for seminoma
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. Early glottic cancer is the standard exception to "head and neck means bilateral neck." The true vocal cords have little lymphatic drainage, so a T1N0 lesion is treated with small fields over the larynx, often to the mid-60 Gy range in conventional fractions, without a comprehensive nodal volume. Nasopharynx, base of tongue, and supraglottic larynx do not get that exemption. Inguinal and para-aortic patterns belong to other primary sites.

### Q3
Stem: A patient with a left testicular seminoma has had an orchiectomy through an inguinal incision. The scrotal skin was not violated. Which nodal volume matches the lymphatic drainage?
A. Inguinal nodes, because the testis drains like the scrotal skin
B. Para-aortic nodes, following the gonadal vessels; the left side emphasizes the nodes near the left renal hilum, and the groin is not electively treated
C. Axillary and internal mammary nodes
D. Retropharyngeal nodes
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. Testicular lymphatics follow the spermatic cord and the gonadal veins, not the scrotal skin. The landing zone is the para-aortic region: left-sided tumors emphasize nodes around the left renal vein, and right-sided tumors emphasize paracaval and interaortocaval nodes. A stage I seminoma field, when radiotherapy is used, is typically the para-aortic nodes to about 20 Gy in 10 fractions. Inguinal nodes are treated if the scrotum was surgically violated or if there is other scrotal involvement. Axillary, internal mammary, and retropharyngeal nodes are not the testicular drainage.

### Q4
Stem: Which prescription is stereotactic body radiotherapy rather than conventional palliative fractionation for a painful bone metastasis with no cord compression?
A. 8 Gy in 1 fraction
B. 20 Gy in 5 fractions
C. 30 Gy in 10 fractions
D. 18 Gy in 3 fractions to a small spinal target with a cord constraint tighter than a conventional cord limit
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer D. Eight gray once, 20 Gy in five, and 30 Gy in ten are conventional palliative schedules. They use large fields and simple technique and do not require stereotactic immobilization. Eighteen gray times three on a small spine volume is a stereotactic regimen: high dose per fraction, steep falloff, and a cord constraint much tighter than the 45 to 50 Gy conventional cord limit. Calling every one-fraction course stereotactic is the error the dose in A is there to prevent.

### Q5
Stem: A left breast tangent plan covers the PTV and includes 3 cm of the heart in the field. The planner argues that coverage means the plan is finished. Which organ-at-risk principle is being skipped?
A. Heart dose is irrelevant if the breast PTV is covered, because the heart is parallel and has no planning limit
B. For a left breast, the heart is a primary reason to change technique: deep-inspiration breath hold, a heart block, or field-in-field shaping to pull the heart out while keeping coverage
C. The heart is blocked by raising the energy to 18 MV, which also improves skin dose
D. Cardiac devices and the heart muscle are the same constraint
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Answer B. Coverage of the PTV is not the only endpoint. Mean heart dose is kept as low as reasonably achievable for a left breast because cardiac risk rises with dose, and a 3 cm heart depth on a tangent is a technique problem, not an acceptable default. Breath hold increases the lung volume and pulls the heart posterior and inferior, out of the tangent. Blocking and field-in-field segments do the same geometric job. Eighteen MV would underdose the superficial breast and, if a pacemaker were present, would add neutrons. A pacemaker constraint and a heart-muscle constraint are both cardiac issues and they are not the same number.

## Overview (Notes)

### Q1
Stem: A fit patient with a newly diagnosed glioblastoma will receive concurrent temozolomide. Which external-beam prescription is the standard radical course, as opposed to a short palliative course?
A. 30 Gy in 10 fractions to the whole brain
B. 60 Gy in 30 fractions to the involved brain volume
C. 8 Gy in 1 fraction
D. 20 Gy in 10 fractions to the para-aortic nodes
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. The Stupp radical regimen is 60 Gy in 30 fractions of 2 Gy to the tumor and a margin, with concurrent and adjuvant temozolomide. Thirty gray in ten is a short course used for poor performance status or for whole-brain palliation, not the radical six-week course. Eight gray in one is a bone palliation dose. Twenty gray in ten to the para-aortic nodes is a seminoma dose. The planning volumes are large enough that the optic structures, brainstem, and uninvolved brain drive the fractionation and the margin, which is why this regimen stays at 2 Gy per fraction rather than a stereotactic schedule.

### Q2
Stem: A locally advanced head-and-neck squamous cell carcinoma is treated with simultaneous integrated boost intensity modulation. Which dose pattern matches a conventional radical course?
A. About 70 Gy in 35 fractions to gross disease, with elective mucosa and nodes nearer 50 to 56 Gy
B. 20 Gy in 10 fractions to gross disease and 70 Gy to uninvolved nodes
C. 54 Gy in 3 fractions to gross disease
D. 36 Gy of total skin electrons
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer A. Gross head-and-neck disease is treated to about 70 Gy at 2 Gy per fraction, or to a biologically similar simultaneous boost such as 69.96 Gy in 33 fractions of 2.12 Gy. Elective regions that are at risk but clinically negative receive a lower dose, commonly about 50 to 56 Gy, because microscopic disease requires less dose than a gross mass. Reversing those doses treats the wrong tissue. Fifty-four gray in three fractions is a lung stereotactic prescription, not a comprehensive mucosal volume. Total skin electrons treat cutaneous lymphoma.

### Q3
Stem: A patient with locally advanced cervical cancer completes 45 Gy in 25 fractions to the pelvis. The planner tells the physician the radical dose has been delivered. What is missing?
A. Nothing; 45 Gy is a curative dose for gross cervical disease
B. Intracavitary or interstitial brachytherapy, which brings the high-risk clinical target to a total equivalent dose in 2 Gy fractions on the order of 80 to 85 Gy
C. A further 40 Gy of whole-abdomen photons
D. Prophylactic cranial irradiation to 25 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. Pelvic external beam of about 45 Gy in 25 fractions covers the uterus, parametria, and pelvic nodes. It is not the curative dose to the cervical gross disease. Brachytherapy is what raises the high-risk clinical target volume, historically described at point A, to a combined equivalent dose in 2 Gy fractions in the mid-80 Gy range, while the bladder, rectum, and sigmoid are held to their own limits. Stopping at 45 Gy leaves the central tumor underdosed. Whole-abdomen photons and prophylactic cranial irradiation are not part of standard definitive cervical treatment.

### Q4
Stem: A 1.5 cm peripheral lung tumor, more than 2 cm from the proximal bronchial tree, is treated with stereotactic body radiotherapy. A 4 cm tumor that touches the main bronchus is planned by the same physician. Which fractionation distinction is the safe one?
A. Both receive 54 Gy in 3 fractions of 18 Gy, because size and location do not change lung stereotactic dose
B. The peripheral tumor may receive an ablative three-fraction regimen such as 54 Gy in 3; the central tumor is not a three-fraction ablative candidate and needs more fractions and a lower dose per fraction
C. The central tumor receives the higher dose per fraction because the bronchus is radioresistant
D. Peripheral tumors are treated to 60 Gy in 30 fractions and central tumors to 8 Gy in 1 fraction
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. RTOG 0236 gave 54 Gy in 3 fractions to small peripheral tumors and excluded lesions in the zone around the proximal bronchial tree because of fatal central toxicity. Central tumors are treated with more fractions, commonly five or more, if stereotactic treatment is appropriate at all. A 4 cm central mass is not given 18 Gy times three because the peripheral protocol existed. Conventional 60 Gy in 30 fractions is a different, non-ablative approach used for larger or regionally advanced disease, not the peripheral stereotactic schedule.

### Q5
Stem: A rectal adenocarcinoma and an anal squamous cell carcinoma both sit in the pelvis. Which treatment pattern matches which disease?
A. Both are treated with short-course radiotherapy alone and no chemotherapy, and neither involves inguinal nodes
B. Rectal adenocarcinoma is commonly treated with preoperative radiotherapy (about 45 to 50.4 Gy in 25 to 28 fractions, or 25 Gy in 5) followed by surgery; anal squamous cell carcinoma is treated with definitive chemoradiotherapy, often including inguinal nodes, with organ preservation
C. Anal cancer is treated like seminoma, to 20 Gy para-aortic only
D. Rectal cancer is treated with total skin electrons
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. The histology changes the plan. Rectal adenocarcinoma is a surgical disease with a neoadjuvant radiotherapy course, either a long course around 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions with chemotherapy or a short course of 25 Gy in 5 fractions. Anal canal squamous cell carcinoma is cured with chemoradiotherapy specifically to avoid an abdominoperineal resection. Inguinal nodes are part of the anal volume when the tumor involves the canal below the dentate line or the verge, or when nodes are clinically positive. That inguinal inclusion is not automatic for a mid-rectal adenocarcinoma.

## Oncology Quiz

### Q1
Stem: A patient with multiple brain metastases and poor performance status will receive whole-brain radiotherapy for palliation. Which prescription is the standard whole-brain course rather than radiosurgery?
A. 24 Gy in 1 fraction
B. 30 Gy in 10 fractions
C. 60 Gy in 30 fractions to a 2 cm glioblastoma margin only
D. 20 Gy in 10 fractions to the para-aortic strip
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. Whole-brain palliation is commonly 30 Gy in 10 fractions of 3 Gy, or 20 Gy in 5. A single 24 Gy fraction is a radiosurgery dose for a small metastasis 2 cm or smaller, not a whole-brain dose. Sixty gray in 30 fractions is radical treatment for glioblastoma, limited to the involved volume. Para-aortic 20 Gy in 10 is seminoma. Hippocampal avoidance is a technique option for selected whole-brain cases; it is not used when a metastasis already sits in the hippocampus.

### Q2
Stem: Using the RTOG 90-05 single-fraction limits (24 Gy if the lesion is 2.0 cm or smaller, 18 Gy if it is larger than 2.0 cm and up to 3.0 cm, 15 Gy if it is larger than 3.0 cm and up to 4.0 cm), what is the maximum single-fraction dose for a 3.4 cm brain metastasis?
A. 24 Gy
B. 18 Gy
C. 15 Gy
D. 30 Gy
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer C. The diameter steps are inclusive at the top of each bin as stated: 2.0 cm and under receives 24 Gy, above 2.0 through 3.0 cm receives 18 Gy, and above 3.0 through 4.0 cm receives 15 Gy. A 3.4 cm lesion is past 3.0 cm and not past 4.0 cm, so the protocol maximum is 15 Gy. Giving it the small-lesion dose of 24 Gy was the toxicity RTOG 90-05 was written to avoid, because the volume of normal brain inside the high-dose shell grows quickly with diameter. Thirty gray is not a single-fraction radiosurgery dose in that protocol.

### Q3
Stem: Stage I seminoma after inguinal orchiectomy will be treated with radiotherapy rather than surveillance. The scrotum was not disturbed. Which field and dose match that decision?
A. Para-aortic nodes, about 20 Gy in 10 fractions, with no elective inguinal field
B. A full mantle field to 40 Gy
C. Whole pelvis to 78 Gy in 39 fractions
D. Inguinal nodes to 70 Gy in 35 fractions
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer A. When radiotherapy is chosen for stage I seminoma, the standard volume is the para-aortic nodes and the standard dose is about 20 Gy in 10 fractions of 2 Gy. A dogleg that also covers the ipsilateral pelvis is a historical or node-positive variant, not the automatic stage I field. The groin is omitted unless the scrotal wall was violated. A mantle field is historical Hodgkin treatment. Seventy-eight gray is a conventional prostate dose. Seventy gray is a gross head-and-neck dose.

### Q4
Stem: A prostate plan is prescribed as 60 Gy in 20 fractions. A second plan is 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions. A third is 78 Gy in 39 fractions. Which description of the three is correct?
A. All three are conventional 2 Gy fractionation
B. 78 Gy in 39 is conventional; 60 Gy in 20 is moderate hypofractionation; 36.25 Gy in 5 is stereotactic fractionation
C. 60 Gy in 20 is stereotactic because 3 Gy exceeds 2 Gy; 36.25 Gy in 5 is palliative
D. 36.25 Gy in 5 is the conventional arm and 78 Gy in 39 is ablative
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. Seventy-eight gray in 39 fractions is 2 Gy per fraction, the long conventional prostate course. Sixty gray in 20 fractions is 3 Gy per fraction, the moderate hypofractionation used in trials such as CHHiP, delivered with intensity modulation and ordinary image guidance rather than a stereotactic platform. Thirty-six and a quarter gray in five fractions is about 7.25 Gy per fraction, which is a stereotactic body regimen. Three gray per fraction is not, by itself, stereotactic. The five-fraction course is not the conventional arm.

### Q5
Stem: A nasopharyngeal carcinoma is node-negative on examination. Which elective nodal statement is correct?
A. No neck and no retropharyngeal nodes are treated, by analogy with T1 glottic cancer
B. Bilateral neck and the lateral retropharyngeal nodes are at risk and are included even when the neck is clinically negative
C. Only the ipsilateral inguinal nodes are treated
D. Only the ipsilateral axilla is treated
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. The nasopharynx has a rich lymphatic network. Microscopic spread to both sides of the neck and to the lateral retropharyngeal nodes (nodes of Rouvière, beside the carotid at about C1) is common enough that those volumes are treated electively. Copying the T1 glottis pattern, which omits the elective neck because the true cord has almost no lymphatics, would miss the first echelon of nasopharyngeal spread. Inguinal and axillary nodes are not that echelon.

### Q6
Stem: A vulvar squamous cell carcinoma and a cervical squamous cell carcinoma are both being planned. Which nodal difference is the important one?
A. Both drain only to the para-aortic nodes and never to the pelvis
B. Vulva drains to the inguinal nodes; cervix drains to the pelvic nodes and, when advanced, to the para-aortic nodes, not to the groin unless the lower vagina is involved
C. Cervix drains to the inguinal nodes and vulva does not
D. Neither primary has lymphatic drainage
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. The vulva drains primarily to the inguinal-femoral nodes, so the groin is in the field. The cervix drains to the obturator, internal iliac, external iliac, and presacral nodes, and onward to the para-aortic nodes. Inguinal irradiation is not part of a standard cervical field unless tumor extends down the vagina onto the introitus or the lower third, which changes the drainage. Swapping these volumes either misses the groin on a vulvar cancer or treats the groin unnecessarily on a cervical cancer.

### Q7
Stem: A conventional lung plan reports a mean lung dose of 22 Gy and a V20 of 38% for the combined lungs minus the GTV. QUANTEC-type goals often used in planning are a mean lung dose under about 20 Gy and a V20 under about 30 to 35%. Which reading is correct?
A. The plan meets both goals
B. The plan exceeds both the mean-dose goal and the V20 goal, so PTV coverage alone does not make it acceptable
C. V20 of 38% means 38 cm³ receives 20 Gy, which is always safe regardless of lung volume
D. Mean lung dose is a serial-organ constraint and is ignored for pneumonitis
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Answer B. Symptomatic pneumonitis risk rises with both the mean lung dose and the volume receiving 20 Gy or more. A mean of 22 Gy is above a 20 Gy goal, and a V20 of 38% is above a 30 to 35% goal. The plan needs a different beam arrangement, a smaller elective volume, or a physician's explicit acceptance of the higher risk. It is not "done" because the PTV is covered. V20 is a percent of the lung volume unless the DVH is quoted in cubic centimeters; 38% of a 3000 cm³ lung is over a liter, not 38 cm³. Lung behaves as a parallel organ for this endpoint, which is why the mean and V20 are the metrics rather than a tiny maximum.

### Q8
Stem: One parotid mean dose is 24 Gy and the other is 18 Gy. QUANTEC's salivary guidance is that severe xerostomia is usually avoided if at least one parotid is kept under a mean of about 20 Gy, or if both are kept under about 25 Gy. An older clinical threshold often quoted for a single gland is about 26 Gy. Which statement matches the QUANTEC "at least one gland" goal?
A. The goal is missed, because the 24 Gy gland exceeds 20 Gy
B. The goal is met by the gland at 18 Gy, even though the other gland is 24 Gy
C. The goal is met only if both glands are under 18 Gy
D. Parotid mean dose is not a xerostomia constraint
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Answer B. QUANTEC allows two ways to spare saliva. One is to hold both glands under about 25 Gy. The other, which this question asks about, is to hold at least one gland under about 20 Gy. The gland at 18 Gy satisfies that second statement. The gland at 24 Gy would be a problem if it were the only one being spared, and it sits near the older 26 Gy single-gland threshold, but the existence of the 18 Gy contralateral gland is the QUANTEC condition this plan meets. That reading is only valid if the 18 Gy gland is not the one destroyed by tumor and if its contour is real.

### Q9
Stem: An extremity sarcoma compartment is covered, and the 50 Gy isodose wraps entirely around the limb with no longitudinal strip of unirradiated soft tissue. Why is that coverage pattern a problem?
A. It is required, because any untreated strip is an automatic marginal miss
B. A circumferential high-dose shell scars the lymphatics and can cause severe distal edema; an unirradiated strip is preserved when it is not part of the target
C. Sarcoma is treated with total skin electrons specifically to create a circumferential shell
D. The strip is only relevant for testicular seminoma
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer B. The limb needs a corridor of unirradiated tissue so lymph can still drain and so fibrosis does not form a constricting ring. The strip is a normal-tissue constraint, not a target. It is omitted only where the tumor actually involves the whole circumference, which is a different and more morbid situation. Preoperative dose is often about 50 Gy in 25 fractions; postoperative dose is higher, about 60 to 66 Gy. Neither regimen is an invitation to circle the limb. Total skin electron therapy and seminoma fields do not set this rule.

### Q10
Stem: Craniospinal irradiation is being planned for medulloblastoma. Which two geographic misses are the classic ones the field borders are built to prevent?
A. Missing the cribriform plate in the brain field, and stopping the spinal field short of the inferior thecal sac (about S2)
B. Missing the inguinal nodes and the external auditory canals
C. Missing the femoral heads and the bladder dome
D. Missing the true vocal cord and the hyoid
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Answer A. The cribriform plate, just behind the nasal bridge, is a common site of failure if the lateral brain fields are blocked too tightly behind the eyes. The spinal field has to cover the thecal sac, which typically ends near S2, not at the conus of the cord near L1. Junctions between the brain and spine fields are feathered so the match line does not sit on the cord for the whole course. Inguinal nodes, femoral heads, and the glottis are not the medulloblastoma coverage problem.

# Module 43. Memory Lane

Original items. Round 2 of the RM Review porting wave: gonadal thresholds, rib and chest-wall limits, the two brainstem radiosurgery figures, lung V20 across fractionation schemes, the lens threshold against the legacy tolerance, and the five-fraction cord gap between QUANTEC and TG-101.

## Tissue Thresholds and Stereotactic Rows: Gonads, Ribs, Brainstem, Lung, Lens, Cord

### Q1
Stem: A young man's testes receive a scattered dose of 0.3 Gy during pelvic radiotherapy. What effect on fertility does the radiobiology teaching table predict?
A. No effect, because the threshold for any change in sperm count is about 2 Gy
B. Temporary oligospermia or azoospermia with recovery over months to a few years, because the temporary-sterility threshold is about 0.15 Gy
C. Permanent sterility, because the permanent threshold is about 0.1 Gy
D. Permanent sterility, because testes are more sensitive than ovaries at every dose
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Spermatogonia are among the most radiosensitive cells in the body; a single dose of about 0.15 Gy depresses the count and doses up to about 1 Gy give temporary azoospermia with recovery that takes longer the higher the dose, so 0.3 Gy predicts a temporary effect. Permanent sterility needs about 3.5 to 6 Gy, not 0.1 Gy. There is no 2 Gy floor; 2 Gy is already in the range of prolonged azoospermia. The testes-versus-ovaries comparison does not fix the outcome at a given dose, and sperm production recovers from doses the ovary would not.

### Q2
Stem: What dose range to the testes is taught as the threshold for permanent sterility?
A. About 0.15 to 1 Gy
B. About 15 to 20 Gy
C. About 50 Gy
D. About 3.5 to 6 Gy
Answer: D
Objective: III.8
Why: Permanent azoospermia follows single or fractionated doses of about 3.5 to 6 Gy, with fractionation lowering rather than raising the effective threshold because stem-cell spermatogonia are killed cumulatively. The 0.15 to 1 Gy range is temporary sterility. Fifteen to 20 Gy is the Leydig-cell (hormone) threshold, which is far higher than the germ-cell threshold; a man can be sterile and hormonally intact. Fifty Gy is a therapeutic tumour dose, not a threshold.

### Q3
Stem: Which statement about radiation-induced permanent ovarian failure is correct?
A. The threshold is a fixed 2 Gy at every age
B. The threshold is age dependent, taught as roughly 5 to 10 Gy in younger women and falling to a few gray as menopause approaches, because the oocyte pool shrinks with age
C. The ovary is more resistant than the testis and needs more than 20 Gy
D. Fractionation raises the threshold indefinitely because oocytes repair sublethal damage
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Oocytes are a fixed, non-renewing pool, so the dose that empties it depends on how many remain: a woman in her twenties may keep function after 5 to 10 Gy fractionated, while a woman in her forties can be sterilised by 2 to 3 Gy, and a single dose of a few gray can do it at any adult age. A single number for all ages ignores this. The ovary is not more resistant than the testis for permanent failure; 20 Gy sterilises essentially everyone. Oocytes do not repopulate, so fractionation cannot raise the threshold without limit, though it spares a little.

### Q4
Stem: A lung SBRT plan reports chest-wall V30 of 42 cc and a maximum rib dose of 44 Gy in three fractions. Against the RTOG 0813 and 0915 chest-wall row, what is the result?
A. Fails, because V30 of 42 cc is 12 cc over the 30 cc limit
B. Passes, because the rib maximum is under 50 Gy
C. Passes, because chest wall is not a scored structure in SBRT
D. Fails, because any chest-wall dose above 30 Gy is prohibited
Answer: A
Objective: III.8
Why: The RTOG lung SBRT protocols write chest wall V30 below 30 cc, and 42 − 30 = 12 cc over, so the plan fails that row. The 50 Gy figure is the conventional-fractionation rib fracture threshold, above which fracture risk climbs; it is not the SBRT metric, and 44 Gy in three fractions is a far hotter dose biologically than 44 Gy in 22 fractions. Chest wall is scored in both protocols. The row limits the volume above 30 Gy, it does not forbid 30 Gy.

### Q5
Stem: In a conventionally fractionated chest-wall or lung course, above roughly what dose does the risk of rib fracture rise appreciably?
A. About 20 Gy
B. About 50 Gy
C. About 30 Gy
D. About 70 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: Rib fracture after conventional fractionation is uncommon below about 50 Gy and becomes a recognised late effect above it, particularly where tangents or electron boosts put full dose on the rib; the breast and chest-wall literature places the risk at a few percent in the 50 to 60 Gy region. Twenty and 30 Gy are below any fracture threshold at 2 Gy per fraction; 30 Gy is the dose level of the SBRT chest-wall volume row, a different fractionation. Seventy Gy is well past the threshold, not at it.

### Q6
Stem: A single-fraction radiosurgery plan reports a brainstem point maximum of 13.5 Gy. The stem offers both the TG-101 point maximum and the QUANTEC brainstem radiosurgery figure. How does the plan read?
A. Under both figures
B. Over both figures
C. Under the QUANTEC figure, over the TG-101 point maximum
D. Over the QUANTEC 12.5 Gy figure, under the TG-101 15 Gy point maximum
Answer: D
Objective: III.8
Why: TG-101 Table III writes a one-fraction brainstem (not medulla) point maximum of 15 Gy with 10 Gy to 0.5 cubic centimetres; the QUANTEC brainstem paper puts the radiosurgery maximum at 12.5 Gy for under about 5 percent risk, and many programs use 12.5 Gy as the conservative teaching number. 13.5 Gy is 1.0 Gy over 12.5 and 1.5 Gy under 15, so the verdict depends on which figure the stem names. This bank keys TG-101 at 15 Gy when the stem says TG-101 and 12.5 Gy when it says QUANTEC or "conservative"; neither pass-both nor fail-both fits a value between them, and option C attaches each verdict to the wrong document.

### Q7
Stem: A five-fraction peripheral lung SBRT plan reports total lung V20 of 12 percent. Which statement about the lung V20 row is correct?
A. Passes, because the QUANTEC conventional row is 30 percent
B. Passes, because RTOG 0617 allows up to 37 percent
C. Fails, because the SBRT protocols write V20 below 10 percent, and 12 is 2 points over
D. Cannot be judged, because V20 is not used in SBRT
Answer: C
Objective: III.8
Why: RTOG 0813 and 0915 write total-lung V20 below 10 percent for SBRT, so 12 percent is 2 percentage points over and fails. The QUANTEC 30 percent row and the RTOG 0617 37 percent allowance are conventional-fractionation figures (60 to 74 Gy in 2 Gy fractions) and cannot be applied to a five-fraction plan; 20 Gy in five fractions is a much larger biological dose than 20 Gy in ten. V20 is used in SBRT alongside the TG-101 critical-volume cells (1,500 cc under 12.5 Gy in five fractions). Check: a 12 percent V20 would pass comfortably on a conventional plan.

### Q8
Stem: Which pair gives the current cataract threshold and the older figure that legacy questions still key for fractionated exposure of the lens?
A. 0.5 Gy (ICRP 2011) and 5 Gy (the older fractionated tolerance)
B. 2 Gy (ICRP 2011) and 10 Gy (older fractionated)
C. 5 Gy (ICRP 2011) and 0.5 Gy (older)
D. 7 Gy (ICRP 2011) and 45 Gy (older)
Answer: A
Objective: III.8
Why: ICRP's 2011 statement set the cataract threshold at about 0.5 Gy whether acute or protracted; the legacy teaching pair was 2 Gy acute and 5 Gy fractionated, and 5 Gy is the figure older items key as the lens "tolerance." Option B borrows the acute legacy number and the Emami TD5/5 of 10 Gy. Option C reverses old and new. Seven Gy is the planning constraint this bank applies when a stem is silent, and 45 Gy is the retina row; neither is a cataract threshold. A planning constraint and a tissue-reaction threshold are different quantities, which is why a 4 Gy lens can pass a plan while sitting above the threshold.

### Q9
Stem: A five-fraction spine SBRT plan reports a cord point maximum of 28 Gy. The stem says "according to QUANTEC." What is the correct response?
A. Passes, because QUANTEC's five-fraction cord line is 30 Gy
B. Fails, because QUANTEC's five-fraction cord line is 25 Gy
C. Passes against the TG-101 30 Gy point maximum; QUANTEC's cord paper gives no five-fraction line, so the stem's attribution is wrong
D. Fails, because 28 Gy exceeds the conventional 50 Gy row after conversion
Answer: C
Objective: III.8
Why: QUANTEC's spinal-cord paper gives stereotactic figures only for one fraction (13 Gy) and three fractions (20 Gy); the five-fraction cord point maximum of 30 Gy, with 23 Gy to 0.35 cubic centimetres, comes from TG-101 Table III. 28 Gy is 2 Gy under that point maximum, so the plan passes on the only document that has the row. There is no QUANTEC 25 or 30 Gy five-fraction line to cite. Converting 28 Gy in five fractions with an alpha-beta of 2 gives about 53 Gy in 2 Gy equivalents (28 × (5.6 + 2) / (2 + 2) = 53.2 Gy), which is over 50, but the conventional row is not the constraint applied to stereotactic plans, and the stem asked for a stereotactic verdict.

### Q10
Stem: Rank the lung V20 figures used in this bank from most permissive to most restrictive.
A. SBRT protocols 10 percent, QUANTEC 30 percent, RTOG 0617 37 percent
B. QUANTEC 30 percent, SBRT protocols 10 percent, RTOG 0617 37 percent
C. RTOG 0617 37 percent, SBRT protocols 10 percent, QUANTEC 30 percent
D. RTOG 0617 37 percent, QUANTEC 30 percent, SBRT protocols 10 percent
Answer: D
Objective: III.8
Why: The conventional dose-escalation protocol RTOG 0617 allowed total-lung V20 up to 37 percent, QUANTEC's summary row is 30 percent for about 20 percent pneumonitis, and the SBRT protocols 0813 and 0915 write below 10 percent because 20 Gy in three to five fractions is a far larger biological dose. The other orderings put the SBRT figure above a conventional one or reverse 0617 and QUANTEC. Remember the fractionation before the number: the same 20 Gy label means different biology on each row.

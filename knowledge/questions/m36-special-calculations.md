## Gap Calculation Tutorial

### Q1
Stem: Tutorial. Equal spine fields, both SSD 100 cm, match at 10 cm depth. Field 1 length at SSD is 20 cm with SSD 100 cm. Field 2 length at SSD is 20 cm with SSD 100 cm. The fields should abut at 10 cm depth. Skin gap = (L1/2)×(d/SSD1) + (L2/2)×(d/SSD2). What is the skin gap?
A. 4.00 cm
B. 2.00 cm
C. 1.00 cm
D. 200.00 cm
Answer: B
Objective: IV.3
Why: Each term is how far that field's edge diverges from its central axis over depth d. Field 1: (20/2) × (10/100) = 10 × 0.1000 = 1.0000 cm. Field 2: (20/2) × (10/100) = 10 × 0.1000 = 1.0000 cm. Gap = 1.0000 + 1.0000 = 2.0000 cm, reported as 2.00 cm. A gap that is too small overlaps the beams at depth (hot). A gap that is too large leaves a cold strip. Dropping the 1/2 and using the full field length gives 4.00 cm, twice the divergence of each edge. Keeping only the larger of the two contributions gives 1.00 cm and leaves the other field's divergence unmatched. Inverting d/SSD into SSD/d gives 200.00 cm, which has the wrong units behavior and is far too large to be a skin gap.

## Couch/Collimator Divergence Tutorial

### Q1
Stem: Tutorial. Craniospinal lateral. SAD is 100 cm and the jaw opening that diverges at the inferior match border of a lateral brain field is 20 cm, symmetric about the central axis. The rotation that follows that divergence is θ = arctan((jaw opening / 2) / SAD). What is θ to the nearest 0.1°?
A. 11.3°
B. 84.3°
C. 0.10°
D. 5.7°
Answer: D
Objective: IV.3
Why: Half the opening is 20/2 = 10 cm. The opposite-over-adjacent ratio is 10/100 = 0.1000. θ = arctan(0.1000) = 5.71°, which rounds to 5.7°. A half-beam jaw (opening closed to the central axis on the match side) has zero divergence at that border, so the matching angle for that border is 0°. Using the full jaw opening in the arctan, arctan(20/100), gives 11.3°. Flipping the triangle to arctan(SAD / half-opening) = arctan(100/10) gives 84.3°. Reporting the ratio 0.1000 as though it were already in degrees gives 0.10°.

### Q4
Stem: Tutorial. How far the edge walks past isocenter. At isocenter the field edge is 7 cm from the central axis. SAD is 100 cm. How much farther from the central axis is that same edge in a plane 10 cm beyond isocenter? Extra divergence = (half-width at isocenter) × (distance past isocenter) / SAD.
A. 1.40 cm
B. 70.00 cm
C. 7.70 cm
D. 0.70 cm
Answer: D
Objective: IV.3
Why: Similar triangles: half-width grows in proportion to distance from the source. Extra = 7 × 10 / 100 = 70 / 100 = 0.7000 cm, reported as 0.70 cm. The half-width in that deeper plane would be 7 + 0.7000 = 7.7000 cm, which is a different quantity. Using the full field width instead of the half-width gives 1.40 cm. Inverting depth and SAD gives 70.00 cm. Reporting the full half-width at the deeper plane, not the extra divergence alone, gives 7.70 cm.

### Q8
Stem: Tutorial. Orthogonal pair, both fields half-beamed on the match. A single-isocenter breast plan puts the superior jaw of the tangents on the central axis (half-beam) to match a supraclavicular field that is also half-beamed. What collimator or couch angle is required to remove superior-border divergence at that match?
A. 0°
B. 5.7°
C. 10°
D. 45°
Answer: A
Objective: IV.3
Why: With the jaw on the central axis, the match border does not diverge: the edge lies on the central axis, so θ = arctan(0/SAD) = 0°. ## Transmission, HVL, Linear Attenuation Coefficient

### Q1
Stem: What does one HVL mean in a narrow beam?
A. Transmission of 25%
B. Transmission of 50%
C. Transmission of 10%
D. The beam is fully stopped
Answer: B
Objective: IV.3
Why: One half-value layer transmits half and attenuates half. Two HVL transmit 25%. A tenth-value layer transmits 10%. Correct choice: Transmission of 50%. 25% is two HVL, because (1/2)^2 = 1/4. 10% is about one TVL, roughly 3.3 HVL. An HVL is a half, not a stop. Intensity never hits zero in this exponential model.

### Q2
Stem: Teaching item. Say the transmitted percent, not the attenuated percent. A shield is exactly two HVL thick in a narrow beam. What percent of the incident intensity is transmitted?
A. 50%
B. 10%
C. 75%
D. 25%
Answer: D
Objective: IV.3
Why: Transmission after n HVL is (1/2)^n. For n = 2, (1/2)^2 = 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%. Substitute 100% × 1/4 = 25%. Correct choice: 25%. 50% is one HVL, not two. The second HVL halves the beam again. 10% is about one TVL (roughly 3.3 HVL), not two HVL. 75% is the attenuated fraction for two HVL (100%−25%), not the transmitted fraction.

### Q3
Stem: How is the linear attenuation coefficient related to the HVL in the narrow-beam model used here?
A. μ = 0.693 / HVL
B. μ = HVL / 0.693
C. μ = 2.303 / HVL
D. μ = HVL
Answer: A
Objective: IV.3
Why: Because (1/2) = e^(−μ × HVL), take ln of both sides: ln(2) = μ × HVL. ln(2) ≈ 0.693, so μ = 0.693/HVL. Correct choice: μ = 0.693 / HVL. That inverts the relationship. A larger HVL means a smaller μ. 2.303 is ln(10), which belongs with the TVL: μ = 2.303/TVL if 2.303 is used for ln(10). μ and HVL have inverse roles and different units.

### Q4
Stem: About how many HVL are in one TVL?
A. 2
B. 10
C. 3.32
D. 1
Answer: C
Objective: IV.3
Why: 10^(−1) = (1/2)^n, so n = ln(10)/ln(2) ≈ 2.3026/0.6931 ≈ 3.32. One TVL is about 3.32 HVL. Correct choice: 3.32. Two HVL transmit 25%, not 10%. Ten is the factor a TVL applies to intensity (divide by 10), not the HVL count. One HVL transmits 50%.

### Q5
Stem: Why can a second measured HVL be thicker than the first for a real broad polyenergetic beam?
A. Inverse square doubles every HVL
B. HVL is defined only for electrons
C. The beam hardens and scatter changes the reading
D. Two HVL must transmit 10%
Answer: C
Objective: IV.3
Why: A polyenergetic beam loses soft photons first, so the remaining beam is harder and the next HVL can be thicker. Broad-beam scatter also adds dose to the detector that a narrow-beam μ does not include. Correct choice: The beam hardens and scatter changes the reading. Inverse square depends on distance, not on how many layers you stack at one location. Electron range is a different rule of thumb. HVL is a photon attenuation concept. Two HVL transmit 25% in the narrow monoenergetic model.

## Geometric Penumbra

### Q1
Stem: Which statement is the physical penumbra, not the geometric penumbra?
A. P = s × (SSD + d − SDD) / SDD
B. The wedge angle
C. The 80% to 20% width at dmax
D. One HVL in the jaw
Answer: C
Objective: IV.3
Why: Physical penumbra is measured on a dose profile, classically the distance between the 80% and 20% points at dmax. Geometric penumbra is the similar-triangles source formula and ignores scatter and transmission through the jaw edge. Correct choice: The 80% to 20% width at dmax. That is the geometric penumbra from a finite source and a diaphragm. Wedge angle is an isodose tilt, not a penumbra width. HVL is transmission through a shield, not the edge width.

### Q2
Stem: Teaching. Cobalt-style source and diaphragm. Source diameter s = 2 cm, SSD = 80 cm, depth d = 10 cm, source-to-diaphragm distance SDD = 50 cm. Geometric penumbra P = s × (SSD + d − SDD) / SDD. What is P?
A. 1.20 cm
B. 3.60 cm
C. 1.60 cm
D. 0.80 cm
Answer: C
Objective: IV.3
Why: The source is not a point, so the diaphragm casts a partial shadow that grows as you move away from the diaphragm. Substitute 2 × (80 + 10 − 50) / 50 = 2 × (40) / 50 = 2 × 0.8000 = 1.6000 cm, reported as 1.60 cm. This geometric width is not the physical penumbra. Physical penumbra is the 80% to 20% dose distance, usually quoted at dmax. Dropping the depth and using (SSD-SDD), which is the surface penumbra gives 1.20 cm. Forgetting to subtract SDD inside the parentheses gives 3.60 cm. Forgetting to multiply by the source diameter gives 0.80 cm.

### Q3
Stem: What happens to geometric penumbra if the source diameter increases and nothing else changes?
A. Penumbra decreases
B. Penumbra stays constant because SSD is fixed
C. Penumbra becomes the physical 80–20 width automatically
D. Penumbra increases in proportion to source diameter
Answer: D
Objective: IV.3
Why: P is directly proportional to s. Double the source diameter and, with SSD, depth, and SDD fixed, the geometric penumbra doubles. Correct choice: Penumbra increases in proportion to source diameter. A larger source casts a larger partial shadow. SSD is in the formula, but source size is a direct multiplier. The formula still returns a geometric width. Scatter is extra.

### Q4
Stem: Moving the diaphragm farther from the source (larger SDD), with SSD and source size fixed, does what to geometric penumbra at the surface?
A. Increases it
B. Leaves it unchanged

Answer: D
Objective: IV.3
Why: At the surface, P = s × (SSD − SDD) / SDD = s × (SSD/SDD − 1). Raising SDD toward SSD shrinks (SSD − SDD) and raises the denominator, so P falls. Correct choice: Decreases it. A diaphragm closer to the patient (larger SDD, if SSD is fixed) trims the partial shadow. SDD is in both the numerator and the denominator. HVL is not a penumbra unit.

### Q1
Stem: Tutorial. Find the set time. Two exposures give 105 cGy in 1 min and 205 cGy in 2 min. Timer error is extra effective on-time during transit, so true dose rate = (R2−R1)/(t2−t1) and timer error = R1/dose rate − t1. Set time = dose/dose rate − timer error. What timer setting delivers 200 cGy?
A. 2.00 min
B. 2.05 min
C. 1.85 min
D. 1.95 min
Answer: D
Objective: IV.3
Why: True dose rate = (205−105)/(2−1) = 100/1 = 100 cGy/min. Timer error = 105/100 − 1 = 1.0500 − 1 = 0.0500 min. Set time = 200/100 − 0.0500 = 2.0000 − 0.0500 = 1.9500 min, reported as 1.95 min. Ignoring timer error and setting dose/rate gives 2.00 min, which overdoses by the transit contribution. Adding the timer error instead of subtracting it gives 2.05 min and overdoses further. Using R1/t1 = 105/1 = 105.0000 cGy/min as the dose rate folds the transit dose into the rate and then still subtracting tau gives 1.85 min.

## Trigonometry

### Q1
Stem: A beam enters at 60° from the normal. Vertical tissue thickness is 5 cm. A ray crosses a vertical thickness of 5 cm at 60° from the normal (0° would be straight down). Path length along the ray = vertical / cos(angle). What is the path length?
A. 5.00 cm
B. 10.00 cm
C. 2.50 cm
D. 5.77 cm
Answer: B
Objective: IV.3
Why: cos(60°) = 0.5000. Path = 5 / 0.5000 = 10.0000 cm, reported as 10.00 cm. Obliquity lengthens the path, so the value must be larger than the vertical thickness. Skin dose also rises with obliquity because buildup is effectively lost along a slanted entrance. Leaving the path equal to the vertical thickness gives 5.00 cm and ignores obliquity. Multiplying by cos instead of dividing shortens the path and gives 2.50 cm. Dividing by sin instead of cos gives 5.77 cm.

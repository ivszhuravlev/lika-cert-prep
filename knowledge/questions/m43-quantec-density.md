# Module 43. Memory Lane

Original CMD practice items. Not retired MDCB questions and not a commercial quiz.

## QUANTEC

### Q1
Stem: Which cord dose is the conventional-fractionation correlate used in this bank?
A. 30 Gy, borrowed from the lung V20 percent
B. 50 Gy
C. A rectal V70 of 20%
D. A parotid mean of 25 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: The cord correlate used here is 50 Gy. It is a dose, not a percent. Lung uses V20 ≤ 30%. Rectum uses the five-volume set, including V70 < 20%. Parotid uses a mean under 25 Gy for both glands, or one gland under 20 Gy. Those are different organs.

### Q2
Stem: For combined lung, which statement matches the correlate used here?
A. V20 ≤ 20%
B. V30 ≤ 30% replaces V20
C. The cord correlate of 50 Gy is the lung limit
D. V20 ≤ 30%
Answer: D
Objective: III.8
Why: The lung correlate used here is combined-lung V20 ≤ 30%. A plan with V20 = 28% is 2 percentage points under 30. A plan with V20 = 36% is 36 − 30 = 6 percentage points over. The row is V20, not V30, and it is not the cord dose of 50 Gy.

### Q3
Stem: Which parotid means are the correlates used here?
A. Both glands under a combined mean of 50 Gy, the cord number
B. One gland under 26 Gy only
C. Both glands under a combined mean of 25 Gy, or at least one gland under 20 Gy
D. V20 ≤ 30% of the parotid
Answer: C
Objective: III.8
Why: Parotid is judged by mean dose: a combined bilateral mean under 25 Gy, or at least one gland under 20 Gy. Equal volumes of 18 Gy and 30 Gy average to (18 + 30) / 2 = 24 Gy, which is under 25, and 18 Gy is under 20. V20 ≤ 30% is the lung row, not a parotid percent.

### Q4
Stem: QUANTEC's prostate rectum constraints are which kind of limit?
A. Any one of those five is enough
B. One set: V50<50%, V60<35%, V65<25%, V70<20%, V75<15%, judged together
C. V70<50% alone
D. Mean rectum <18 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: The rectum correlate is one set: V50 < 50%, V60 < 35%, V65 < 25%, V70 < 20%, and V75 < 15%, read together. If V70 is 24%, the excess is 24 − 20 = 4 percentage points, and the set is missed even when the other four pass. V70 < 20% is not V70 < 50%. A kidney mean under 15–18 Gy is not a rectal limit.

### Q5
Stem: Which pairing matches the QUANTEC summary for liver, kidney, and small bowel?
A. Liver mean under 18 Gy, kidney V20 ≤ 30%, bowel V50<50%
B. Liver mean under 50 Gy, kidney mean under 50 Gy, bowel unlimited
C. Use the peritoneal 195 cc number on individually contoured loops
D. Liver mean under about 30–32 Gy without liver disease, kidney mean under about 15–18 Gy, bowel either V15<120 cc (loops) or V45<195 cc (peritoneal space)
Answer: D
Objective: III.8
Why: Liver mean dose stays under 30–32 Gy. Bilateral kidney mean dose stays under 15–18 Gy. Bowel is either V15 < 120 cc for individual loops or V45 < 195 cc for the peritoneal space. A liver mean of 34 Gy is 34 − 32 = 2 Gy over the top of the liver band. A kidney mean of 18 Gy is the top of the kidney band, not the cord dose of 50 Gy, and not lung V20 ≤ 30%. Loops do not borrow the 195 cc peritoneal number.

## QUANTEC Mini-Quiz

### Q1
Stem: Conventional fractionation, full cord cross-section, plan Dmax 54 Gy. The correlate used here is the QUANTEC 50 Gy row. By how many Gy does the stated value exceed the published correlate of 50 Gy?
A. 0 Gy
B. 4 Gy
C. 54 Gy
D. 50 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: The cord correlate is 50 Gy. Excess = 54 − 50 = 4 Gy. Scoring the plan as meeting 50 Gy would be 0 Gy over. Quoting 54 Gy reports the DVH value, not the excess. Quoting 50 Gy reports the correlate, not how far the plan sits above it.

### Q2
Stem: Combined lungs, conventional fractionation, V20 = 36%. The correlate is V20 ≤ 30%. By how many percentage points does the stated value exceed the published correlate of 30 percentage points?
A. 0 percentage points
B. 6 percentage points
C. 36 percentage points
D. 30 percentage points
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: The lung correlate is V20 ≤ 30%. Excess = 36 − 30 = 6 percentage points. Calling it met would be 0 points over. Quoting 36 reports the DVH percent, not the excess. Quoting 30 reports the correlate. Do not relabel the row as V30.

### Q3
Stem: Equal-volume parotids have means of 18 Gy and 30 Gy. What is the combined mean, and which QUANTEC sparing statement is true?
A. 48 Gy, the sum, so both correlates fail
B. 30 Gy, so the single-gland spare fails and that is the combined mean
C. 18 Gy, so the hotter gland can be ignored
D. 24 Gy, and both the combined mean under 25 Gy and the single gland under 20 Gy are met
Answer: D
Objective: III.8
Why: Equal volumes: combined mean = (18 + 30) / 2 = 24 Gy. That is under 25 Gy, and the cooler gland, 18 Gy, is under 20 Gy, so both parotid statements are met. The sum 18 + 30 = 48 Gy is not the mean. 30 Gy is only the hotter gland. 18 Gy is only the cooler gland.

### Q4
Stem: Rectum, conventional prostate fractionation: V50=48%, V60=33%, V65=22%, V70=24%, V75=11%. Which conclusion matches the QUANTEC set?
A. The set is not met, because V70 is over 20%
B. The set is met because four of the five passed
C. V50 fails because 48% is under 50%
D. V75 fails because 11% is under 15%
Answer: A
Objective: III.8
Why: The set is V50 < 50, V60 < 35, V65 < 25, V70 < 20, V75 < 15. Here V70 is 24%, and 24 − 20 = 4 percentage points over. The other four pass: 48 < 50, 33 < 35, 22 < 25, and 11 < 15. Four passes do not erase the V70 miss. 11% under 15% means V75 passes.

### Q5
Stem: No prior liver disease and no hepatocellular carcinoma. Mean liver-minus-GTV dose is 34 Gy at conventional fractionation. Use the top of the QUANTEC <5% band, 32 Gy. By how many Gy does the stated value exceed the published correlate of 32 Gy?
A. 2 Gy
B. 0 Gy
C. 34 Gy
D. 32 Gy
Answer: A
Objective: III.8
Why: The liver correlate is a mean under 30–32 Gy. Using the top of that band, excess = 34 − 32 = 2 Gy. Calling the plan met would be 0 Gy over. Quoting 34 Gy reports the DVH mean, not the excess. Quoting 32 Gy reports the top of the band, not the excess.

### Q6
Stem: Both kidneys, conventional fractionation, mean dose 22 Gy. Use the upper end of the QUANTEC <5% band, 18 Gy. By how many Gy does the stated value exceed the published correlate of 18 Gy?
A. 0 Gy
B. 22 Gy
C. 4 Gy
D. 18 Gy
Answer: C
Objective: III.8
Why: The kidney correlate is a bilateral mean under 15–18 Gy. Using 18 Gy as the top of that band, excess = 22 − 18 = 4 Gy. Calling it met would be 0 Gy over. Quoting 22 Gy reports the DVH mean. Quoting 18 Gy reports the top of the band. Lung V20 ≤ 30% is not this kidney mean.

### Q7
Stem: Small bowel was contoured as individual loops. V15 = 150 cc. Chemotherapy is planned. Use the loop correlate. By how many cc does the stated value exceed the published correlate of 120 cc?
A. 0 cc
B. 150 cc
C. 30 cc
D. 120 cc
Answer: C
Objective: III.8
Why: Individual loops use V15 < 120 cc. Excess = 150 − 120 = 30 cc. The peritoneal number is V45 < 195 cc and does not license a loop V15 of 150 cc. Calling the loop constraint met would be 0 cc over. Quoting 150 cc reports the DVH volume. Quoting 120 cc reports the correlate.

### Q8
Stem: The peritoneal space has V45 = 210 cc. Individual loops were not contoured. By how many cc does this exceed the peritoneal correlate?
A. 0 cc
B. 15 cc
C. 90 cc
D. 210 cc
Answer: B
Objective: III.8
Why: The peritoneal correlate is V45 < 195 cc. Excess = 210 − 195 = 15 cc. The loop correlate is V15 < 120 cc, and 210 − 120 = 90 cc would be the wrong subtraction because these are not loop contours. Calling V45 met would be 0 cc over. Quoting 210 cc reports the DVH volume, not the excess.

## Physical Density of Materials

### Q1
Stem: What is the physical density of water used in treatment planning?
A. 1.00 g/cm³
B. 0.25 g/cm³
C. 1.85 g/cm³
D. 11.3 g/cm³
Answer: A
Objective: I.9
Why: Water is the reference: physical density 1.00 g/cm³ and relative electron density 1.00. CT number of water is 0 HU. Air is near −1000 HU and near 0.001 g/cm³. Correct choice: 1.00 g/cm³. 0.25 is a typical teaching density for lung, not water. About 1.85 g/cm³ is cortical bone's physical density, not water. About 11.3 g/cm³ is lead.

### Q2
Stem: A ray of 3 cm soft tissue plus 5 cm lung. Use the stated lung density 0.30. Path: 3 cm at relative density 1, 5 cm at relative density 0.3. Water-equivalent depth = sum of (thickness × relative electron density). What is the water-equivalent depth? This equivalent-path estimate ignores the change in lateral scatter.
A. 8.00 cm
B. 4.50 cm
C. 19.67 cm
D. 1.50 cm
Answer: B
Objective: I.9
Why: Substitute 3×1 + 5×0.3 = 3.00 + 1.50 = 4.50 cm, reported as 4.50 cm. The physical depth is 8.00 cm, which would be the answer only if every segment had density 1. Beyond lung, a photon plan that ignores the low density underestimates dose, because the real attenuation is less than the water calculation. The equivalent-path method does not restore lateral electron equilibrium, so small fields in lung still need a better algorithm than this hand factor. Adding physical thicknesses only gives 8.00 cm. Dividing thickness by density instead of multiplying gives 19.67 cm. Keeping only the inhomogeneous segments and dropping unit-density tissue gives 1.50 cm.

### Q3
Stem: This planning CT table, stated for this problem, maps 0 HU to 1.00 and 1000 HU to 1.80. It is not a universal scanner curve. A table lists relative density = 1 at 0 and relative density = 1.8 at 1000. Linearly interpolate relative density at 250. No curve fit, just the straight fraction of the interval.
A. 1.00
B. 1.20
C. 1.40
D. 1.60
Answer: B
Objective: I.9
Why: The point 250 is (250−0)/(1000−0) = 0.2500 of the way from 0 to 1000. The table changes by 0.8 over that whole step. Change on this step = 0.2500 × (0.8) = 0.2000. relative density = 1 + (0.2000) = 1.2000, reported as 1.20. Using the nearer table entry without interpolating gives 1.00. Averaging the endpoints as if the point were midway gives 1.40. Measuring the fraction from the wrong end of the interval gives 1.60.

### Q4
Stem: A metal hip reads several thousand HU and the planning table saturates. Why is a manual density still required?
A. Saturation means the density is exactly 1.00
B. The saturated HU does not equal the true metal density or the true streak density
C. Lead and tissue have the same electron density
D. HU −1000 should be assigned to the metal
Answer: B
Objective: I.9
Why: Once the CT number is clipped, the lookup is no longer a measurement of density. Metal and its streaks need an assigned density, or the beams should avoid the metal. Correct choice: The saturated HU does not equal the true metal density or the true streak density. Saturation means the table ran out of range, not that the object is water. They do not. −1000 is air.

### Q5
Stem: Mass thickness of a 4 cm slab with physical density 2.5 g/cm³ is which value?
A. 1.6 g/cm²
B. 2.5 g/cm²
C. 10 g/cm²
D. 4 g/cm²
Answer: C
Objective: I.9
Why: Mass thickness = physical density × thickness = 2.5 g/cm³ × 4 cm = 10 g/cm². That product, not the HU by itself, is what attenuation cares about in this hand estimate. Correct choice: 10 g/cm². 4 / 2.5 = 1.6 would be a division, not mass thickness. 2.5 is the density alone. 4 cm times 1 g/cm³ would be water. This slab is 2.5 g/cm³.

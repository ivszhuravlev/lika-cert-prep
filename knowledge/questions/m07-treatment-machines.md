# Module 7 — Treatment Machine Characteristics

Original items on linac components, flattening filters, and kV therapy units.

## Parts of a Linac

### Q1
Stem: In photon mode, which order matches the beam path after the electrons leave the bending magnet?
A. Target, primary collimator, flattening filter, monitor chamber, jaws
B. Jaws, target, monitor chamber, flattening filter
C. Flattening filter, target, bending magnet, patient
D. Scattering foil, applicator, target, jaws
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Electrons hit a transmission target, the primary collimator defines the largest field, the flattening filter shapes the forward-peaked bremsstrahlung, the monitor chamber counts the beam, and the jaws (then the MLC) shape the portal. The target cannot come after the jaws. A scattering foil and applicator are the electron-mode path, and the target is removed in that mode.

### Q2
Stem: The monitor chamber’s job is to:
A. Flatten the beam profile
B. Integrate the beam and stop the exposure at the set monitor units
C. Bend the electron pencil beam by 270°
D. Produce bremsstrahlung
Answer: B
Objective: I.5
Why: The sealed ion chamber sits after the filter or foil and terminates the beam when the set MU are reached. Flattening is the filter. Bending is the magnet. Bremsstrahlung is the target.

### Q3
Stem: A magnetron and a klystron both supply the RF that accelerates electrons. The practical distinction is:
A. A magnetron is an RF oscillator. A klystron amplifies RF and is used when more microwave power is required.
B. A klystron produces the photons and a magnetron produces the electrons.
C. Neither device is part of a linac. Both are CT tubes.
D. A magnetron is the MLC motor controller.
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Both are microwave sources for the accelerating waveguide. Magnetrons oscillate and are common on lower-energy machines. Klystrons amplify a drive signal and supply the higher power used on many high-energy linacs. Neither one is the target, and neither one is the MLC.

### Q4
Stem: A 270° bending magnet is used instead of a simple 90° bend primarily because it:
A. Selects and refocuses the electron energy spread, giving a tighter energy spectrum at the target
B. Removes the need for a target in photon mode
C. Replaces the monitor chamber
D. Converts photons into electrons at the patient
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: An achromatic 270° bend brings different electron energies back to a small focal spot, so the x-ray source stays small and the energy is better defined. The target, the chamber, and the photon interactions in the patient are separate systems.

### Q5
Stem: In electron mode, compared with photon mode on the same linac, the machine typically:
A. Retracts the target and the flattening filter and inserts a scattering foil
B. Leaves the flattening filter in and removes the scattering foil
C. Replaces the jaws with a Co-60 source
D. Uses the same target but turns off the monitor chamber
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Electron cones need a broadened electron beam, not bremsstrahlung. The target and flattening filter come out, scattering foils come in, and an applicator shapes the field. The monitor chamber stays in the beam. A cobalt source is a different machine.

## Linear Accelerator

### Q1
Stem: A standing-wave accelerating guide differs from a traveling-wave guide in that the standing-wave design:
A. Is generally shorter for the same energy, with side-coupled cavities
B. Cannot accelerate electrons
C. Is used only on Co-60 units
D. Removes the need for an RF source
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Standing-wave guides use coupling cavities so the accelerating structure can be shorter, which suits a gantry-mounted linac. Both designs accelerate electrons and both need RF. Co-60 has no waveguide.

### Q2
Stem: Approximate depth of maximum dose for a 10 × 10 cm field is about 1.5 cm for 6 MV and about 2.5 cm for 10 MV. The depth increases with nominal MV because:
A. Secondary electrons have a longer range
B. The flattening filter is removed
C. Photoelectric absorption increases with energy
D. The monitor chamber is moved downstream
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Buildup is the range of the electrons set in motion by the photons. Higher photon energy produces longer-range electrons, so dmax is deeper. Photoelectric absorption falls with energy. Filter and chamber position are not the reason dmax moves from 1.5 cm to 2.5 cm.

### Q3
Stem: Co-60 teletherapy, compared with a 6 MV linac, has:
A. A larger source size, more geometric penumbra, and dmax near 0.5 cm
B. No penumbra, because the source is a point
C. dmax near 3 cm, like an 18 MV beam
D. A flattening filter that must be changed monthly as the source decays
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: The Co-60 source is about a centimeter across, so geometric penumbra is larger than a linac target. Average energy is about 1.25 MeV and dmax is about 0.5 cm. Output falls with the 5.26-year half-life, but there is no linac-style flattening filter whose thickness is changed for decay.

### Q4
Stem: Photoneutrons become a shielding and pacemaker concern when the photon energy is high enough for photonuclear reactions. That concern starts to matter:
A. On a superficial 100 kVp unit
B. On a linac operated above about 10 MV
C. Only for electron beams below 6 MeV
D. Only for Ir-192 HDR
Answer: B
Objective: I.5
Why: The threshold for neutron emission from high-Z head components is in the region of 8–10 MV, so 15 MV and 18 MV beams are the usual neutron sources. kV units, low-energy electrons, and Ir-192 do not produce that head-neutron field.

### Q5
Stem: An MR-linac differs from a conventional C-arm linac in that treatment imaging is:
A. Magnetic-resonance imaging during the session, with the beam still produced by an accelerator
B. A Co-60 source viewed by an ultrasound probe only
C. Megavoltage CT with no magnetic field
D. A diagnostic x-ray tube replacing the waveguide
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: An MR-linac combines a magnetic resonance imager with a linac so soft-tissue motion can be seen at treatment. The treatment beam is still megavoltage photons from an accelerator. It is not a cobalt unit, not MVCT alone, and not a kV tube in place of the waveguide.

## Flattening Filters

### Q1
Stem: A flattening filter is thicker on the central axis because:
A. Bremsstrahlung is forward-peaked and the center must be attenuated more than the edge
B. The heel effect of a diagnostic tube must be copied
C. Electrons need a scattering foil on axis only
D. The monitor chamber cannot count an unflat beam
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: The x-ray intensity off a transmission target is highest on the axis. A high-Z cone (tungsten, steel, or a similar metal) pulls the center down so the profile is flat at a stated depth, often 10 cm. That is not the diagnostic heel effect, not an electron foil, and not a limit of the ion chamber.

### Q2
Stem: Removing the flattening filter (FFF), with the same nominal energy, typically:
A. Raises the dose rate, peaks the profile, and lowers the mean energy on the central axis relative to the flattened beam
B. Lowers the dose rate and makes the profile flatter than the flattened beam
C. Hardens the central axis more than the thickest part of the filter did
D. Converts the beam to electrons
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: The filter was absorbing a large fraction of the fluence, especially on axis, and it was hardening the center. Without it, dose rate goes up, the profile stays forward-peaked, head scatter falls, and the central-axis spectrum is softer than the flattened beam. The beam is still photons.

### Q3
Stem: A flattened 6 MV profile measured at dmax often shows “horns,” while the same field at 10 cm is flat. The horns exist because:
A. The filter is designed to flatten the beam at depth, so the shallower depth is over-flattened at the shoulders
B. The target is missing
C. The jaws are outside the primary collimator
D. Neutron dose adds two peaks at dmax
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Scatter and the filter thickness are chosen so the profile is flat near 10 cm. Closer to the surface there is less phantom scatter to fill the center, and the shoulders sit above the center. Those horns are not a missing target and they are not neutrons.

### Q4
Stem: The flattening filter hardens the beam mostly on the central axis. One planning consequence is:
A. The off-axis spectrum is slightly softer than the central axis of a flattened beam
B. The off-axis spectrum is harder than the axis
C. Energy is identical at every point in the field, by definition of “flat”
D. PDD at the field edge is higher than PDD on the axis for that reason alone
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Less filter material off axis removes fewer low-energy photons, so the off-axis beam is a bit softer. “Flat” refers to dose at one depth, not to a constant spectrum. A softer edge does not by itself give a higher percent depth dose than the axis.

### Q5
Stem: Electron scattering foils and photon flattening filters are easy to confuse. Which statement is correct?
A. Foils spread electrons. Filters flatten photons. Using the filter in electron mode would generate unwanted bremsstrahlung.
B. They are the same piece of metal inserted for both modes.
C. Foils are used only for Co-60 gamma rays.
D. Filters are made of tissue-equivalent plastic so they do not harden the beam.
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: The two devices solve different problems and are swapped by the carousel when the mode changes. A high-Z filter in an electron beam would be a bremsstrahlung target. Filters are high-Z metal precisely so they can attenuate the photon peak, which also hardens it.

## Superficial Therapy Units

### Q1
Stem: Superficial therapy is typically:
A. 50–150 kVp, short SSD, HVL stated in mm Al, dmax at the skin
B. 6–18 MV, dmax at 1.5–3.5 cm
C. A proton beam of 150–250 MeV
D. Ir-192 at 1 cm from a cylinder
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Superficial units treat shallow lesions with kV x-rays, applicators, and SSDs often between about 15 and 30 cm. There is no megavoltage buildup; the dose is highest at the surface. Linacs, protons, and HDR are different modalities.

### Q2
Stem: Orthovoltage differs from superficial therapy in that orthovoltage:
A. Uses a harder beam, often specified in mm Cu, and can treat somewhat deeper than a superficial unit
B. Is another name for 18 MV photons
C. Has a Bragg peak
D. Uses a mean energy of 1.25 MeV
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Orthovoltage is still kilovoltage, classically up to a few hundred kVp, with a Thoraeus or copper filter and HVL in mm Cu. It is not a linac energy, not a proton beam, and not Co-60. Dose still starts at the surface, but the falloff is slower than a soft superficial beam.

### Q3
Stem: A superficial cone (applicator) is used mainly to:
A. Set a fixed SSD and limit the field to the lesion
B. Flatten a 15 MV beam
C. Produce a Bragg peak at 2 cm
D. Replace the HVL measurement
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: The cone touches or stands off the skin at a known SSD and defines the field. It does not flatten megavoltage photons, create a Bragg peak, or take the place of an HVL specification.

### Q4
Stem: Percent depth dose from a superficial beam is highest at the surface and falls in the first centimeter. A planner who expects 6 MV-style skin sparing will:
A. Underestimate the skin dose and may overdose the surface
B. Correctly predict a 1.5 cm buildup
C. Need a bolus of 1.5 cm to reach dmax
D. Be looking at the right buildup curve
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: kV dose is already maximum at the skin. There is no 1.5 cm buildup and no reason to add 6 MV-style bolus to “reach dmax.” Treating a superficial lesion with megavoltage skin-sparing assumptions is the mismatch this question is aimed at.

### Q5
Stem: Beam quality on the superficial unit is checked with HVL. Beam quality on a 6 MV linac is checked with a depth-dose or TPR index because:
A. An aluminum HVL for a 6 MV beam would be many centimeters and is not the clinical specification
B. Linacs do not have energy
C. HVL is defined only for electrons
D. TPR cannot be measured in water
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Megavoltage beams are specified by percent depth dose at 10 cm or by TPR 20/10, not by mm of aluminum. HVL is a photon concept, not an electron-only concept, and TPR is measured in water or plastic.

## Linac Accelerator Quiz

### Q1
Stem: Which component is in the beam for flattened photons and out of the beam for electrons?
A. Target and flattening filter
B. Monitor chamber
C. Electron scattering foil
D. Applicator
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Photon mode needs the target and the flattening filter. Electron mode parks both and uses a scattering foil and an applicator instead. The monitor chamber stays for both modes.

### Q2
Stem: Dose rate on an FFF beam is higher than on the flattened beam of the same nominal energy mainly because:
A. The flattening filter is no longer attenuating the center of the beam
B. The waveguide is longer
C. The half-life of the target is shorter
D. The ion chamber applies a factor of 1.44
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Taking the high-Z cone out of the central axis removes that attenuation, so more photons reach the patient per pulse and the dose rate rises. The target is not radioactive with a treatment half-life, and the chamber does not multiply by the mean-life factor.

### Q3
Stem: Geometric penumbra is larger for a Co-60 unit than for a linac primarily because:
A. The Co-60 source has a finite diameter, while a linac target is nearly a point
B. Co-60 has a higher energy than 18 MV
C. The linac has no collimators
D. Co-60 dose is prescribed in roentgen
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Penumbra width grows with source size and with distance from the collimator to the patient. A cobalt source is large. A linac focal spot is millimeters or less. Co-60 is lower in energy than 18 MV, linacs do have collimators, and the prescription is absorbed dose.

### Q4
Stem: A 15 MV photon beam is chosen instead of 6 MV for a deep pelvis. Besides better percent depth dose, the planner should also expect:
A. A deeper dmax and a neutron component from the head that 6 MV does not have
B. A shallower dmax and no change in shielding
C. A Bragg peak at mid-pelvis
D. Skin dose higher than a superficial kV beam
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Higher MV moves dmax deeper and improves the depth dose, and photonuclear neutrons appear in the high-energy head. There is no Bragg peak. Skin dose with buildup is still far below a superficial kV beam, which deposits its maximum at the surface.

### Q5
Stem: The light field and the crosshair are aligned to the radiation axis during machine QA because:
A. The light is what the therapists use to set the patient, and it must represent the radiation aperture
B. The light field measures HVL
C. The crosshair is the bending-magnet energy slit
D. A mismatch changes the decay constant of the beam
Answer: A
Objective: I.5
Why: Patients are set up to light and lasers. If those disagree with the radiation field or the central axis, the block or MLC edge will not fall where the plan shows it. Light does not measure HVL, and photon beams do not have a decay constant.

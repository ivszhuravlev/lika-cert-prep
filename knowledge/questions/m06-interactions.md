# Module 6 — Interaction of Radiation with Matter

Original items. Photon energy and wavelength use E (keV) = 12.4 / λ (Å) and E (keV) = 1.24 / λ (nm). Speed of light is 3.00 × 10^8 m/s. The Compton wavelength shift is 0.0243(1 − cos θ) Å.

## Interaction of Photons with Matter

### Q1
Stem: In a photoelectric event the photon:
A. Scatters and keeps most of its energy
B. Is absorbed, and an electron leaves with the photon energy minus the binding energy
C. Creates an electron-positron pair if its energy is 200 keV
D. Changes direction with no energy loss
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: Photoelectric absorption removes the photon. The photoelectron kinetic energy is hν minus the shell binding energy, and the atom then emits a characteristic x-ray or an Auger electron. Compton scatter is the partial-energy event. Pair production needs at least 1.022 MeV. Coherent scatter is the no-energy-loss direction change.

### Q2
Stem: Compton scatter in soft tissue depends mainly on:
A. Z^3 / E^3
B. Electron density, almost independent of Z per electron
C. Z^2, and it turns on only above 1.022 MeV
D. The half-life of the target nucleus
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: Compton interaction is with a loosely bound electron, so the probability per gram tracks electron density, not Z the way the photoelectric effect does. Z^3/E^3 is photoelectric. Z^2 and the 1.022 MeV threshold belong to pair production. Half-life is not an interaction cross section.

### Q3
Stem: If Z is doubled and photon energy is unchanged, the photoelectric mass attenuation coefficient changes by about a factor of:
A. 2
B. 4
C. 8
D. 16
Answer: C
Objective: I.4
Why: The mass attenuation coefficient for the photoelectric effect scales about as Z^3/E^3. Doubling Z multiplies it by 2^3 = 8. A factor of 2 is linear in Z. A factor of 4 is Z^2, which is closer to pair production per atom. A factor of 16 is Z^4, which is the per-atom photoelectric estimate, not the per-gram coefficient this stem asked for.

### Q4
Stem: Pair production in the field of a nucleus:
A. Can occur for a 511 keV photon
B. Produces an electron and a positron and requires at least 1.022 MeV
C. Is the main interaction of a 6 MV beam in water
D. Leaves the photon energy unchanged
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: The threshold is two electron rest energies, 1.022 MeV. A 511 keV photon is below threshold. In water, Compton still dominates through the usual linac range. Pair production absorbs the photon; it does not leave the energy unchanged.

### Q5
Stem: Coherent (Rayleigh) scatter is most relevant when:
A. The photon energy is low and Z is high, and essentially no energy is transferred
B. The photon energy is 15 MeV in water
C. A neutron is captured
D. The event must produce a 511 keV pair
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: Coherent scatter is an atomic, no-energy-transfer deflection. It contributes to diagnostic scatter, especially at low energy and higher Z, and it is negligible for absorbed dose at therapy energies. It is not neutron capture and it is not pair production.

### Q6
Stem: If Z is doubled and photon energy is unchanged, the photoelectric probability per atom changes by about a factor of:
A. 2
B. 8, because the mass coefficient goes as Z^3
C. 4
D. 16, from a Z^4 per-atom estimate
Answer: D
Objective: I.4
Why: The atomic photoelectric cross section is steeper than the mass coefficient. A usual per-atom estimate is about Z^4, so doubling Z multiplies it by 16. The factor of 8 is 2^3, which belongs to the mass attenuation coefficient, not to this per-atom stem. Z^2 gives 4. Linear Z gives 2. Some tables use Z^5 per atom, which would be 32, and that is not one of these choices.

## Energy of Photons

### Q1
Stem: A 511 keV photon Compton-scatters at 180°. The scattered photon energy is E / (1 + 2E/511). What is that energy?
A. 170 keV
B. 255 keV
C. 511 keV
D. 1022 keV
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: E' = 511 / (1 + 2 × 511/511) = 511 / (1 + 2) = 511/3 = 170 keV. 255 keV is the 90° result, 511 / (1 + 511/511) = 511/2. 511 keV assumes no energy loss. 1022 keV adds a second rest energy instead of sharing the incident energy.

### Q2
Stem: At a given incident energy, the Compton electron receives the most energy when the photon scatters at:
A. 0°
B. 90°
C. 180°
D. An angle that leaves the photon energy unchanged
Answer: C
Objective: I.4
Why: The scattered-photon formula has (1 − cos θ). Cos 180° = −1, so (1 − cos θ) = 2, the scattered photon keeps the least energy, and the electron gets the most. A 0° scatter transfers nothing. 90° is intermediate.

### Q3
Stem: The same 511 keV photon scatters at 90°. The scattered photon energy is:
A. 170 keV
B. 255 keV
C. 340 keV
D. 511 keV
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: E' = E / (1 + E/511) = 511 / (1 + 1) = 255.5 keV, which is 255 keV at this precision. 170 keV is the 180° result. 340 keV is the energy given to the electron at 180° (511 − 170), not the 90° photon. 511 keV is the unscattered beam.

### Q4
Stem: A photoelectric event and a Compton event both occur in tissue at 80 keV. The photoelectric event is more likely to:
A. Deposit the entire photon energy locally, aside from a small characteristic or Auger escape
B. Send a scattered photon out of the patient with most of the energy
C. Produce a positron
D. Leave Z of the atom lower by 2
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: Photoelectric absorption removes the photon. At 80 keV in tissue the binding energy is small compared with 80 keV, so most of the energy is in the photoelectron. Compton scatter is the event that lets a photon leave. Pair production is impossible at 80 keV. Z does not drop by 2; that is alpha decay.

### Q5
Stem: Photon energy and frequency are related by E = hν. Doubling the frequency of a photon:
A. Doubles its energy and halves its wavelength
B. Halves its energy and doubles its wavelength
C. Doubles its wavelength and leaves the energy unchanged
D. Changes only the speed of the photon in vacuum
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: E is proportional to ν, and c = λν, so a higher frequency is a shorter wavelength at the same speed c. The photon speed in vacuum does not change with frequency.

## Wavelength and Frequency Tutorial

### Q1
Stem: A photon has wavelength 0.248 Å. What is its energy? Use E (keV) = 12.4 / λ (Å).
A. 50 keV
B. 25 keV
C. 12.4 keV
D. 5.0 keV
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: E = 12.4 / 0.248 = 50 keV. 25 keV would be twice the wavelength (0.496 Å). 12.4 keV is the constant itself, as if λ were 1 Å. 5.0 keV moves the decimal.

### Q2
Stem: A photon energy is 124 keV. What is the wavelength in angstroms?
A. 0.100 Å
B. 1.00 Å
C. 10.0 Å
D. 0.010 Å
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: λ = 12.4 / E = 12.4 / 124 = 0.100 Å. 1.00 Å is 12.4 keV, not 124 keV. 10.0 Å and 0.010 Å are decimal errors on the division.

### Q3
Stem: A wavelength is 0.0124 nm. Using E (keV) = 1.24 / λ (nm), the energy is:
A. 10 keV
B. 100 keV
C. 1.24 keV
D. 1000 keV
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: E = 1.24 / 0.0124 = 100 keV. The same photon is 0.124 Å, and 12.4 / 0.124 = 100 keV. 10 keV uses 0.124 nm. 1.24 keV treats the wavelength as 1 nm. 1000 keV moves the decimal by ten.

### Q4
Stem: A 10.0 keV photon has wavelength 1.24 Å, which is 1.24 × 10^(−10) m. What is the frequency? Use c = 3.00 × 10^8 m/s.
A. 2.42 × 10^18 Hz
B. 3.00 × 10^8 Hz
C. 2.42 × 10^16 Hz
D. 1.24 × 10^10 Hz
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: ν = c / λ = (3.00 × 10^8) / (1.24 × 10^(−10)) = 2.42 × 10^18 Hz. Written without parentheses, 3.00 × 10^8 / 1.24 × 10^(−10) is not that quotient. 3.00 × 10^8 Hz is c, not a frequency of this photon. 2.42 × 10^16 Hz uses λ = 1.24 × 10^(−8) m. 1.24 × 10^10 Hz is not c/λ.

### Q5
Stem: An incident photon has wavelength 0.050 Å and Compton-scatters at 90°. The wavelength increases by 0.0243 Å. What is the scattered wavelength?
A. 0.0743 Å
B. 0.050 Å
C. 0.0243 Å
D. 0.0986 Å
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: Δλ = 0.0243(1 − cos 90°) = 0.0243 Å. Scattered wavelength = 0.050 + 0.0243 = 0.0743 Å. 0.050 Å ignores the shift. 0.0243 Å is only the shift. 0.0986 Å uses the 180° shift, 0.0486 Å, added to 0.050 Å.

### Q6
Stem: What is the wavelength of a 511 keV annihilation photon? Use λ (Å) = 12.4 / E (keV).
A. 0.0243 Å
B. 0.243 Å
C. 12.4 Å
D. 511 Å
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: λ = 12.4 / 511 = 0.0243 Å. That is also the Compton wavelength of the electron, which is why a 90° Compton shift equals the wavelength of a 511 keV photon. 0.243 Å is a tenfold error. 12.4 Å and 511 Å skip the division.

### Q7
Stem: A photon is 60 keV. Its wavelength is then cut in half by a different process that truly halves λ. The new energy is:
A. 30 keV
B. 60 keV
C. 120 keV
D. 15 keV
Answer: C
Objective: I.4
Why: Energy is inversely proportional to wavelength. Half the wavelength doubles the energy: 60 × 2 = 120 keV. 30 keV halves the energy, which would require double the wavelength. 15 keV quarters it.

### Q8
Stem: A photon frequency is 6.00 × 10^18 Hz. Using λ = c/ν with c = 3.00 × 10^8 m/s, then E (keV) = 12.4 / λ (Å), what is the energy?
A. 24.8 keV
B. 12.4 keV
C. 49.6 keV
D. 6.0 keV
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: λ = (3.00 × 10^8) / (6.00 × 10^18) = 5.00 × 10^(−11) m = 0.500 Å, because 1 Å = 10^(−10) m. E = 12.4 / 0.500 = 24.8 keV. Written without parentheses, 3.00 × 10^8 / 6.00 × 10^18 is not that quotient. The same result is hν with h = 4.14 × 10^(−15) eV·s: 4.14 × 10^(−15) × 6.00 × 10^18 = 2.48 × 10^4 eV. 12.4 keV uses λ = 1 Å. 49.6 keV doubles the correct energy. 6.0 keV treats the frequency’s leading digit as the energy.

### Q9
Stem: A 6.00 MeV photon undergoes pair production in the nuclear field. The kinetic energy shared by the electron and positron is:
A. 6.00 MeV
B. 5.49 MeV
C. 4.98 MeV
D. 3.96 MeV
Answer: C
Objective: I.4
Why: Pair production spends 1.022 MeV creating the two rest masses. Shared kinetic energy is 6.00 − 1.022 = 4.978 MeV, about 4.98 MeV. 5.49 MeV subtracts only one 511 keV. 3.96 MeV subtracts 1.022 MeV twice. 6.00 MeV forgets the threshold.

## Interaction of Photons with Matter Quiz

### Q1
Stem: At 25 keV in bone versus soft tissue, the image contrast is high mainly because:
A. The photoelectric effect scales steeply with Z, and bone has a higher effective Z
B. Pair production occurs only in bone
C. Compton scatter is impossible in tissue at 25 keV
D. Neutrons from the tube are stopped by bone
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: Diagnostic contrast between bone and soft tissue is a photoelectric, Z-dependent effect. Pair production is not available at 25 keV. Compton scatter still exists at 25 keV, but it is not what creates the strong bone contrast. A diagnostic tube does not produce treatment neutrons.

### Q2
Stem: In the buildup region of a megavoltage photon beam, absorbed dose rises even though kerma is highest at the surface. The particles that carry dose deeper are:
A. The secondary electrons set in motion by photon interactions
B. Alpha particles from the target
C. Thermal neutrons only
D. The photons themselves, which have a Bragg peak
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: Photons interact and set electrons moving, mostly by Compton at therapy energies. Those electrons travel forward and deposit dose downstream, which is the buildup. Photons do not have a Bragg peak. Alphas and neutrons are not the secondary particles of a clinical photon buildup curve.

### Q3
Stem: A 10 MeV photon in lead is more likely than a 10 MeV photon in water to undergo pair production because pair production:
A. Scales about as Z^2 and lead has a much higher Z
B. Scales as 1/Z
C. Occurs only in hydrogen
D. Has a threshold of 10 MeV, so it is forbidden in water
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: The pair-production cross section per atom rises approximately with Z^2, so high-Z shielding favors pair production once the photon is above 1.022 MeV. The threshold is the same in water and in lead. It does not scale as 1/Z and it is not limited to hydrogen.

### Q4
Stem: Which event changes the photon wavelength by 0.0243 Å and no more, for any incident energy?
A. Compton scatter at 90°
B. Photoelectric absorption
C. A 180° Compton scatter
D. Pair production at threshold
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: The Compton shift depends only on angle: Δλ = 0.0243(1 − cos θ) Å. At 90°, cos θ = 0, so Δλ = 0.0243 Å. At 180° the shift is 0.0486 Å. Photoelectric absorption removes the photon. Pair production does not leave a Compton-shifted photon.

### Q5
Stem: A planner comparing kV CT contrast with MV dose deposition should remember that:
A. CT contrast is largely photoelectric, while MV dose in tissue is largely Compton, so a high-Z streak on CT is not the same as a large MV attenuation
B. Both are pair production, so the CT number can be used uncorrected for a hip prosthesis
C. Compton scatter cannot occur during a CT scan
D. MV beams interact only by coherent scatter
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: kV photons emphasize Z^3 photoelectric absorption, which is why iodine and metal look extreme on CT. Megavoltage dose in tissue is driven by Compton scatter and electron density. That is why contrast and metal need a density override rather than a raw CT number. Pair production and coherent scatter are not the dominant MV dose processes in tissue.

# Module 03. Radioactivity

Original items. Source-card rows added after the RM review: Ra-226, Au-198, and Pd-103 lead half-value layers and constants, and how to choose an option when published lead HVL tables disagree.

## Source table additions

### Q1
Stem: A filtered Ra-226 source has a mean gamma energy of about 0.83 MeV. Which lead half-value layer belongs to it on a brachytherapy source card?
A. 0.025 mm
B. 2.5 mm
C. 8 mm
D. 12 mm
Answer: C
Objective: I.7
Why: Radium's teaching lead HVL is about 8 mm, between cesium (0.662 MeV, 5.5–6.5 mm) and cobalt (1.25 MeV, 11–12 mm), which is where a 0.83 MeV mean energy should fall. 0.025 mm is the I-125 figure, 2.5 mm is the Au-198 and narrow-beam Ir-192 figure, and 12 mm is Co-60. The option spacing is wide enough that the exact radium figure in a given table does not change the letter.

### Q2
Stem: Which set of values describes Au-198 on a brachytherapy source card?
A. Half-life 2.7 days, gamma 0.41 MeV, lead HVL about 2.5 mm, permanent grains
B. Half-life 2.7 days, gamma 0.66 MeV, lead HVL about 6 mm, temporary tubes
C. Half-life 17 days, photons about 21 keV, lead HVL about 0.008 mm, permanent seeds
D. Half-life 74 days, gamma about 0.38 MeV, lead HVL about 3 mm, temporary HDR source
Answer: A
Objective: I.7
Why: Au-198 is 2.7 days, 0.412 MeV, about 2.5 mm of lead for one HVL, and its grains are a permanent implant despite the short half-life; its exposure-rate constant is about 2.38 R cm² per mCi per hour. Option B mixes gold's half-life with cesium's energy and shielding. Option C is Pd-103. Option D is Ir-192. The short half-life is the reason the grains can be left in place, not a reason to call them temporary.

### Q3
Stem: Pd-103 seeds and I-125 seeds are both low-energy electron-capture sources. Which statement about their lead half-value layers is correct?
A. Both are about 2.5 mm, because both are brachytherapy seeds
B. Pd-103 needs more lead than I-125, because it decays faster
C. Both are about 0.025 mm, because their photon energies are within a few keV
D. Pd-103 is about 0.008 mm and I-125 about 0.025 mm, because 21 keV photons are softer than 28 keV photons
Answer: D
Objective: I.7
Why: Lead HVL follows photon energy, and at these energies the photoelectric cross section rises steeply as energy falls, so the 21 keV Pd-103 photons are stopped by about one-third the lead that 28 keV I-125 photons need: 0.008 mm versus 0.025 mm. Half-life has nothing to do with shielding thickness. Neither seed is anywhere near 2.5 mm; that is the figure for 0.4 MeV sources such as Au-198 and Ir-192. The 7 keV difference matters because the attenuation coefficient changes by roughly the cube of the energy ratio in this range.

### Q4
Stem: An item asks for the lead half-value layer of Cs-137 and offers 0.025 mm, 6 mm, 2.5 mm, and 12 mm. One textbook prints 5.5 mm and this bank prints 6.5 mm. Which option is correct, and does the disagreement matter?
A. 2.5 mm, because the smaller textbook figure should be rounded down
B. 6 mm, and the disagreement does not matter because both 5.5 and 6.5 are nearest to 6
C. 12 mm, because 6.5 rounds up to the next option
D. The item cannot be answered until the two sources agree
Answer: B
Objective: I.7
Why: Exam option sets space the lead HVLs about one nuclide family apart, so 5.5 and 6.5 mm both sit nearest the 6 mm option and the published range (5.5–6.5 mm) selects one letter. 2.5 mm is the Au-198 and narrow-beam Ir-192 figure, and 12 mm is Co-60. The difference between the textbooks is narrow-beam versus broad-beam geometry and rounding, not a physics dispute, so it never needs to be resolved to pick an option. A stem that needs the HVL for arithmetic prints it.

### Q5
Stem: An item asks for the lead half-value layer of Ir-192 and offers 0.025 mm, 12 mm, 2.5 mm, and 6.5 mm. This bank quotes Ir-192 as 3 to 6 mm. Which option should be chosen?
A. 6.5 mm, because it is inside the bank's quoted range
B. 12 mm, because iridium has a complex high-energy spectrum
C. 2.5 mm, because it is the narrow-beam table value and 6.5 mm is the cesium option
D. 0.025 mm, because Ir-192 is a brachytherapy source
Answer: C
Objective: I.7
Why: When a set offers both a 2.5 mm choice and a cesium-sized choice, 2.5 mm is the Ir-192 answer: it is the narrow-beam first HVL that Khan-style tables print, and the bank's upper figure of 6 mm is a broad-beam or equilibrium value that would collide with Cs-137. 6.5 mm is the cesium option. 12 mm is Co-60. 0.025 mm is I-125; being a brachytherapy source says nothing about energy, and Ir-192's 0.38 MeV mean is ten times I-125's.

### Q6
Stem: Rank Co-60, Ra-226, Cs-137, and Ir-192 from the thickest lead half-value layer to the thinnest.
A. Co-60, Ra-226, Cs-137, Ir-192
B. Ra-226, Co-60, Cs-137, Ir-192
C. Co-60, Cs-137, Ra-226, Ir-192
D. Ir-192, Cs-137, Ra-226, Co-60
Answer: A
Objective: I.7
Why: Lead HVL tracks photon energy: Co-60 at 1.25 MeV needs 11–12 mm, radium at 0.83 MeV about 8 mm, Cs-137 at 0.662 MeV 5.5–6.5 mm, Ir-192 at about 0.38 MeV 2.5–6 mm with 2.5–3 mm as the narrow-beam figure. Option B puts radium above cobalt, which would need radium's mean energy to exceed 1.25 MeV; it does not. Option C swaps radium and cesium. Option D is the order reversed. Checking the energies in order (1.25, 0.83, 0.662, 0.38 MeV) confirms the ranking without memorizing any HVL.

### Q7
Stem: A stem prints the lead half-value layer of Ra-226 as 8 mm. How much lead reduces the exposure rate from a radium source to one-eighth of its unshielded value?
A. 2.7 mm
B. 8 mm
C. 64 mm
D. 24 mm
Answer: D
Objective: I.10
Why: One-eighth is 2 to the minus 3, so three half-value layers are needed: 3 × 8 mm = 24 mm. Check by halving: 8 mm leaves one-half, 16 mm one-quarter, 24 mm one-eighth. 2.7 mm is 8 divided by 3, dividing where the problem multiplies. 8 mm is a single HVL, which leaves one-half. 64 mm is 8 × 8, multiplying the HVL by the denominator instead of by the number of halvings.

### Q8
Stem: A 10 mCi Au-198 source has an exposure-rate constant of 2.38 R cm squared per mCi per hour. What is the unshielded exposure rate at 50 cm?
A. 2.4 mR/h
B. 9.5 mR/h
C. 38 mR/h
D. 476 mR/h
Answer: B
Objective: I.10
Why: Exposure rate is the constant times activity divided by distance squared: 2.38 × 10 = 23.8; 50 squared = 2500; 23.8 / 2500 = 0.00952 R/h = 9.5 mR/h. Check with inverse square from 100 cm: at 1 m the rate is 23.8 / 10000 = 2.38 mR/h, and halving the distance quadruples it to 9.5 mR/h. 2.4 mR/h is the 100 cm value. 38 mR/h is the 25 cm value. 476 mR/h comes from dividing by 50 instead of 50 squared.

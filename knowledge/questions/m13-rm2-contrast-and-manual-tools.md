# Module 13. Acquisition of Patient Data

Original items. Manual contour tools and the caliper check of CT separation, iodinated contrast precautions, and the retrograde urethrogram.

## Manual Contour Tools, Contrast Precautions and Urethrogram

### Q1
Stem: The anterior-posterior separation measured with calipers at the central axis is 22.0 cm, but the planning CT shows 20.4 cm between the anterior and posterior skin surfaces at the same level. The most likely explanation is:
A. The caliper reading must be scaled by the CT magnification factor
B. The external contour on the CT is incomplete or the skin was clipped at the edge of the field of view
C. The difference is normal because calipers measure tissue density, not distance
D. The CT was acquired with contrast, which shrinks the apparent separation
Answer: B
Objective: II.1
Why: The caliper separation is the independent check of the CT geometry, and a disagreement of over a centimeter points to a problem with the CT outline: a clipped skin surface, a wrong body-contour threshold, couch included as patient, or a different patient state. CT images are reconstructed at true scale, so no magnification factor applies. Calipers measure distance only. Contrast changes Hounsfield numbers inside vessels, not the skin separation.

### Q2
Stem: Before CT simulation was available, which tool reproduced the patient's external outline at full scale by following the skin with a stylus linked to a pen?
A. A pantograph
B. A lead wire
C. A set of calipers
D. A spirometer
Answer: A
Objective: II.1
Why: The pantograph is a mechanical linkage that traces the skin surface directly onto paper at 1:1 and avoids the spring-back distortion of a wire lifted off the patient. A lead or solder wire was pressed to the skin and then traced, which is the simpler and more error-prone method. Calipers measure separations, not outlines. A spirometer measures breathing volume.

### Q3
Stem: A patient scheduled for a contrast-enhanced planning CT takes metformin and has an eGFR of 28 mL per minute per 1.73 m². The appropriate action is to:
A. Proceed, because metformin protects the kidneys from contrast injury
B. Hold metformin for 48 hours before the scan and resume immediately afterward
C. Obtain a physician decision on whether to give contrast, and if given, hold metformin for 48 hours after the scan until renal function is rechecked
D. Replace the intravenous contrast with oral contrast, which carries the same vascular information
Answer: C
Objective: II.4
Why: An eGFR below about 30 is the threshold at which most protocols require a physician to weigh the benefit of contrast and order hydration, and metformin is held after contrast because it accumulates and can cause lactic acidosis if contrast injures the kidneys; it is restarted once renal function is confirmed. Metformin is not protective. Holding it only before the scan and restarting immediately misses the window of risk. Oral contrast outlines bowel and gives no vascular enhancement.

### Q4
Stem: On a lateral simulation film with a retrograde urethrogram, the contrast column ends in a beak-shaped tip. That tip marks:
A. The bladder neck, so the superior field border is placed at the beak
B. The seminal vesicles, so the posterior border is placed at the beak
C. The prostatic base, which lies 1 cm inferior to the beak
D. The level of the external sphincter, with the prostatic apex about 1 to 1.5 cm superior to the beak
Answer: D
Objective: II.4
Why: Retrograde contrast fills the urethra and stops at the external sphincter, and the prostatic apex sits about 1 to 1.5 cm above that beak, so the inferior field edge was conventionally set about 1 cm below the beak tip. The bladder neck and the base are at the superior end of the gland, well above the beak. The seminal vesicles are posterior-superior and are not outlined by a urethrogram. MRI fusion has largely replaced the technique, but the landmark is still asked.

# Module 44. Timed Exam Planning A

Original CMD practice items. Not retired MDCB questions and not a commercial quiz. Totals below are the recognition regimens and organ correlates already used in the site-oncology and QUANTEC notes. The signed prescription is the patient's dose.

## Timed Exam Planning A

### Q1
Stem: An oropharyngeal primary and both sides of the neck are planned at one isocenter. Which energy and technique fit the superficial nodes and the air column in the larynx?
A. 18 MV opposed laterals, because a thick neck needs the higher photon energy
B. 6 MeV en face electrons to the primary and both necks
C. 6 MV IMRT or VMAT at one isocenter
D. Total-skin electrons in six positions
Answer: C
Objective: III.1
Why: Head-and-neck treatment is 6 MV IMRT or VMAT, usually one isocenter, so the low neck does not need a separate supraclavicular match. Eighteen MV is a poor neck energy: buildup is deeper, superficial nodes are underdosed, and the air column in the larynx adds lateral disequilibrium. Six MeV electrons fit a superficial skin lesion, not an oropharyngeal primary with neck nodes. Six-position total-skin electrons are a different procedure. The isodose problem is buildup and lateral disequilibrium, which is why 6 MV is the neck energy.

### Q2
Stem: A well-lateralized tonsil primary is planned for elective neck treatment. The primary does not approach midline. Which nodal volume fits?
A. Ipsilateral levels II through IV
B. Bilateral levels II through V and the retropharyngeal nodes
C. Levels I through III on both sides
D. No elective nodes
Answer: A
Objective: III.4
Why: Oropharynx elective nodes are levels II through IV. A well-lateralized tonsil can take the ipsilateral neck. Midline disease and base of tongue take both necks. Bilateral levels II through V plus the retropharyngeal nodes are the nasopharynx volume. Oral cavity uses levels I through III, bilateral when the primary approaches midline. Early glottic cancer on one vocal cord is the site that takes no elective nodes. This tonsil does not.

### Q3
Stem: Early glottic cancer is confined to one vocal cord. Which nodal decision, beam, and recognition dose fit?
A. Bilateral levels II through V and the retropharyngeal nodes, 70 Gy in 35 fractions
B. Bilateral levels II through IV plus level VI, 70 Gy in 35 fractions
C. Inguinal nodes to 45 Gy
D. No elective nodes, opposed lateral 6 MV photons to about 63 Gy in 28 fractions
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: Early glottic cancer on one vocal cord takes no elective nodes. Opposed lateral 6 MV photons go to about 63 Gy in 28 fractions. The arytenoids limit the posterior edge. Nasopharynx, not this glottis, takes bilateral levels II through V and the retropharyngeal nodes, and 70 Gy in 35 fractions is gross head-and-neck disease. Hypopharynx, and larynx with subglottic extension, take bilateral II through IV plus level VI, the anterior compartment between the carotids. Inguinal nodes at 45 Gy are not a glottic volume. Level VI is not added to a single-cord early glottic field.

### Q4
Stem: A 35-fraction simultaneous integrated boost treats gross head-and-neck disease, intermediate-risk disease, and elective nodes together. Which three doses are those volumes, in that order?
A. 69.96 Gy, 59.4 Gy, and 54.12 Gy
B. 70 Gy, 63 Gy, and 56 Gy
C. 60 Gy, 60 Gy, and 60 Gy
D. 40 Gy, 30 Gy, and 20 Gy
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: The 35-fraction boost is 70 Gy to gross disease, 63 Gy to intermediate-risk disease, and 56 Gy to elective nodes. Per fraction that is 70/35 = 2.0 Gy, 63/35 = 1.8 Gy, and 56/35 = 1.6 Gy. The 33-fraction schedule is a different boost: 69.96 Gy at 2.12 Gy per fraction, intermediate 59.4 Gy, and elective 54.12 Gy. Do not hang 63 Gy and 56 Gy on those 33 fractions. Postoperative dose at the primary site stays near 60 Gy and is not the three-volume boost. Forty gray in 15, 30 Gy in 10 or in 15, and 20 Gy in 10 are other recognition totals, not this boost.

### Q5
Stem: Left-sided whole-breast radiotherapy uses a conventional course in the 45 to 50.4 Gy range. A cavity boost is added only if the prescription includes one. Heart and the left anterior descending region limit this side. Which conventional heart statement fits?
A. The heart limit is lung V20 at or under 30%, and the breast course is 25 Gy in 5 fractions
B. The heart limit is a cord Dmax of 50 Gy
C. Pericarditis correlates are mean dose under 26 Gy and V30 under 46%. V25 under 10% is the separate long-term cardiac mortality correlate
D. The heart limit is the whole-liver mean under 30 to 32 Gy
Answer: C
Objective: III.8
Why: QUANTEC heart correlates for pericarditis are mean dose under 26 Gy and V30 under 46%. V25 under 10% is a different endpoint, long-term cardiac mortality, and it does not replace the pericarditis pair. Left-sided tangents are the cardiac cases; the left anterior descending region is part of that problem. A conventional whole-breast dose is 45 to 50.4 Gy. Lung V20 at or under 30% is the lung row. The cord correlate is a Dmax of 50 Gy. The liver mean under 30 to 32 Gy is the conventional whole-liver row. Twenty-five gray in 5 is short-course rectum. Ipsilateral lung and contralateral breast still limit a breast plan, on their own rows.

### Q6
Stem: The prescription is 40 Gy in 15 fractions to the whole breast. Which site and which limiting organs fit this prescription?
A. Hypofractionated whole breast, limited by heart on the left and by ipsilateral lung
B. A short glioblastoma course, limited by brainstem and optic structures
C. Peripheral lung stereotactic radiotherapy, 54 Gy in 3 fractions
D. Palliative whole-brain treatment, 30 Gy in 10 fractions
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Forty gray in 15 fractions is a standard hypofractionated breast course and also a short glioblastoma course. This prescription names the whole breast, so the limits are heart on a left-sided case, ipsilateral lung, and contralateral breast. The other breast hypofractionation to recognize is 42.5 Gy in 16 fractions, which is not the schedule written here. Brainstem and optic structures limit a glioblastoma near those organs. Fifty-four gray in 3 is a peripheral lung stereotactic example. Thirty gray in 10 is palliative whole-brain treatment. The same fraction count does not move 40 Gy in 15 onto those sites.

### Q7
Stem: Intact prostate, conventional fractionation. Which total and which organs fit?
A. 60 Gy in 20 fractions of 3 Gy, limited by the lenses
B. 25 Gy in 5 fractions, limited by the chest wall
C. 64.8 Gy in 36 fractions to the fossa, plus inguinal nodes
D. A high-70s course such as 78 Gy or 79.2 Gy at 1.8 to 2 Gy per fraction, limited by rectum and bladder
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: Intact conventional prostate courses to recognize sit in the high 70s, including 78 Gy and 79.2 Gy, at 1.8 to 2 Gy per fraction. Rectum and bladder limit the plan, then femoral heads and bowel. Sixty gray in 20 fractions of 3 Gy is moderate hypofractionation of an intact gland, a different schedule, and the lenses are a cranial-field issue. Twenty-five gray in 5 is short-course rectum. 64.8 Gy in 36 fractions is a postoperative fossa course, and elective pelvic nodes for prostate are iliac and obturator nodes, not inguinal nodes. Penile-bulb correlates include a mean dose to 95% of the gland under 50 Gy, recorded in this bank as D95 under 50 Gy, plus D90 under 50 Gy. Those 50 Gy bulb figures are not the cord Dmax of 50 Gy.

### Q8
Stem: Conventional fractionation after prostatectomy. The target is the fossa. Risk calls for elective pelvic nodes. Which fossa dose and which nodes fit?
A. An intact high-70s prescription to the fossa, plus inguinal nodes
B. About 64 to 66 Gy to the fossa, for example 64.8 Gy in 36 fractions or 66 Gy in 33 fractions, and about 45 Gy to the iliac and obturator nodes
C. 60 Gy in 20 fractions to the fossa
D. 52.5 Gy in 20 fractions, with the inguinal nodes included as the pelvic volume
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: The postoperative recognition dose is about 64 to 66 Gy at 1.8 to 2 Gy per fraction. 64.8/36 = 1.8 Gy, and 66/33 = 2 Gy, which are the courses 64.8 Gy in 36 fractions and 66 Gy in 33 fractions. That is lower than an intact course in the high 70s. Elective pelvic nodes, when the risk calls for them, are about 45 Gy to the iliac and obturator nodes. They are not inguinal nodes, and they do not raise the fossa to an intact prescription. The postoperative hypofractionated course is 52.5 Gy in 20 fractions. It is not the intact 60 Gy in 20. Femoral heads limit the plan and are bone, not nodes. Rectum and bladder still limit the fossa.

### Q9
Stem: Preoperative rectal radiotherapy uses either 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions or 25 Gy in 5 fractions. Small bowel is contoured. Which bowel correlate matches the contour that was drawn?
A. Individual loops use V15 under 120 cm³. The peritoneal space uses V45 under 195 cm³. Loops do not borrow the peritoneal number
B. Loops use V45 under 195 cm³, and the peritoneal space uses V15 under 120 cm³
C. Small bowel uses the rectal V70 under 20%
D. Small bowel uses the cord Dmax of 50 Gy
Answer: A
Objective: III.8
Why: Both rectal courses are standard: 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions of 1.8 Gy, since 50.4/28 = 1.8, and short course 25 Gy in 5 fractions. Small bowel often limits the high dose. The bowel correlate depends on the contour. Individual loops use V15 under 120 cm³. The peritoneal space uses V45 under 195 cm³. Swapping those pairs puts the peritoneal volume on loops. The rectal set, including V70 under 20%, is the rectum, not the bowel. The cord correlate is a Dmax of 50 Gy. A passing rectal bin does not clear a bowel volume that was scored on the wrong definition.

### Q10
Stem: A peripheral lung tumor is treated with stereotactic radiotherapy. Which schedule fits the periphery, and which copying error does that schedule create if the tumor is ultracentral?
A. 25 Gy in 5 fractions, and that schedule is also the ultracentral lung dose
B. 60 Gy in 30 fractions, and the same course is the 3-fraction peripheral dose
C. A peripheral course such as 54 Gy in 3, 50 Gy in 5, or 48 Gy in 4. A 3-fraction peripheral dose is not copied onto an ultracentral target
D. 8 Gy in 1 fraction, because one fraction makes every lung plan stereotactic
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Peripheral lung stereotactic schedules cluster at 50 to 54 Gy in 3 to 5 fractions. Recognizable examples are 54 Gy in 3, 50 Gy in 5, and 48 Gy in 4. Central and ultracentral tumors sit next to the bronchial tree, esophagus, and great vessels and use a milder schedule from the named protocol. Do not put a 3-fraction peripheral dose on an ultracentral target because the numbers were familiar. Twenty-five gray in 5 is short-course rectum. Sixty gray in 30 is definitive non-small-cell chemoradiation. Eight gray once, 20 Gy in 5, and 30 Gy in 10 are bone-pain schedules. Chest wall, brachial plexus, cord, esophagus, heart, great vessels, and the proximal bronchial tree are the organs that change between a peripheral lesion and a central one.

### Q11
Stem: Definitive non-small-cell lung cancer is 60 Gy in 30 fractions with concurrent chemotherapy. The cord and the combined lungs are both scored. Which pairing of conventional correlates fits those two organs?
A. Cord scored by V20 at or under 30%, and lung scored by a Dmax of 50 Gy
B. Cord scored by a parotid mean under 25 Gy, and lung scored by an optic limit under 55 Gy
C. Cord scored by a whole-brainstem limit under 54 Gy, and lung scored by a kidney mean under 15 Gy to 18 Gy
D. Cord scored by Dmax 50 Gy. Combined lungs scored by V20 at or under 30% and by mean lung dose 20 Gy
Answer: D
Objective: III.1
Why: The course is 60 Gy in 30 fractions of 2 Gy, because 60/30 = 2. The cord is serial, so the conventional correlate is Dmax 50 Gy, a near-maximum, not a mean and not a lung percent. Lung is parallel. The pair is V20 at or under 30% and mean lung dose 20 Gy. Quoting only one of those two lung rows misses the other. A parotid mean under 25 Gy, an optic limit under 55 Gy, a brainstem limit under 54 Gy, and a kidney mean under 15 Gy to 18 Gy are other organs. On this chest plan the esophagus has its own mean under 34 Gy, which is not the cord row and not the lung pair. The target is the primary and the involved nodes, not a routine elective bath of the uninvolved mediastinum.

### Q12
Stem: Limited-stage small-cell lung cancer is treated with concurrent chemotherapy. Which thoracic course is the twice-daily schedule, and what is prophylactic cranial irradiation after a response?
A. 60 Gy in 30 fractions once daily, then 30 Gy in 10 to the whole brain and a spine field
B. 45 Gy in 30 fractions of 1.5 Gy, twice daily at least 6 hours apart, then 25 Gy in 10 to the whole brain with no spine field
C. 54 Gy in 3 fractions to the chest, then 23.4 Gy craniospinal irradiation
D. 66 Gy in 33 fractions twice daily, then 20 Gy in 5 to the brain
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: The twice-daily course is 45 Gy in 30 fractions of 1.5 Gy, because 45/30 = 1.5, two fractions a day at least 6 hours apart. A once-daily alternative is 66 Gy in 33 fractions, not a twice-daily 66 Gy course. After a response, prophylactic cranial irradiation is 25 Gy in 10 to the whole brain. It has no spine field. It is a different prescription from palliative whole-brain treatment of 30 Gy in 10. The lenses stay out of those cranial fields when the technique allows. Sixty gray in 30 is non-small-cell chemoradiation. Fifty-four gray in 3 is peripheral lung stereotactic radiotherapy. 23.4 Gy is average-risk craniospinal axis dose. Twenty gray in 5 is a short whole-brain or bone-pain schedule, not prophylactic cranial irradiation. Cord, lungs, and esophagus limit the chest plan. The anterior-posterior pair is dropped, or the plan becomes IMRT, before the cord carries the full tumor dose.

### Q13
Stem: Squamous cell carcinoma of the anal canal is T3 and node-positive. The drugs are mitomycin and 5-FU. Which dose and which nodes fit?
A. About 54 Gy in 30 fractions of 1.8 Gy, including the mesorectum, the pelvic nodes, and the inguinal nodes
B. 25 Gy in 5 fractions, with the pelvic field stopping at the inguinal ligament
C. 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions to the primary only, because that total is reserved for node-negative T2 disease and the groins can be omitted on a T3 case
D. 70 Gy in 35 fractions to the primary, with retropharyngeal nodes and no pelvic nodes
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Gross anal disease that is T3, T4, or node-positive is often about 54 Gy in 30 fractions of 1.8 Gy, and 54/30 = 1.8. Capecitabine can stand in for 5-FU. The volume includes the anal primary, the mesorectum, the pelvic nodes, and the inguinal nodes. A pelvic field that stops at the inguinal ligament misses the groins. A node-negative T2 primary is often 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions, with the same drugs and the same nodes, so 50.4 Gy is not a reason to drop the groins on a T3 node-positive case. Twenty-five gray in 5 is preoperative rectum. Seventy gray in 35 with retropharyngeal nodes is nasopharyngeal treatment. Small bowel and perineal skin limit the plan. Femoral heads limit the groin dose.

### Q14
Stem: Node-negative bladder cancer is treated with organ preservation after maximal resection and concurrent chemotherapy. The whole bladder is the target. Which technique, dose, and nodal choice fit?
A. 6 MeV electrons to 45 Gy, including inguinal nodes
B. 33 Gy in 5 fractions, including celiac and superior mesenteric nodes
C. 6 MV IMRT or VMAT, about 64 Gy in 32 fractions or 55 Gy in 20 fractions, bladder only, often simulated with an empty bladder
D. 54 Gy in 3 fractions, limited by the chest wall
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Bladder preservation uses 6 MV IMRT or VMAT. Recognition schedules are about 64 Gy in 32 fractions and 55 Gy in 20 fractions. Node-negative treatment covers the bladder. Whole-bladder treatment is often simulated with an empty bladder. A partial-bladder boost is often simulated with a full bladder so small bowel is pushed away. Pelvic nodes are added for node-positive disease. Inguinal nodes are not the bladder volume. Electrons do not reach the bladder. Six MeV fits a superficial skin cancer. Thirty-three gray in 5, with celiac and superior mesenteric nodes, is pancreatic treatment. Fifty-four gray in 3 is peripheral lung stereotactic radiotherapy. Small bowel limits the high dose. A bladder primary uses a Dmax under 65 Gy. The four-volume bladder set belongs to prostate treatment, not to this primary.

### Q15
Stem: Pancreatic chemoradiation is planned in two possible ways: a conventional course, or stereotactic treatment. Which doses and which nodes fit those two choices?
A. Both use 25 Gy in 5 fractions and inguinal nodes
B. Conventional treatment is 70 Gy in 35 fractions to retropharyngeal nodes. Stereotactic treatment adds elective pelvic nodes to 45 Gy
C. Both use 60 Gy in 30 fractions to the uninvolved mediastinum
D. Conventional treatment is commonly 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions and includes celiac and superior mesenteric nodes. Stereotactic treatment is often 33 Gy in 5 fractions to the gross target only, with no elective nodes
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: Conventional pancreas chemoradiation is commonly 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions of 1.8 Gy, with 6 MV IMRT, and motion is handled with a 4DCT or with breath hold. Regional nodes on that course are the celiac and superior mesenteric stations, not the inguinal nodes. The stereotactic alternative is often 33 Gy in 5 fractions to the gross target only, with no elective nodes. The duodenum limits both. The QUANTEC summary has no conventional duodenum row, so stomach whole-organ D100 under 45 Gy is not borrowed for it. On the five-fraction course the TG-101 duodenum row for 5 cm³ is a threshold of 18 Gy and a point maximum of 32 Gy. The 10 cm³ duodenum row at five fractions has a threshold of 12.5 Gy and no point maximum, so 32 Gy is not copied onto that blank. Twenty-five gray in 5 is short-course rectum. Seventy gray in 35 with retropharyngeal nodes is nasopharynx. Sixty gray in 30 is non-small-cell lung chemoradiation.

### Q16
Stem: A thoracic esophageal cancer is treated with combined-modality radiotherapy to 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions. The same numbers are used for long-course rectum. Which organs limit the esophageal plan?
A. Small bowel, bladder, and femoral heads, the rectal high-dose limits
B. Heart, lungs, and cord
C. Lenses and scalp
D. The contralateral kidney as the only limit, with a flank dose of 10.8 Gy in 6 fractions
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Esophagus uses a typical combined-modality dose of 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions. Those are the same numbers as long-course rectum, and the organs are different: heart, lungs, and cord. Long-course rectum is limited by small bowel, with bladder and femoral heads in the pelvic plan. Lenses and scalp are whole-brain field issues. 10.8 Gy in 6 fractions is favorable-histology Wilms flank radiotherapy, and the contralateral kidney limits that flank, not this esophageal course. The esophageal mean correlate under 34 Gy is the esophagus row if that organ is scored. It does not turn the cord into a mean-lung problem or the heart into a rectal volume.

### Q17
Stem: Extremity soft-tissue sarcoma is resected with a positive margin. No preoperative radiotherapy was given. Which postoperative dose fits, and what strip of limb stays out of the high dose?
A. About 66 Gy, with an untreated longitudinal strip of soft tissue outside the high dose
B. Stay at the preoperative 50 Gy in 25 fractions, and treat the full circumference
C. About 60 Gy, the negative-margin dose, and treat the full circumference
D. 8 Gy in 1 fraction, because a limb target is bone palliation
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Preoperative extremity dose is 50 Gy in 25 fractions. Postoperative dose is higher: about 60 Gy after a negative margin and about 66 Gy after a positive margin. An untreated longitudinal strip stays outside the high dose so lymph can still drain. Treating the full circumference produces severe lymphedema. A positive margin is not left at the preoperative 50 Gy, and 66 Gy is not given before the operation. The negative-margin postoperative dose is about 60 Gy, which is the wrong total for this positive margin. Eight gray once is bone palliation. Weight-bearing bone and the adjacent joint limit the plan. The technique is 6 MV IMRT or conformal photons along the limb.

### Q18
Stem: A small eyelid skin cancer is treated locally. The deep edge is superficial. Which beam and isodose choice fit, and which organ limits the deep edge?
A. 18 MV photons with no bolus, because the higher energy stops in the lid
B. Exiting 6 MV photons with no bolus, through the brain
C. En face 6 MeV or 9 MeV, so the 90% depth covers the deep edge and then stops, with bolus about 0.5 to 1 cm. The lens limits the deep edge
D. Total-skin electrons in six positions, with no eye shield
Answer: C
Objective: III.1
Why: A small skin cancer is en face electrons, not total-skin electron irradiation. Energy is chosen so the 90% depth covers the deep edge and then stops. Six MeV or 9 MeV fits a superficial lesion. A higher energy treats the tissue under the cancer. Bolus, often about 0.5 to 1 cm, brings dose to the skin surface. For an eyelid the limit is the lens, and the internal eye shield is coated with wax so lead backscatter does not dose the lid. Exiting megavoltage photons are the wrong tool, and 18 MV deepens buildup rather than stopping at the lid. Recognition doses for local electrons are about 50 Gy in 20 fractions and 60 Gy in 30 fractions. Total-skin electrons are recognized by six patient positions, not by assigning that local tumor dose to the whole skin.

### Q19
Stem: Locally advanced vulvar squamous cell carcinoma needs the deep inguinal nodes in the same plan as the vulva. Which technique reaches those nodes, and which gross-disease dose is in the recognition range?
A. A 6 MeV groin field to 45 Gy only
B. Opposed lateral 6 MV photons to 63 Gy in 28 fractions
C. Para-aortic 6 MV photons to 20 Gy in 10 fractions
D. 6 MV IMRT, with gross disease about 60 to 70 Gy
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: Locally advanced vulvar treatment is 6 MV IMRT with concurrent cisplatin. One plan covers the vulva, the inguinal-femoral nodes, and the pelvic nodes when the groins or the primary require them. Elective nodal dose is about 45 to 50.4 Gy. Gross primary or gross nodal disease is about 60 to 70 Gy. Deep inguinal nodes lie centimeters under the skin, so a 6 MeV groin field underdoses them. A perineal photon field matched to electron groins leaves a junction in the groin. Superficial groin nodes still need buildup. Sixty-three gray in 28 is early glottic radiotherapy. Twenty gray in 10 to the para-aortic nodes is stage I seminoma. Femoral heads, small bowel, rectum, bladder, and perineal skin limit the plan.

### Q20
Stem: Squamous carcinoma of the lower third of the vagina is treated with external beam of 45 Gy in 25 fractions, then brachytherapy. Which nodes are in the external-beam volume?
A. Pelvic nodes only
B. Pelvic nodes and inguinal nodes
C. Retropharyngeal nodes and level V
D. Celiac nodes and porta hepatis nodes
Answer: B
Objective: III.4
Why: External beam to the pelvis is about 45 Gy in 25 fractions. Brachytherapy then raises the vaginal primary above that external-beam dose. Upper vaginal disease drains to the pelvic nodes. Disease in the lower third also drains to the inguinal nodes, and those groins are in the volume. Retropharyngeal nodes and level V belong to a nasopharyngeal neck. Celiac nodes and porta hepatis nodes belong to a gastric volume. Rectum, bladder, and small bowel limit the plan. Femoral heads limit it when the groins are treated.

### Q21
Stem: Average-risk medulloblastoma needs craniospinal irradiation and a primary boost. Which axis dose, boost rule, and spine energy fit?
A. 23.4 Gy in 13 fractions of 1.8 Gy to the axis, a primary boost to about 54 Gy that stays off the cord, and a 6 MV spine field
B. 36 Gy in 20 fractions to the axis, with the boost drawn down the whole cord, at 18 MV
C. 30 Gy in 10 fractions to the whole brain and a matching spine field
D. 25 Gy in 10 fractions to the brain, with the lens block covering the cribriform plate
Answer: A
Objective: III.2
Why: Average-risk medulloblastoma is 23.4 Gy to the axis in 13 fractions of 1.8 Gy, and 23.4/13 = 1.8. High-risk disease is 36 Gy in 20 fractions of 1.8 Gy. In either risk group the primary is boosted to about 54 Gy, and that boost stays off the cord so the cord remains at the axis dose. Spine energy is 6 MV. A higher energy increases exit dose through thyroid, heart, bowel, and kidney, and the kidneys limit that exit. Cranial fields cover the cribriform plate. A lens block that clips the cribriform plate is a geographic miss. Thirty gray in 10 is palliative whole-brain treatment, not craniospinal irradiation. Twenty-five gray in 10 is prophylactic cranial irradiation and has no spine field. The inferior spine border covers the thecal sac. The junction is feathered.

### Q22
Stem: An apical non-small-cell lung cancer is treated to 60 Gy in 30 fractions. The plan already accounts for lung, esophagus, heart, and cord. Which additional structure can the apical field cross, and where does that structure sit?
A. The spinal cord, still the only structure inside the supraclavicular fossa
B. Level IA nodes, between the anterior digastric muscles
C. The brachial plexus, between the anterior and middle scalene muscles, above the first rib
D. Inguinal nodes, below the inguinal ligament
Answer: C
Objective: III.4
Why: An apical tumor adds the brachial plexus. The plexus leaves the foramina from C5 through T1, passes between the anterior and middle scalene muscles, and crosses above the first rib with the subclavian artery. On a supraclavicular slice it is the soft-tissue bundle in that gap, posterior to the clavicle, above the lung apex. It has already left the canal, so it is not the cord. It is nerve, not a node. Level IA is midline between the anterior digastric muscles. Inguinal nodes are in the groin and are not an apical lung volume. This 60 Gy in 30 fraction course does not take a stereotactic plexus cell from a 1-fraction or 5-fraction table.

### Q23
Stem: A nonfunctioning pituitary adenoma sits against the optic chiasm. Which technique and dose fit?
A. A single fraction of 16 to 18 Gy, the spine stereotactic tumor dose
B. 20 Gy in 10 fractions to a mantle field
C. 70 Gy in 35 fractions with elective neck nodes
D. Fractionated 6 MV photons to 45 to 50.4 Gy at 1.8 Gy per fraction
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: A nonfunctioning adenoma, and any adenoma sitting against the optic chiasm, is fractionated 6 MV photons to 45 to 50.4 Gy at 1.8 Gy per fraction. The chiasm is the organ that makes that choice. Single-fraction radiosurgery is the alternative only when a gap separates the target from the chiasm. Functioning adenomas are often taken toward 50.4 to 54 Gy when the chiasm allows, which is a different adenoma and a different gap. Sixteen to 18 Gy is a recognizable single-fraction spine stereotactic tumor dose. Twenty gray in 10 can be favorable early Hodgkin involved-site radiotherapy or stage I seminoma, not a mantle and not a sellar adenoma. Seventy gray in 35 is gross head-and-neck disease.

### Q24
Stem: One child has favorable-histology Wilms tumor and needs flank radiotherapy. Another has high-risk neuroblastoma after chemotherapy and resection, with no gross residual. Which doses fit those two cases, in that order?
A. 21.6 Gy in 12 fractions, then 10.8 Gy in 6 fractions
B. 10.8 Gy in 6 fractions of 1.8 Gy, then 21.6 Gy in 12 fractions of 1.8 Gy
C. 10.5 Gy in 7 fractions for both
D. 36 Gy to the Wilms flank, then 54 Gy to the neuroblastoma
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: Favorable-histology Wilms flank radiotherapy is 10.8 Gy in 6 fractions of 1.8 Gy, and 10.8/6 = 1.8. High-risk neuroblastoma to the primary site is 21.6 Gy in 12 fractions of 1.8 Gy, and 21.6/12 = 1.8. Those doses are not interchangeable. Bulky gross residual in favorable Wilms histology is boosted to a total of 21.6 Gy, so 21.6 Gy is not the standard flank dose. Gross residual neuroblastoma is boosted to 36 Gy. Diffuse peritoneal spill changes Wilms to the whole abdomen, 10.5 Gy in 7 fractions of 1.5 Gy, and 10.5/7 = 1.5. That is not the flank dose and not the neuroblastoma dose. Fifty-four gray in 30 is fractionated grade 1 meningioma. Age under one year is a separate Wilms reduction, and that infant dose is not 10.8 Gy copied forward. The flank technique is anterior-posterior 6 MV photons.

### Q25
Stem: A right-sided Wilms flank field is anterior-posterior 6 MV photons. How far must the medial border go, and which kidney limits the field?
A. Cross midline far enough to cover the full width of the vertebral bodies. The contralateral kidney limits the field
B. Stop at midline so only the right half of each vertebral body is treated. The right kidney is the limit
C. Include both inguinal regions and stop at the pubic symphysis
D. Match a cranial field at the cribriform plate and block the contralateral kidney out of the flank entirely, including the vertebral body on that side
Answer: A
Objective: III.4
Why: The flank field crosses the midline far enough to cover the full width of the vertebral bodies, so one side of the growth plate is not treated alone. The contralateral kidney limits that field. Stopping at midline treats one side of the vertebral body and is the growth asymmetry this border is written to avoid. Inguinal coverage is an anal, vulvar, or lower-vaginal decision. The cribriform plate is the anterior cranial border of craniospinal irradiation, not the medial border of a flank field. The dose for favorable histology on this flank is 10.8 Gy in 6 fractions, and the field description does not change that total.

### Q26
Stem: Locally advanced cervix cancer is treated with external beam and brachytherapy. Which description is the full recognition course?
A. 45 Gy external beam is the entire treatment
B. 60 Gy in 30 fractions down the craniospinal cord
C. About 45 Gy external beam to the pelvis, then brachytherapy that brings the high-risk CTV into the mid-to-high 80s Gy EQD2. One worked combination is 45 Gy in 25 fractions plus four HDR fractions of 7 Gy
D. 25 Gy in 5 fractions to the pelvis and the inguinal nodes
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Typical cervix external beam is 45 Gy to the pelvis, and 45 Gy in 25 fractions is the worked external course. Brachytherapy then brings the high-risk CTV into the mid-to-high 80s Gy EQD2, using an alpha/beta of 10. One worked combination is 45 Gy in 25 fractions plus four HDR fractions of 7 Gy.

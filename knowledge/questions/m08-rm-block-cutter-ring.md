# Module 8. Geometric Characteristics (Magnification, Minification)

Original items. Gaps identified in the RM Review port: block-cutter geometry, block shifts through magnification, magnification ring, distance scaling.

## Block-Cutter Geometry and Block Shifts

### Q1
Stem: When a simulator film is loaded into a divergent hot-wire block cutter, the film must be placed at:
A. The same source-to-film distance at which it was exposed, with the styrofoam at the source-to-tray distance
B. The isocenter distance, with the styrofoam at the film distance
C. Any distance, because the cutter rescales automatically
D. The tray distance, so that the drawn aperture is cut at full size
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: The cutter reproduces the beam geometry: the wire pivot stands in for the source, the film sits at its exposure distance, and the foam sits where the block will sit in the head, so the drawn aperture is reduced by the tray-to-film ratio and the block edge is focused toward the source. Putting the film at the tray distance would cut a block as large as the film drawing, far too big for the tray. Nothing rescales automatically; the distances are the scaling.

### Q2
Stem: A physician marks a block edge to be moved 2.8 cm on a film taken at 140 cm from the source. The block tray is 65 cm from the source. How far must the block be moved on the tray?
A. 2.8 cm
B. 6.0 cm
C. 1.3 cm
D. 2.0 cm
Answer: C
Objective: I.6
Why: Shift at the tray equals shift on film times tray distance over film distance: 2.8 times 65 divided by 140 equals 1.3 cm. Check: 1.3 times 140 divided by 65 returns 2.8 cm. 2.8 cm applies no scaling. 6.0 cm inverts the ratio. 2.0 cm is the shift at the 100 cm isocenter plane, not at the tray.

### Q3
Stem: A block edge is moved 2.1 cm on a port film exposed at 150 cm from the source. The isocenter is at 100 cm. How far does the field edge move in the patient at the isocenter plane?
A. 1.4 cm
B. 2.1 cm
C. 3.2 cm
D. 0.7 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: The isocenter plane is nearer the source than the film, so the shift is reduced by 100 over 150: 2.1 times 100 divided by 150 equals 1.4 cm. Check: 1.4 times 1.5 returns 2.1 cm. 2.1 cm ignores the magnification. 3.2 cm multiplies by 150 over 100. 0.7 cm is the difference between the film and patient values, not a projection.

### Q4
Stem: A block is positioned 5 mm off its intended place in a tray 65 cm from the source. The error at the isocenter, 100 cm from the source, is approximately:
A. 3.3 mm
B. 5 mm
C. 7.7 mm
D. 10 mm
Answer: C
Objective: I.6
Why: Anything at the tray is magnified toward the patient by 100 over 65, so 5 times 100 divided by 65 equals 7.7 mm. Check: 7.7 times 0.65 returns 5.0 mm. 3.3 mm shrinks the error with 65 over 100, the wrong direction. 5 mm assumes no divergence. 10 mm doubles rather than scales. This is why block and MLC tolerances are stated at isocenter.

## Magnification Ring and Distance Scaling

### Q1
Stem: A ring of known 3.0 cm diameter is taped at the level of the target. On the film it measures 4.2 cm. A lymph node in the same plane measures 5.6 cm on the film. What is the true node length?
A. 7.8 cm
B. 4.0 cm
C. 4.2 cm
D. 5.6 cm
Answer: B
Objective: I.6
Why: Magnification equals 4.2 divided by 3.0, which is 1.40. True length equals 5.6 divided by 1.40, which is 4.0 cm. Check: 4.0 times 1.40 returns 5.6 cm. 7.8 cm multiplies the film measurement by the magnification instead of dividing. 4.2 cm is the ring's image size. 5.6 cm ignores magnification entirely.

### Q2
Stem: The main reason a planner places a marker of known size on the patient before a localization film is to:
A. Determine the magnification of that plane without needing the source distances
B. Measure the dose rate at the skin
C. Mark the isocenter for the laser alignment
D. Correct the film for inverse-square fall-off
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: The ratio of the marker's projected size to its true size is the magnification of the plane the marker lies in, and every length in that plane divides by the same ratio; this replaces the distance ratio when depths or geometry are unknown. A marker does not measure dose or correct inverse square, and laser alignment uses skin marks, not a magnification ring.

### Q3
Stem: A 10 by 10 cm field is set at 100 cm SSD. What is the field size at a depth of 8 cm?
A. 10.8 by 10.8 cm
B. 9.3 by 9.3 cm
C. 18 by 18 cm
D. 10 by 10 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: The field diverges in proportion to distance from the source: 10 times (100 plus 8) divided by 100 equals 10.8 cm. Check: 10.8 times 100 divided by 108 returns 10.0 cm. 9.3 cm uses 100 over 108, the wrong direction. 18 cm multiplies by the depth instead of by the ratio. 10 cm ignores divergence, which is only true at the surface.

### Q4
Stem: A block aperture measures 6.5 cm across on a tray located 65 cm from the source. The field it produces at the 100 cm isocenter plane is:
A. 4.2 cm
B. 6.5 cm
C. 10.0 cm
D. 13.0 cm
Answer: C
Objective: I.6
Why: Length at isocenter equals tray length times 100 over 65: 6.5 times 100 divided by 65 equals 10.0 cm. Check: 10.0 times 0.65 returns 6.5 cm. 4.2 cm uses 65 over 100 and shrinks the field. 6.5 cm assumes no divergence. 13.0 cm doubles instead of scaling by the distance ratio.

# Module 44 — Timed Exam: Physics and Localization

Original items for this bank. Not retired MDCB questions and not a commercial quiz.

## Timed Exam Physics and Localization

### Q1
Stem: A nucleus emits an alpha particle. Which change in the nucleus is correct?
A. The mass number falls by 4 and the atomic number falls by 2
B. The mass number is unchanged and the atomic number rises by 1
C. The mass number falls by 2 and the atomic number is unchanged
D. The mass number is unchanged and the atomic number falls by 1
Answer: A
Objective: I.1
Why: An alpha particle is a helium nucleus, two protons and two neutrons, so the mass number falls by 4 and the atomic number falls by 2. Beta-minus decay leaves the mass number unchanged and raises the atomic number by 1. Losing two mass units, or lowering the atomic number by 1 with no change in mass number, is a different decay.

### Q2
Stem: A proton-rich nucleus emits a positron, and that positron later annihilates. Which description fits this decay?
A. The atomic number rises by 1, and the beta spectrum is a single energy
B. The atomic number falls by 1, and annihilation produces two 0.511 MeV photons
C. The mass number falls by 4, and one 1.022 MeV photon leaves the nucleus
D. The atomic number is unchanged, because the positron was an orbital electron
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: Beta-plus decay converts a proton to a neutron, so the atomic number falls by 1 and the mass number stays the same. The positron then annihilates with an electron and produces two 0.511 MeV photons in opposite directions. Beta-minus raises the atomic number. An alpha particle changes the mass number by 4. An orbital electron ejected with no change in atomic number is internal conversion, not positron emission.

### Q3
Stem: In photon mode, a linac produces the treatment beam when the accelerated electrons do which of the following?
A. Pass through a scattering foil after the target has been removed
B. Strike a high-Z transmission target and generate bremsstrahlung
C. Are stored in the head as a sealed cobalt source
D. Leave a heated filament and stop in a reflection anode at diagnostic voltage
Answer: B
Objective: I.2
Why: Linac photons are bremsstrahlung from electrons stopped in a transmission target. A scattering foil, with the target removed, is the electron-mode path. Cobalt-60 is a radioactive gamma source, not electrons accelerated in a waveguide. A heated filament and a reflection anode describe an x-ray tube, not the linac photon beam.

### Q4
Stem: How is a clinical proton treatment beam produced, as distinct from linac photons?
A. Electrons hit tungsten, and the patient is treated with that bremsstrahlung
B. Protons boil off a filament by thermionic emission
C. Protons are accelerated and delivered as the charged-particle beam, so the range follows the energy
D. A sealed source with a 5.26-year half-life is timed out of a shielded head
Answer: C
Objective: I.2
Why: A proton machine accelerates protons and sends those protons into the patient. Their range is set by energy. Linac photons are bremsstrahlung from electrons on a target. Thermionic emission is how an x-ray tube frees electrons. A 5.26-year sealed source is cobalt-60 teletherapy.

### Q5
Stem: Which beam deposits dose with a useful Bragg peak at the end of a finite range?
A. A clinical proton beam
B. A 6 MV photon beam in water
C. A clinical electron beam, because multiple scattering leaves a sharp peak
D. Cobalt-60 gamma rays, because the spectrum is two lines
Answer: A
Objective: I.3
Why: Protons slow down and the stopping power rises sharply near the end of the range. That is the Bragg peak, and there is essentially no dose beyond the range. Megavoltage photons and cobalt gamma rays are attenuated without a finite range. Clinical electrons scatter enough that a sharp Bragg peak is not useful, even though they do have a practical range.

### Q6
Stem: A clean 18 MV photon beam has a deeper dose maximum than a clean 6 MV beam. A tray is then placed against the skin in the 18 MV beam. What should be expected at the surface?
A. Skin dose falls, because a higher nominal energy always increases skin sparing
B. Skin dose is unchanged, because a tray does not add charged particles
C. The beam becomes an electron beam whose practical range is 18 cm
D. Skin dose can rise, because the tray adds electron contamination
Answer: D
Objective: I.3
Why: On a clean megavoltage photon beam, higher energy deepens the dose maximum and lowers surface dose. A tray or wedge against the skin puts contaminant electrons on the surface, so skin dose can rise even though the nominal energy is high. The tray does not convert the beam into an electron treatment, and 18 cm is not the practical range of an 18 MV photon beam.

### Q7
Stem: In soft tissue at ordinary linac photon energies, which interaction accounts for most of the absorbed dose?
A. Photoelectric absorption
B. Compton scatter
C. Coherent scatter
D. Pair production as the majority process
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: Compton scatter dominates in soft tissue through the therapy megavoltage range, so dose follows electron density. Photoelectric absorption dominates at low energy and in high-Z material. Coherent scatter does not deposit the therapeutic dose. Pair production is allowed above its energy threshold and is still a minority of interactions in tissue at ordinary linac energies.

### Q8
Stem: A 6 MV beam is energetic enough for pair production. What does crossing that energy threshold decide?
A. Pair production is still forbidden until the nominal energy is above 10 MV
B. Every photon in the beam becomes one 1.022 MeV photon
C. Pair production may occur, while Compton scatter remains the dominant process in soft tissue
D. Photoelectric absorption becomes the only interaction left in tissue
Answer: C
Objective: I.4
Why: The nuclear pair-production threshold is 1.022 MeV, so the process is permitted in a 6 MV beam. Permission is not dominance. In soft tissue at 6 to 18 MV, Compton scatter still accounts for most interactions. The beam is a bremsstrahlung spectrum, not a beam of single 1.022 MeV photons, and photoelectric absorption is not the megavoltage process in tissue.

### Q9
Stem: Bone and iodinated contrast are bright on a kilovoltage CT image. Which interaction is the main reason those high-Z materials attenuate that beam so strongly?
A. Photoelectric absorption
B. Compton scatter, because its probability per gram rises as the cube of the atomic number
C. Pair production, which is available below 100 keV
D. Coherent scatter, which deposits the entire photon energy locally
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: At CT energies the photoelectric effect is strong in bone and iodine, which is why those materials look bright. Compton scatter, per gram, depends on electron density rather than on a steep power of atomic number. Pair production cannot occur below 1.022 MeV. Coherent scatter changes direction and does not hand the photon energy to the medium.

### Q10
Stem: The linac is switched from photons to electrons. Which machine state matches electron treatment?
A. Target in, flattening filter in, and scattering foil out
B. A cobalt source driven out, with the exposure metered by source decay
C. Target left in, and the flattening filter replaced by a physical wedge
D. Target out, flattening filter out, and scattering foil in
Answer: D
Objective: I.5
Why: Electron mode removes the transmission target and the flattening filter and spreads the pencil beam with a scattering foil. Leaving the target or the filter in the beam keeps photon hardware in place. A cobalt unit is a gamma source, not an electron applicator. A physical wedge tilts a photon profile; it does not replace the electron foil.

### Q11
Stem: Which description belongs to cobalt-60 teletherapy rather than to a linac?
A. Output is counted in monitor units, and the head has no radioactive source
B. Output is timed from a source that emits 1.17 and 1.33 MeV photons, and the source is about 1 to 2 cm across
C. The dose maximum is at the skin, and beam quality is stated as a filter thickness
D. A single iridium source steps through catheters
Answer: B
Objective: I.5
Why: Cobalt-60 teletherapy emits 1.17 and 1.33 MeV gammas, uses a timer, and has a source about 1 to 2 cm in diameter, so the penumbra is wider than a linac source spot. A linac meters monitor units and has no teletherapy source to decay. Skin dose maximum and a filter thickness describe kilovoltage therapy. A stepping iridium source is a high-dose-rate afterloader.

### Q12
Stem: On an MR-linac the magnetic field is on during treatment, and secondary electrons at a tissue-air interface can curve back into tissue. What is the practical consequence?
A. Skin dose falls, because the field stops every electron at the surface
B. The photon beam is replaced by a proton Bragg peak
C. Skin dose can rise relative to the same photon beam with no magnetic field
D. The effect removes the need for an electron-density data set
Answer: C
Objective: I.5
Why: The Lorentz force on secondary electrons produces the electron-return effect, which can increase skin dose at a tissue-air interface compared with a conventional linac. The treatment beam is still a linac photon beam, and the dose calculation still needs density. The field does not turn the beam into protons.

### Q13
Stem: A localization film sits beyond the patient, farther from the source than the marker being imaged. The marker's image is longer than the marker. That length ratio is:
A. Geometric magnification greater than 1
B. An inverse-square increase in dose rate at the film
C. A later display reduction, which is always less than 1
D. A statement of beam quality
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Geometric magnification is a length ratio from similar triangles. It is greater than 1 when the image receptor is farther from the source than the object. Inverse square changes dose rate, not the length of the image. A print can be reduced afterward; that reduction is not the geometry of the film. Beam quality is not a size ratio.

### Q14
Stem: A planner has a magnified film measurement and needs the true object size. Which step recovers it?
A. Multiply the image length by the magnification
B. Multiply the image length by the inverse-square factor for the same distances
C. Leave the image length unchanged, because film magnification is zero at 100 cm SSD
D. Divide the image length by the magnification
Answer: D
Objective: I.6
Why: True size is the image length divided by the magnification. Multiplying enlarges the image a second time. Inverse square scales intensity, not object length. Magnification is 1 when the film is in the object plane; it is not zero, and SSD alone does not put the film at the object.

### Q15
Stem: Which half-life pair matches the high-dose-rate iridium source and the cesium source used for older gynecologic tubes?
A. Iridium-192 about 30 years, and cesium-137 about 73.8 days
B. Iridium-192 about 73.8 days, and cesium-137 about 30 years
C. Iridium-192 about 5.26 years, and cesium-137 about 8 days
D. Iridium-192 about 110 minutes, and cesium-137 about 6 hours
Answer: B
Objective: I.7
Why: Iridium-192 is recalled at 73.8 days and cesium-137 at 30 years. Swapping them assigns the high-dose-rate source a decades-long half-life. 5.26 years is cobalt-60 and 8 days is iodine-131. 110 minutes is fluorine-18 and 6 hours is technetium-99m.

### Q16
Stem: A strontium-90 eye applicator is stored until its yttrium-90 daughter is in equilibrium. Why is that equilibrium secular rather than transient?
A. The daughter half-life is longer than the parent half-life, so the activities never lock
B. The two half-lives lie within about a factor of ten, as with molybdenum-99 and technetium-99m
C. The parent half-life is vastly longer than the daughter half-life, and the daughter activity comes to equal the parent activity
D. Eluting the applicator restarts the strontium half-life
Answer: C
Objective: I.7
Why: Strontium-90, about 28.8 years, vastly outlives yttrium-90, about 64 hours. After several daughter half-lives the daughter activity equals the parent activity, which is secular equilibrium. Molybdenum-99 and technetium-99m are closer in half-life and are the transient pair. If the daughter lived longer than the parent, equilibrium of this kind would not form. Elution separates atoms; it does not change a decay constant.

### Q17
Stem: A brain target is drawn on MRI, and the photon dose is calculated afterward. Which data set supplies the electron density?
A. The planning CT, through the commissioned density curve
B. The MRI, after the gray values are renamed as Hounsfield units
C. The PET uptake values
D. The surface-camera point cloud
Answer: A
Objective: I.8
Why: A planning CT is a map of attenuation that the planning system converts with a measured density curve. MRI pixel values are not Hounsfield units and must not be sent through that table. PET uptake is not electron density. Surface guidance has no internal attenuation image.

### Q18
Stem: A kilovoltage cone-beam CT from the linac is proposed as the data set for a new dose calculation, using the simulator density table unchanged. What is the objection?
A. The cone-beam contains no patient anatomy
B. A kilovoltage image cannot show bone
C. The cone-beam is a non-ionizing surface trace and has no voxels
D. Cone-beam scatter and the reconstruction make those numbers a poor match to the commissioned planning-CT density table
Answer: D
Objective: I.8
Why: A linac cone-beam is acquired to align the patient to the planning CT. Scatter, a limited field of view, and motion make its Hounsfield numbers a different quantity from the density table commissioned for the simulator. The image does show anatomy, including bone. It is not surface guidance.

### Q19
Stem: Which pair matches the Hounsfield definition used for treatment planning, rather than a digital storage limit?
A. Air is 0 and water is 1000
B. Air is -1000 and water is 0
C. Air is -1024, and that floor is the physical value of air
D. Water is 1000 because its attenuation is defined as zero
Answer: B
Objective: I.9
Why: Water is defined as 0 and air as -1000. Setting water to 1000 and air to 0 drops the comparison with water. -1024 is a common digital floor, not the physical Hounsfield value of air. Water does not have zero attenuation.

### Q20
Stem: Iodinated contrast makes the bladder bright on a pelvic planning CT. For a megavoltage photon calculation, that region should be:
A. Left at the raw number, because megavoltage dose in tissue is photoelectric
B. Left at the raw number, because the contrast has the electron density of cortical bone
C. Overridden toward tissue or water density, because the brightness is a kilovoltage photoelectric effect
D. Accepted as edited already, because the display window rewrites the stored numbers
Answer: C
Objective: I.9
Why: Iodine raises kilovoltage attenuation. At megavoltage energy Compton scatter dominates, and the bladder contents are not bone, so the raw contrast number is overridden toward tissue or water density. Window width and level change the gray display, not the stored Hounsfield numbers.

### Q21
Stem: A photon prescription states absorbed dose. Which pairing of quantity and unit is correct?
A. The gray is absorbed dose, and the roentgen is exposure rather than a tissue dose
B. The becquerel is absorbed dose, and the gray is activity
C. The roentgen is absorbed dose in tissue, and the gray is exposure in air
D. The sievert is unweighted absorbed dose, and the gray is used only for alpha particles
Answer: A
Objective: I.10
Why: Absorbed dose is energy imparted per mass, and its SI unit is the gray. The roentgen is an older unit of photon exposure in air. Activity is the becquerel. Equivalent dose, in sievert, applies a radiation weighting factor. For photons that factor is 1, but the sievert is still not the unit of exposure, and the gray is not reserved for alpha particles.

### Q22
Stem: At the entrance surface of a megavoltage photon beam in water, a planner sets absorbed dose equal to kerma. Why does that equality fail there?
A. Kerma is zero at the surface and absorbed dose is already at its maximum
B. Charged-particle equilibrium holds throughout the buildup region
C. Both quantities stay at zero until one half-life of the beam has passed
D. Kerma is highest at the surface while absorbed dose is still rising toward its maximum
Answer: D
Objective: I.10
Why: Photon fluence, and therefore kerma, is highest at the entrance surface and falls with depth. Absorbed dose is carried by secondary electrons that have not yet reached equilibrium, so dose rises through buildup to the dose maximum. Equilibrium does not hold in the buildup region. Half-life is a radioactive-decay lifetime, not a depth in a phantom.

### Q23
Stem: A vacuum bag is indexed and still holds the thorax shape from the planning CT. The patient breathes freely. What does the bag not control?
A. Respiratory motion of a lower-lobe tumor
B. The couch index of the cradle, while the vacuum holds
C. The supported external shape, while the vacuum holds
D. The requirement to name the device in the setup note
Answer: A
Objective: II.3
Why: A vacuum bag reproduces the supported shape from the planning CT when the vacuum holds, and that shape is indexed to the couch. It does not stop the diaphragm. Respiratory motion of a lower-lobe target still needs the motion strategy that was simulated. The device remains part of the setup note.

### Q24
Stem: A lung plan was simulated and gated at end-exhalation. At treatment the gate is moved to end-inspiration because the lung image looks larger. What is the localization error?
A. There is no error, because any stable respiratory phase matches the planning scan
B. The beam is enabled in a respiratory phase the plan did not use
C. The larger inspiration image proves that residual motion inside the gate is zero
D. End-inspiration is required for every gate, because that phase lasts longer than end-exhalation
Answer: B
Objective: II.3
Why: The planning scan, the target volume, and the treatment gate have to be the same respiratory state. This plan was built at end-exhalation, which is the more reproducible window used in this course. Treating at end-inspiration delivers a different anatomy. A larger lung image does not remove residual motion, and end-inspiration is not the longer, more stable default.

### Q25
Stem: A breast tangent plan was simulated with the arm on a named wing-board grip. Treatment uses a different grip, and the setup note still says only that the arm is up. What has changed?
A. Nothing, because every overhead grip is the same tangent geometry
B. Only the photon energy
C. The indexed arm position, so the tangent path is no longer the simulated one
D. The Hounsfield calibration of the breast
Answer: C
Objective: II.2
Why: The wing-board notch and the grip are part of the geometry. A different grip is a different arm position even when the note still says the arm is up, and the tangent path through lung and heart can change. Energy and the density calibration are separate from that index.

### Q26
Stem: A prone pelvic plan uses a belly board so small bowel can fall anteriorly through the opening. At treatment the patient is prone, but the opening sits caudal to the abdomen and the belly rests on the solid board. What is the setup?
A. Correct, because any prone contact with the board drops the bowel
B. Correct, because a belly board drops bowel the same way when the patient is supine
C. Improved, because supporting the belly removes the need for the planning CT
D. A different geometry, because the opening is not under the abdomen the plan expected to drop
Answer: D
Objective: II.3
Why: The belly board works when the patient is prone and the opening is under the abdomen, so small bowel can fall anteriorly. An opening that misses the abdomen leaves the belly supported, which is not the simulated drop. The device is not a supine device, and a wrong opening does not replace the planning CT.

### Q27
Stem: The external contour for a breast plan is drawn while the display is on a narrow soft-tissue window, and the anterior skin is not visible. What should happen before that contour is used?
A. Review a window wide enough to show the skin, and redraw the surface that the narrow window clipped
B. Accept the contour, because a narrow window deletes skin voxels from the stored data
C. Accept the contour, because a narrow window is the full-window review
D. Drop the external contour, because a tangent plan does not need a skin surface
Answer: A
Objective: II.4
Why: Window width and level change the gray display, not the stored Hounsfield numbers. A full-window review includes a window wide enough to show skin, so the external contour is not clipped. A clipped surface assigns air to tissue that was present and can change scatter and the tangent flash. The external contour is required.

### Q28
Stem: The simulation order says to scan the pelvis with a comfortably full bladder. The acquired images show an empty bladder. Why is this a poor planning data set for that order?
A. It is acceptable whenever the target itself appears on some slice
B. The preparation does not match the anatomy the plan and the later fractions are supposed to reproduce
C. An empty bladder changes only the Hounsfield number of cortical bone
D. An empty bladder automatically extends the scan through the rest of the pelvis
Answer: B
Objective: II.4
Why: Bladder filling is part of the simulation preparation. A scan with an empty bladder is a different arrangement of bowel and target than the full-bladder anatomy the order specified. Later fractions cannot recreate a filling state that was never scanned. Slice coverage and bone attenuation are separate checks.

### Q29
Stem: A scar wire and a setup marker are both bright on the simulation CT. Before the target is localized, the planner should:
A. Contour the wire as gross tumor and the marker as a bone island
B. Include the marker in the external contour as a horn of tissue
C. Identify each marker so the wire is not called tumor and the marker is not called bone
D. Crop the skin out of the field of view so the markers become the only surface
Answer: C
Objective: II.4
Why: Wires and markers have to be named before contouring. A scar wire is not the gross tumor, and a marker is not a bone island. Leaving a marker inside the external contour adds a false horn of tissue. Cropping the skin does not identify the markers and damages the external contour.

### Q30
Stem: The patient is resimulated and new reference markers are placed. The shift sheet from the first marker set is copied onto the new setup. What is wrong?
A. Nothing, because shifts belong to the patient and survive a new origin
B. Nothing, because new markers force the isocenter onto the skin
C. Nothing, because a shift is a beam-quality index and does not use the markers
D. The copied shifts start from a marker that is no longer the origin
Answer: D
Objective: II.7
Why: A new simulation creates a new reference origin. Shifts are the offset from that origin to the planned isocenter, so the old vector does not apply to the new markers. New markers do not force the isocenter onto the skin, and a shift is not a beam-quality index.

### Q31
Stem: Monthly imaging-to-treatment isocenter coincidence measures 1.6 mm on a linac used for SRS. TG-142 allows ≤2 mm for a non-stereotactic machine and ≤1 mm for SRS/SBRT. Which conclusion fits that test?
A. The SRS/SBRT machine test fails, and the 1.6 mm reading is still not a clinical action level for a treatment site
B. The test passes, because 1.6 mm is under 2 mm for every machine class
C. The test passes, and 1.6 mm becomes the action level for prostate, breast, and palliation
D. The SRS/SBRT allowance is ≤2 mm, so 1.6 mm passes the stereotactic test
Answer: A
Objective: II.6
Why: TG-142 monthly coincidence of the imaging and treatment isocenters is ≤2 mm for a non-stereotactic machine and ≤1 mm for SRS/SBRT. A 1.6 mm result fails the SRS/SBRT machine test and would meet only the non-stereotactic allowance. That machine test does not write the clinical action level for a site. Using 1.6 mm, or 2 mm, as every site's shift limit invents a patient threshold.

### Q32
Stem: Cone-beam alignment uses the anatomy named in the departmental policy. Filling, body shape, and the immobilization match the plan, and the rigid couch shift is inside that policy. What should the team do?
A. Hold the beam, because every nonzero shift requires a physician
B. Finish the localization the policy allows, and treat
C. Replan, because acquiring a cone-beam always replaces the dose distribution
D. Make this cone-beam the new match reference in place of the planning CT
Answer: B
Objective: II.6
Why: When the match is on the named anatomy, the preparation and devices agree with the plan, and the rigid shift is inside the departmental rule, localization is complete and the fraction can be treated. A nonzero shift inside that rule is not, by itself, a physician call. A cone-beam does not replan the dose, and the match reference remains the planning CT.

### Q33
Stem: A prostate protocol requires a soft-tissue check of bladder filling. The only image proposed is one anterior kilovoltage radiograph. What can that radiograph not provide?
A. A two-dimensional projection
B. A view in which pelvic bone may be visible
C. A volumetric assessment of bladder filling
D. An image that can be stored with the fraction
Answer: C
Objective: II.6
Why: One anterior radiograph is a projection. It can show bone and it can be stored, but it collapses the patient into one plane and does not show bladder filling as a volume. A cone-beam CT is the image that can show soft-tissue filling. A portal image of the treatment aperture is a different check.

### Q34
Stem: Today's cone-beam shows neck flexion and a shoulder position that one rigid shift cannot restore together. A deformable registration displays a warp that lines the images up. What decision fits?
A. Deliver the warp, because the couch can apply whatever the registration displays
B. Treat on the lasers, because a shape change needs no further review
C. Average the bone shift and the soft-tissue shift so both anatomies are partly matched
D. Apply only a rigid correction; a shape change that a rigid shift cannot restore is a reason to hold for the physician
Answer: D
Objective: II.6
Why: A couch can translate and, on some machines, rotate. It cannot deliver a deformable warp. Averaging a bone shift with a soft-tissue shift satisfies neither anatomy. Lasers on the marks do not repair a change in shape. Hold the beam and call the physician when a rigid shift cannot restore the simulated geometry.

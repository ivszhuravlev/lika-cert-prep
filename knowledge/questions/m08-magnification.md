# Module 8 — Geometric Characteristics (Magnification, Minification)

Original items. Magnification = source-to-image distance / source-to-object distance = image size / object size. True size is the image size divided by the magnification.

## Magnification Tutorial

### Q1
Stem: Source-to-image distance is 140 cm and source-to-object distance is 100 cm. A node measures 11.2 cm on the film. What is the true size?
A. 8.0 cm
B. 15.7 cm
C. 11.2 cm
D. 9.8 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Magnification = 140/100 = 1.40. True size = 11.2 / 1.40 = 8.0 cm. 15.7 cm multiplies 11.2 × 1.40. 11.2 cm ignores magnification. 9.8 cm is 11.2 × (140/160), which uses 160 cm in place of the 100 cm object distance.

### Q2
Stem: An implant needle is 4.0 cm long. Source-to-film distance is 160 cm and source-to-object distance is 80 cm. How long is the needle on the film?
A. 2.0 cm
B. 4.0 cm
C. 8.0 cm
D. 6.0 cm
Answer: C
Objective: I.6
Why: Magnification = 160/80 = 2.0. Image size = 4.0 × 2.0 = 8.0 cm. 2.0 cm divides by the magnification and reports the demagnified length. 4.0 cm ignores geometry. 6.0 cm adds the object length to the magnification number in the wrong units (4 + 2).

### Q3
Stem: A vessel measures 15 cm on the film and 10 cm in the patient. Source-to-film distance is 120 cm. What is the source-to-object distance?
A. 80 cm
B. 180 cm
C. 120 cm
D. 115 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Magnification = image/object = 15/10 = 1.5. Source-to-object = source-to-film / magnification = 120 / 1.5 = 80 cm. 180 cm multiplies 120 × 1.5. 120 cm ignores magnification. 115 cm subtracts the 5 cm difference of the two measured lengths from 120 cm.

### Q4
Stem: Isocenter is 100 cm from the source. The film is 135 cm from the source. Two seeds are 5.4 cm apart on the film. What is the true separation?
A. 4.0 cm
B. 7.3 cm
C. 5.4 cm
D. 4.9 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Magnification = 135/100 = 1.35. True separation = 5.4 / 1.35 = 4.0 cm. 7.3 cm multiplies 5.4 × 1.35. 5.4 cm is the magnified image. 4.9 cm uses 135/110 or a similar wrong distance ratio (5.4 / 1.10 ≈ 4.9).

### Q5
Stem: The magnification factor is given as 1.25. A scar measures 6.0 cm on the film. The true length is:
A. 4.8 cm
B. 7.5 cm
C. 6.0 cm
D. 5.0 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: True size = 6.0 / 1.25 = 4.8 cm. 7.5 cm multiplies 6.0 × 1.25, which is the image you would get from a 6.0 cm object, not the object under a 6.0 cm image. 5.0 cm divides by 1.20 instead of 1.25.

### Q6
Stem: A block opening is drawn 2.4 cm wide on a radiograph whose magnification is 1.20. The opening cut in the block, at the tray, must match the patient size. How wide should the cut be if the drawing is the magnified image of the desired opening?
A. 2.0 cm
B. 2.9 cm
C. 2.4 cm
D. 1.2 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Patient (true) size = 2.4 / 1.20 = 2.0 cm. 2.9 cm is 2.4 × 1.20, which makes the block too large. 2.4 cm treats the film as life size. 1.2 cm divides by 2 instead of by 1.20.

### Q7
Stem: Source-to-film distance stays 150 cm. The object is moved from 100 cm to 75 cm from the source. The magnification changes from:
A. 1.50 to 2.00
B. 1.50 to 1.25
C. 2.00 to 1.50
D. 1.00 to 1.50
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: At 100 cm, m = 150/100 = 1.50. At 75 cm, m = 150/75 = 2.00. Moving the object closer to the source increases magnification. 1.25 would be 150/120. The reverse change, 2.00 to 1.50, would be the object moving away from the source.

### Q8
Stem: Magnification is 1.80 and the image of a fiducial pair is 9.0 cm long. The true distance between the fiducials is:
A. 5.0 cm
B. 16.2 cm
C. 9.0 cm
D. 10.8 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: True distance = 9.0 / 1.80 = 5.0 cm. 16.2 cm multiplies 9.0 × 1.80. 10.8 cm multiplies by 1.20. The image is the enlarged one, so the patient distance is smaller than 9.0 cm.

## Magnification Concepts

### Q1
Stem: Magnification greater than 1 means:
A. The image is larger than the object
B. The image is smaller than the object
C. The object is at the film
D. Source-to-object distance is larger than source-to-film distance
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: m = image/object = source-to-film / source-to-object. For a radiograph the film is beyond the patient, so m > 1 and the image is enlarged. If the object sat on the film, m would be 1. Source-to-object larger than source-to-film would make m < 1, which is not the usual therapy-film geometry.

### Q2
Stem: Minification, as the reverse step, is used when the candidate has a magnified film measurement and needs the patient size. The operation is:
A. Divide the image size by the magnification
B. Multiply the image size by the magnification
C. Add the source-to-film distance to the image size
D. Use the image size unchanged, because minification cancels magnification
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Object = image / m. Multiplying again applies the enlargement a second time. Adding a distance in centimeters to a size is dimensionally wrong. The image size is not already life size when m is greater than 1.

### Q3
Stem: A known 2.5 cm marker projects to 4.0 cm on the film. What is the magnification?
A. 1.60
B. 0.63
C. 6.5
D. 1.50
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: m = image/object = 4.0 / 2.5 = 1.60. 0.63 inverts the ratio (2.5/4.0). 6.5 adds the two lengths. 1.50 would be 3.75/2.5, a different image length.

### Q4
Stem: An air gap is introduced so the film moves farther from the patient while the source and the patient stay fixed. Magnification:
A. Increases, because source-to-film distance increases and source-to-object distance does not
B. Decreases, because the object is now “smaller”
C. Stays 1, because the patient did not move
D. Becomes the patient thickness in centimeters
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: m = SFD/SOD. Raising SFD with SOD fixed raises m. The patient size did not change; the shadow got larger. Magnification is a ratio, not a thickness.

### Q5
Stem: A CT slice and a DRR are not handled like a piece of localization film. Why can a DRR length still be trusted when the CT pixel size is known?
A. The planning system knows the reconstruction geometry, so a DRR measurement can be reported at the isocenter plane rather than as a magnified shadow.
B. DRRs are always magnified by 1.44.
C. CT has no geometry, so every DRR measurement must be divided by 1.25.
D. Pixel size is the Hounsfield number.
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: Once the source position and the image plane are in the planning model, the system can remove the projection and report true distance. There is no universal 1.44 or 1.25 factor. Pixel size is millimeters, not HU.

## Magnification Quiz

### Q1
Stem: Source-to-object distance is 90 cm and source-to-film distance is 126 cm. A node measures 2.1 cm on the film. The true size is:
A. 1.5 cm
B. 2.9 cm
C. 2.1 cm
D. 1.2 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: m = 126/90 = 1.40. True size = 2.1 / 1.40 = 1.5 cm. 2.9 cm multiplies 2.1 × 1.40. 1.2 cm divides by 1.75 (using 126/72 or a similar wrong ratio). The film measurement is not the patient size.

### Q2
Stem: Source-to-image distance is 110 cm and source-to-object distance is 80 cm. An applicator opening measures 8.8 cm on the film. The true opening is:
A. 6.4 cm
B. 12.1 cm
C. 8.8 cm
D. 8.0 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: m = 110/80 = 1.375. True opening = 8.8 / 1.375 = 6.4 cm. Check: 6.4 × 1.375 = 8.8. 12.1 cm multiplies 8.8 × 1.375. 8.0 cm divides by 1.10 only, using the SSD-like 110 and dropping the 80 cm object distance.

### Q3
Stem: Two setups share a source-to-film distance of 140 cm. Setup A has the object at 100 cm. Setup B has the object at 70 cm. Which statement is correct?
A. Setup B has the larger magnification (2.0 versus 1.4).
B. Setup A has the larger magnification because the object is farther from the source.
C. The magnifications are equal because the film did not move.
D. Both magnifications are less than 1.
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: mA = 140/100 = 1.4. mB = 140/70 = 2.0. The closer object throws the larger shadow. Equal film distance does not mean equal magnification, and both values are greater than 1.

### Q4
Stem: A candidate multiplies a 5.0 cm film measurement by a magnification of 1.50 and writes 7.5 cm as the tumor size. The tumor size is actually:
A. 3.3 cm
B. 7.5 cm
C. 5.0 cm
D. 1.5 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: The film is already enlarged, so the patient size is 5.0 / 1.50 = 3.33 cm, which is 3.3 cm. 7.5 cm applies the magnification in the wrong direction. 5.0 cm would be correct only if m were 1. 1.5 cm is the factor, not a size.

### Q5
Stem: Similar triangles are the reason magnification equals SFD/SOD. If the film is twice as far from the source as the object is, a 3.0 cm object projects to:
A. 6.0 cm
B. 1.5 cm
C. 3.0 cm
D. 9.0 cm
Answer: A
Objective: I.6
Why: m = 2, so image = 3.0 × 2 = 6.0 cm. 1.5 cm divides by 2. 9.0 cm multiplies by 3. The image of an object halfway from source to film is twice the object, not the object itself.

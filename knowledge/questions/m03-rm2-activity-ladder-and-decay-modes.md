# Module 3. Radioactivity

Original items. Order-of-magnitude source activities, alpha versus beta bone agents, and which side of the stability line gives which decay mode.

## Activity Magnitude Ladder and Decay Mode Rules

### Q1
Stem: Rank these sources from largest to smallest activity: a new HDR Ir-192 source, a Co-60 teletherapy source, a single I-125 prostate seed, and a Cs-137 tandem-and-ovoid LDR load.
A. HDR Ir-192, Co-60 teletherapy, Cs-137 LDR load, I-125 seed
B. HDR Ir-192, Cs-137 LDR load, Co-60 teletherapy, I-125 seed
C. Co-60 teletherapy, Cs-137 LDR load, HDR Ir-192, I-125 seed
D. Co-60 teletherapy, HDR Ir-192, Cs-137 LDR load, I-125 seed
Answer: D
Objective: I.7
Why: A Co-60 teletherapy head holds on the order of 10 kCi, an HDR source is about 10 Ci, an LDR cesium load is about 100 to 200 mCi, and one I-125 seed is a few tenths of a millicurie. Each step is roughly a factor of a thousand, a hundred, and a few hundred respectively. Option A puts the 10 Ci HDR source above the kilocurie teletherapy source. Options B and C put the millicurie cesium load above a source that is one or more orders of magnitude larger.

### Q2
Stem: Which activity is closest to a single newly installed HDR Ir-192 source?
A. 10 mCi
B. 10 Ci
C. 6 kCi
D. 0.4 mCi
Answer: B
Objective: I.7
Why: HDR afterloaders are loaded with about 10 Ci of Ir-192 and the source is exchanged at roughly 3 to 4 Ci after about three months. 6 kCi is the total Gamma Knife cobalt load spread across about two hundred small sources. 10 mCi is in the range of a diagnostic FDG injection. 0.4 mCi is a single I-125 permanent seed.

### Q3
Stem: Which pair lists a diagnostic FDG injection and a thyroid remnant ablation dose of I-131 in the correct order of magnitude?
A. FDG about 15 mCi; I-131 ablation about 100 to 150 mCi
B. FDG about 15 µCi; I-131 ablation about 10 mCi
C. FDG about 150 mCi; I-131 ablation about 15 mCi
D. FDG about 1.5 Ci; I-131 ablation about 10 Ci
Answer: A
Objective: I.7
Why: A typical FDG PET dose is 10 to 15 mCi (370 to 555 MBq), and a remnant ablation dose of I-131 is about 100 to 150 mCi, with hyperthyroidism treatments at 10 to 30 mCi. Option B is a thousand times too low for FDG. Option C swaps the two. Option D is in the range of brachytherapy and teletherapy sources, not injected radiopharmaceuticals.

### Q4
Stem: A semiannual leak test of a Cs-137 tube returns removable activity exactly at the regulatory limit. The 10 µCi check source for the same counter is also on the bench. Approximately how do their activities compare?
A. The check source is about ten times the leak-test limit
B. The check source and the limit are about the same, near 10 µCi
C. The check source is about two thousand times the leak-test limit
D. The leak-test limit is larger, because it is quoted in becquerels
Answer: C
Objective: I.10
Why: The sealed-source leak-test limit is 0.005 µCi (185 Bq, about 11,000 dpm), and 10 µCi divided by 0.005 µCi is 2,000, so a check source is three orders of magnitude above anything a wipe should show. Check: 0.005 × 2,000 = 10. A ten-fold ratio would make a check source barely above background. The two are not the same, and quoting the limit as 185 Bq does not make it larger; 10 µCi is 370,000 Bq.

### Q5
Stem: Ra-223 and Sr-89 are both used for painful osseous metastases. Which statement correctly contrasts them?
A. Both are beta emitters; Ra-223 is preferred only because of its shorter half-life
B. Ra-223 is an alpha emitter given in microcurie amounts with a sub-millimeter range; Sr-89 is a beta emitter given in millicurie amounts with a range of millimeters
C. Ra-223 is a gamma emitter used for imaging while Sr-89 delivers the therapeutic dose
D. Sr-89 is an alpha emitter and Ra-223 is a beta emitter, which is why Sr-89 spares more marrow
Answer: B
Objective: I.7
Why: Ra-223 (11.4 days) decays by alpha emission; the alpha range in tissue is under 100 µm, so about 50 kBq per kg (roughly 100 µCi total) is enough and marrow is spared. Sr-89 (50.5 days) is a pure beta emitter given at about 4 mCi, with a millimeter-scale range and more marrow suppression; Sm-153 behaves similarly but adds an imageable gamma. Ra-223 is also the one with a survival benefit in prostate cancer. Options A, C and D each assign the wrong particle to at least one nuclide.

### Q6
Stem: A nuclide sits below the line of stability on a plot of neutron number against proton number, meaning it has too many neutrons. Which decay mode restores stability?
A. Positron emission, which lowers Z by one
B. Electron capture, which lowers Z by one
C. Alpha emission, which lowers A by four
D. Beta-minus emission, which raises Z by one
Answer: D
Objective: I.10
Why: A neutron-rich nucleus converts a neutron into a proton and ejects a beta-minus particle and an antineutrino, raising Z by one at constant A; reactor-activated nuclides such as Co-60, Ir-192 and I-131 all behave this way. Positron emission and electron capture are the proton-rich routes and lower Z, which would move the nucleus further from the line. Alpha emission is the route for very heavy nuclei and does not depend on the neutron excess.

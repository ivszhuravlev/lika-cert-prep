# Module 24 — Special treatment procedures

Original practice items for CMD preparation. Not retired MDCB items and not a commercial quiz.

## Total Body Irradiation (TBI)

### Q1
Stem: A total-body photon plan is delivered at about 4 m source-to-skin distance with 6 MV opposed beams. The physician wants the skin and the superficial tissues to receive a therapeutic dose because the disease involves those tissues. The open 6 MV beam at that distance still has skin sparing. Which device is added, and what does it do?
A. A beam spoiler, a low-Z sheet placed near the patient, to raise superficial dose by adding scatter and buildup
B. A steel compensator on the gantry head, to filter the beam into a higher energy and increase skin sparing
C. A lung block of zero transmission, to raise the skin dose in the thorax
D. A range shifter in the proton nozzle, because total-body irradiation is a proton technique
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. At large distance a megavoltage beam is still built up over the first centimeters, so the skin can be underdosed relative to the midline prescription. A plastic spoiler close to the patient puts tissue-equivalent material in the beam so that electrons and scatter reach the skin. That is a different job from a missing-tissue compensator, which fills in the neck or legs so the midline dose stays even, and different again from a partial-transmission lung block, which lowers lung dose. Raising the energy would increase skin sparing, which is the wrong direction. This technique is a photon technique.

### Q2
Stem: Midline dose is prescribed to 12 Gy. Because lung is less dense than muscle, the unblocked lung would receive 14 Gy. The protocol limit for lung is 10 Gy. What transmission should a lung block provide, as a fraction of the unblocked lung dose?
A. 10/12 = 0.83
B. 10/14 = 0.71
C. 14/10 = 1.40
D. 12/14 = 0.86
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. The block sits in the beam that would otherwise deliver 14 Gy to the lung. Transmission T multiplies that dose: 14 × T = 10, so T = 10/14 = 0.71. About 71% transmission, not a thick shielding block. Using 12 Gy in the denominator (A) answers a different question: the ratio of the lung limit to the midline prescription, which ignores the extra lung dose from low density. C inverts the ratio and would increase the lung dose. D is the midline dose divided by the unblocked lung dose, which is not the transmission that lands on 10 Gy.

### Q3
Stem: During total-body irradiation the neck and ankles would receive a higher dose than the mid-pelvis if the patient is treated as an uncompensated slab, because they are thinner. Rice bags or tissue-equivalent compensators are placed around the neck. What is their job, and how is it different from the spoiler?
A. They add missing tissue so the dose through thin anatomy is closer to the dose through thick anatomy; the spoiler's job is skin dose, not missing tissue
B. They are the spoiler, only renamed; both exist to harden 6 MV into 18 MV
C. They shield the lungs to 10 Gy and do not affect the neck dose
D. They are placed at the gantry head to flatten a 40 by 40 cm field at isocenter
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. Dose across a thin section is higher than dose across a thick section for the same entrance fluence, because there is less attenuation and the exit and entrance peaks sit closer together. Bolus-equivalent material around the neck increases the path length and pulls the neck dose down toward the prescription. The spoiler is a sheet in front of the whole beam to restore surface dose. Lung blocks lower lung dose. None of these devices is a linac flattening filter.

### Q4
Stem: A myeloablative total-body course is 12 Gy in 6 fractions of 2 Gy, given twice on each of three days. The interval between the morning and afternoon fractions is 4 hours. Why is that interval a problem for normal tissue?
A. It is not; sublethal damage repair in lung is complete in under an hour
B. Repair of sublethal damage is not complete in 4 hours; a same-day pair needs an interval of about 6 hours or more so the second fraction is not delivered on unrepaired injury
C. The 4 hour gap makes the BED lower than a single 12 Gy fraction, so the lung is safer than with a 6 hour gap
D. Twice-daily fractionation is used only for skin electron therapy, not for total-body photons
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. The biologic reason to split 12 Gy into fractions is repair in late-responding tissue, especially lung. Sublethal damage repair is largely complete by about 6 hours and is not complete at 4 hours. A short gap makes the pair behave more like a larger single dose and raises the effective lung injury. A single 12 Gy fraction would be harsher, not safer, than a properly spaced fractionated course; shortening the gap moves the treatment toward that harsher result. Twice-daily photons are a standard total-body schedule when the gap is respected.

### Q5
Stem: In vivo diodes are placed at the head, neck, mid-mediastinum, pelvis, knees, and ankles for a total-body treatment. The pelvis reads within 5% of the prescription and the neck reads 18% high before compensators are adjusted. What are the diodes for?
A. They measure the machine output in a water tank at 100 cm, not the patient
B. They check that the dose along the patient is acceptably uniform once compensators, blocks, and the large-field geometry are in place
C. They measure neutron dose only, so a high neck reading means the energy was below 6 MV
D. They replace the need for lung blocks because a diode reading of 18% is a lung transmission
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. Extended-distance total-body irradiation is not a standard source-to-axis setup, and patient thickness varies from neck to pelvis. Diodes (or TLDs) on the patient are how the team confirms the prescription point and finds a thin region that still needs compensator. An 18% hot neck is a geometry and missing-tissue problem to correct before the full dose is committed, not a water-tank output measurement and not a neutron result. Lung transmission is set by the lung block, which is checked with its own point, typically under the block.

## Total Skin Electron Irradiation (TSEB)

### Q1
Stem: Total skin electron therapy for cutaneous lymphoma is delivered with a degraded 6 MeV beam at large distance, with the patient standing in six positions (anterior, posterior, and four obliques). Why is a low energy used instead of 18 MeV?
A. 18 MeV would underdose the dermis and increase x-ray contamination
B. The target is skin; a low energy after degradation keeps the practical range in the first 1 to 2 cm so deep organs are spared, and it keeps bremsstrahlung contamination small
C. Electrons at 6 MeV cannot treat a standing patient, so the six positions are a photon technique
D. Higher electron energy lowers surface dose, so 18 MeV would spare the epidermis more than 6 MeV
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. Mycosis fungoides in this technique is a skin target. An undegraded high-energy electron beam would penetrate far beyond the dermis and would carry a larger bremsstrahlung tail. That photon contamination irradiates the whole body, which is why the technique starts with a low energy, often 6 MeV, and then degrades it further so the 80% depth is only a few millimeters and the range stays shallow. Surface dose for electrons rises with energy, so D has the surface-dose rule backward. The six standing positions are the Stanford electron technique, not a photon plan.

### Q2
Stem: A total skin course delivers 36 Gy to the skin. The x-ray contamination of the degraded beam is 1%. If an undegraded higher-energy beam with 5% contamination were substituted and the same 36 Gy skin dose were given, what whole-body photon dose would that 5% imply?
A. 36 × 0.01 = 0.36 Gy, the same as the degraded beam
B. 36 × 0.05 = 1.8 Gy
C. 36 / 0.05 = 720 Gy
D. 5% of 1 Gy = 0.05 Gy, because contamination is defined per fraction of 1 Gy only
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. Contamination here means the photon dose beyond the electron range as a fraction of the skin dose. At 5%, a 36 Gy skin prescription deposits 36 × 0.05 = 1.8 Gy of whole-body photon dose, five times the 36 × 0.01 = 0.36 Gy of the 1% beam. That is a large unnecessary dose to bone marrow and deep organs, and it is independent of the electron range, which would also be too deep at the higher energy. Dividing by 0.05 inverts the definition. Contamination scales with the full skin dose, not with a 1 Gy reference fraction.

### Q3
Stem: During a six-field total skin treatment the soles, the perineum, the scalp, and the skin under a pannus are shielded by the patient's own body or by the beam geometry. What is the usual management of those sites?
A. They are left untreated, because self-shielding means those sites are not at risk for skin disease
B. They are boosted with a separate small electron field, because the total skin fields underdose them
C. They are boosted with 18 MV photons to a depth of 10 cm
D. They receive a full extra 36 Gy from bremsstrahlung, so a boost would overdose them
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. The Stanford-type arrangement does not see the soles, the top of the scalp, the perineum, and skin folds as if they were open skin in the beam. Those sites are supplemented with local electron boosts. The disease can involve them; self-shielding is a cold spot, not a reason to omit them. A deep photon boost would treat muscle and organs the technique is built to avoid. Bremsstrahlung at about 1% of 36 Gy is a fraction of a gray, not another 36 Gy.

### Q4
Stem: Internal eye shields are used for a low-energy total skin electron treatment. A planner suggests removing the degraders and treating at 12 MeV so the shields can stay in and the depth dose will "cover thicker plaques." Why is a higher energy a problem for an internal shield?
A. It is not; internal lead shields are designed for 12 to 20 MeV, where backscatter is lowest
B. Electrons energetic enough to reach or cross the shield deposit extra dose in the eyelid from backscatter off the high-Z shield, so internal shields are limited to low electron energies
C. 12 MeV electrons cannot backscatter, so the eyelid dose falls to zero
D. Eye shields are used only for total-body photon irradiation, never for electrons
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. An internal eye shield protects the globe only if the electron energy is low enough that the shield stops the beam. At higher energy, electrons reach the shield and high-Z backscatter raises the dose to the conjunctiva and eyelid, which can be worse than the unshielded dose. Clinical internal shields are therefore restricted to low energies, about 9 MeV and below. Removing the degrader to chase thicker disease moves the beam out of that safe range; thicker plaques need a different field design, not a hotter energy behind the same shield.

### Q5
Stem: The six patient positions in a classic total skin electron technique are used at an extended source-to-skin distance. Why six positions instead of a single anterior-posterior pair?
A. Two positions already produce a uniform dose around a curved body surface at close distance
B. Multiple oblique positions, often with dual gantry angles aimed above and below the umbilicus, improve the dose uniformity around the circumference and along the height of the body
C. Six positions are required so each position can use a different photon energy
D. The positions replace in vivo dosimetry, so TLDs on the skin are unnecessary
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. A body is not a flat phantom. An anterior-posterior pair underdoses the lateral skin and, at large distance, the dose also falls off toward the head and feet. The Stanford arrangement uses six standing angles and commonly dual fields (one angled up, one angled down) so the electron fluence joins more evenly. The energy stays a degraded electron beam. TLD or diode measurements on the skin are still how uniformity is checked on the actual patient; the positions do not replace them.

## Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS)

### Q1
Stem: A single brain metastasis of 1.6 cm maximum diameter is treated in one fraction. Using the RTOG 90-05 size rules as the planning guide (24 Gy for lesions 2 cm or smaller, 18 Gy for lesions larger than 2 cm up to 3 cm, 15 Gy for lesions larger than 3 cm up to 4 cm), which prescription matches the size?
A. 15 Gy, because every single-fraction brain metastasis receives 15 Gy
B. 24 Gy, because 1.6 cm is in the 2 cm-or-smaller group
C. 18 Gy, because any lesion under 3 cm receives 18 Gy
D. 8 Gy, because a single fraction outside the thorax is limited to the palliative bone dose
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. RTOG 90-05 escalated single-fraction radiosurgery and set the maximum tolerated doses by tumor diameter: 24 Gy at 2 cm or less, 18 Gy above 2 cm through 3 cm, and 15 Gy above 3 cm through 4 cm. A 1.6 cm lesion falls in the highest of those three doses. The increase in dose for smaller volumes is acceptable because less normal brain is inside the high-dose shell. A single 8 Gy fraction is a palliative bone regimen, not an intracranial radiosurgery dose.

### Q2
Stem: The PTV margin for a framed intracranial radiosurgery target is 0 to 1 mm, much tighter than a conventional 5 mm margin. Why is the tight margin acceptable, and what makes a loose margin harmful?
A. The margin is tight because the dose falls off steeply and a larger margin would place normal brain in a very high dose per fraction
B. The margin is tight because radiosurgery dose falls off slowly, similar to a large palliative field
C. A tight margin is used because image guidance cannot localize the skull
D. The margin must be at least 2 cm to cover microscopic disease along white-matter tracts for every metastasis
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. Stereotactic plans are prescribed to a high isodose shell that drops over a few millimeters. That steep gradient is what makes a 1 mm margin meaningful: the tissue just outside the target is already on the slope. Expanding the margin by a centimeter would move a large shell of normal brain onto the prescription dose, and at 15 to 24 Gy in one fraction that shell is at high risk of injury. The tight margin is only acceptable because localization (frame or high-precision image guidance) and a Winston-Lutz-level isocenter keep the residual error inside that millimeter. It is not a claim that metastases have no biology; it is a geometric and fractionation choice for a lesion whose entire target is the visible nodule.

### Q3
Stem: A Winston-Lutz test images a ball at mechanical isocenter with the treatment beam at several gantry and couch angles. The radiation center and the ball center differ by 0.4 mm at every angle. What has been measured?
A. Absolute output of the 6 MV beam in water
B. Coincidence of the radiation isocenter with the mechanical isocenter, which is a prerequisite for a 1 mm radiosurgery margin
C. The α/β of normal brain
D. Electron cutout factors for the cone
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. Winston-Lutz is a geometric test. The ball represents the mechanical isocenter. The radiation field's center should stay on that ball as the gantry and couch move. A 0.4 mm residual supports a radiosurgery program whose margin is about a millimeter. A residual of several millimeters would make a 1 mm PTV a fiction. The test does not measure output, radiobiology, or electron cutout factors.

### Q4
Stem: A frame-based radiosurgery coordinate system is built from the stereotactic frame fixed to the skull. After the planning CT, the frame has visibly slipped on one pin. What is the status of the coordinates?
A. Still valid; the frame is only an immobilization mask and the coordinates live in the mask tattoo
B. Not valid; the frame is the localization system, and a slip means the target coordinates no longer describe the skull that will be treated
C. Still valid if the prescription is lowered from 24 Gy to 8 Gy
D. Not valid only for Gamma Knife, because linac radiosurgery does not use coordinates
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. In a frame-based system the rods or fiducials on the frame define the coordinate box the target was localized in. If the frame moves relative to the skull, those coordinates point at the wrong intracranial point. The case is re-imaged and replanned, or it is converted to a frameless, image-guided delivery. Lowering the dose does not repair a wrong coordinate. Both Gamma Knife and frame-based linac radiosurgery depend on that localization.

### Q5
Stem: A single-isocenter linac radiosurgery plan and a multi-shot Gamma Knife plan both treat a 2 cm metastasis. Which statement about a Gamma Knife shot is correct?
A. Each shot is one isocenter delivered by the cobalt-60 sources through a chosen collimator; a larger or irregular target is built from several shots
B. Each shot is one monitor unit of a 15 MV photon sliding window
C. A shot is the helical pitch of a tomotherapy plan
D. Gamma Knife shots are electron cones placed on the skin
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. A Gamma Knife shot is an isocentric exposure: the sources focus through one collimator size at one point for a set time. Dose is shaped by stacking shots of different sizes and weights. That is a different delivery from a single-isocenter linac plan that modulates a multileaf aperture around one point, but both are intracranial stereotactic techniques. Shots are not linac monitor units, helical pitch, or electron cones.

### Q6
Stem: On a lung stereotactic plan the volume enclosed by the prescription isodose is much larger than the PTV. Which RTOG 0813 and RTOG 0915 metric is that, and what does a value far from 1 mean?
A. The conformity index. It compares the prescription-isodose volume with the PTV volume, and a value well above 1 means prescription dose is spilled outside the target
B. R50%. It becomes large only when the 50% cloud is smaller than the PTV
C. D2cm. It is the dose 2 cm inside the PTV
D. Lung V20. It is the prescription-isodose volume reported as a chest-wall percent
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. The conformity index asks whether the prescription isodose hugs the PTV. Near 1, the prescription volume and the PTV volume are comparable. Well above 1, the prescription dose covers a lot of tissue outside the target. R50% looks at the half-prescription volume, not the prescription volume. D2cm is a dose 2 cm outside the PTV, not inside it. Lung V20 is the portion of lung receiving 20 Gy or more.

### Q7
Stem: A planner is asked for the intermediate-dose falloff on an RTOG 0813 and RTOG 0915 lung plan. Which description is R50%?
A. The highest dose 2 cm outside the PTV
B. The volume enclosed by half the prescription dose, compared with the PTV volume
C. The portion of lung that receives 20 Gy or more
D. The chest-wall volume that receives 30 Gy and must stay under 30 cc
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. R50% compares the 50% isodose volume with the PTV volume. A large value means the intermediate dose spreads too far for the size of the target. The number a protocol will accept depends on PTV size, so this note does not teach one cutoff for every volume. The highest dose 2 cm outside the PTV is D2cm. Lung at 20 Gy is V20. Chest-wall volume at 30 Gy is the separate V30 constraint.

### Q8
Stem: The conformity index and R50% look acceptable, but a finger of high dose reaches tissue 2 cm outside the PTV. Which metric is built for that finding?
A. Lung V20, because every gray beyond the PTV is scored as a lung percent
B. The conformity index, because it only samples dose 2 cm away
C. D2cm, the highest dose 2 cm outside the PTV, judged against the prescription dose
D. The Stanford up-and-down pair, because D2cm is a total-skin uniformity check
Answer: C
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer C. D2cm catches high-dose spill at a fixed 2 cm distance from the PTV. R50% can look fine while a narrow hot finger still reaches that distance, which is why the metrics are not interchangeable. Lung V20 does not describe a point 2 cm from the target. The conformity index compares the whole prescription-isodose volume with the PTV. The Stanford angles belong to total skin electrons.

### Q9
Stem: Which quantity is lung V20 on an RTOG 0813 and RTOG 0915 stereotactic plan?
A. The maximum dose to 20% of the spinal cord
B. The volume of the 20% isodose divided by the PTV volume
C. The chest-wall volume receiving 30 Gy
D. The portion of lung that receives 20 Gy or more
Answer: D
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer D. Lung V20 is a lung-sparing check: how much lung is carried to 20 Gy or higher. It sits beside the conformity index, R50%, and D2cm. It is not a cord dose, not the 20% isodose around the target, and not the chest-wall V30. This note does not add a single percent cutoff for V20.

### Q10
Stem: A peripheral lung stereotactic plan reports 46 cc of chest wall receiving 30 Gy. The chest-wall constraint this course cites is a V30 under 30 cc. What is the finding?
A. The chest wall exceeds that limit. 46 cc receiving 30 Gy is above 30 cc
B. The chest wall passes, because the cited limit is a lung percent and cubic centimeters are ignored
C. The chest wall passes, because 17.5 Gy is the chest-wall limit and 30 Gy was the wrong score
D. The plan fails a hippocampal constraint, because chest-wall V30 belongs to RTOG 0933
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. The numeric chest-wall constraint cited here for these lung stereotactic protocols is V30 under 30 cc. Forty-six cubic centimeters is over that limit. The unit is cubic centimeters of chest wall, not a percent of lung. 17.5 Gy is the single-fraction brachial-plexus limit, not the chest-wall limit. RTOG 0933 is the hippocampal avoidance trial, not a chest-wall trial.

### Q11
Stem: A single-fraction lung stereotactic plan gives the brachial plexus a maximum of 20 Gy. The RTOG 0915-style single-fraction plexus limit this course cites is 17.5 Gy. Which statement is right?
A. The plexus is inside the limit, because 17.5 Gy is the chest-wall volume constraint and 20 Gy is under 30 cc
B. The plexus exceeds the cited single-fraction limit of 17.5 Gy
C. That 17.5 Gy limit is the whole-brain dose in RTOG 0933, so a lung plan does not use it
D. A plexus maximum of 20 Gy is the hippocampal D100% limit, so the plan meets RTOG 0933
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. For a single fraction, the brachial-plexus limit this exam cites from the RTOG 0915 style is 17.5 Gy. A maximum of 20 Gy is above it. Do not paste 17.5 Gy onto the chest wall. The chest-wall citation is V30 under 30 cc. RTOG 0933 is 30 Gy in 10 fractions to the whole brain, with hippocampal D100% at or below 9 Gy and maximum dose at or below 16 Gy. Those are not plexus numbers, and 17.5 Gy is not a 10-fraction hippocampal limit.

### Q12
Stem: A lung nodule is contoured on one free-breathing CT. A 4DCT of the same patient shows the nodule moving about a centimeter from superior to inferior. What should the internal target volume represent?
A. A centimeter expansion of the spinal cord
B. Only the exhale image, with inhale discarded
C. The envelope of the target over the breathing phases, the union of where the target sits during the cycle
D. The highest dose 2 cm outside the PTV
Answer: C
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer C. A 4DCT sorts the cycle into phases. The internal target volume is the union of the target across those phases. It is the motion envelope, not a cord expansion and not a dose metric. Using only exhale leaves the inhale position outside the volume. D2cm is a plan-quality dose, not a target definition.

### Q13
Stem: The internal target volume of a lung nodule was drawn on the maximum-intensity projection and was never compared with the individual 4DCT phases. What is the limitation?
A. The projection is a beam spoiler and does not show tumor
B. The projection replaces Winston-Lutz, so the phases are a couch-rotation test
C. The projection is the prescription isodose, so the contour is a conformity index
D. The projection keeps the brightest voxel over the phases and can outline the path, but it can include tissue the tumor never occupied, so the phases still have to be checked
Answer: D
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer D. A maximum-intensity projection helps a lung tumor paint its motion so the internal target volume can be drawn. It is not a substitute for the phases. Extra bright tissue can be pulled in, and a bad window can hide a phase. It is not a spoiler, not an isocenter test, and not the prescription isodose.

### Q14
Stem: Output for a 5 mm stereotactic cone is measured with a large Farmer-type chamber and with a detector that is small relative to the cone. The Farmer reading at the center is the lower one. Why?
A. A detector that is large compared with the field averages the peak with the penumbra and underestimates the dose at the center
B. A large detector overestimates the center, so the lower reading has to be the small detector
C. The Farmer chamber is the right detector for a cone because a small field has extra lateral equilibrium
D. The difference is a neutron dose, so the cone should be commissioned at 18 MV
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. Small-field dosimetry fails when the detector is too big for the field. Volume averaging pulls the center reading down toward the penumbra. The field is also short of lateral electronic equilibrium, so the output is not a scaled 10 by 10 factor. The small detector is the one that can see the peak. Raising the energy does not repair a chamber that averages the peak with the shoulder of the field.

### Q15
Stem: Cone-beam imaging of a stereotactic setup shows roll and pitch. The couch can correct only left-right, superior-inferior, and anterior-posterior shifts. What remains uncorrected?
A. The three translations. A six-degree couch cannot move the patient laterally
B. The rotations. Six degrees of freedom add pitch, roll, and yaw to the three translations. A translation-only couch leaves the rotation in the setup
C. Nothing. Roll and pitch are translations
D. Only the Winston-Lutz ball, which a couch shift is not allowed to change
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. Lateral, longitudinal, and vertical are the three translations. Pitch, roll, and yaw are the three rotations that make six degrees of freedom. A couch that cannot apply those rotations leaves a rotational miss. Far from the isocenter that rotation becomes a shift of the target, and a stereotactic margin has little room for it. Winston-Lutz is the isocenter agreement test. It is not the leftover roll on this setup.

### Q16
Stem: A framed radiosurgery plan is built from the frame. Before treatment one pin is loose. A colleague wants to treat without new images and call the delivery frameless, because no mask was used for localization. What is the distinction?
A. The frame is cosmetic. The coordinates live in the tattoos, so a loose pin does not matter
B. Frameless means the frame stays on and imaging is skipped
C. The frame is the coordinate system, so a slip means those coordinates no longer match the skull. Frameless treatment uses a mask plus imaging at the machine, and it still requires the stereotactic isocenter test
D. Frameless treatment is total skin electrons in six standing positions
Answer: C
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer C. In a frame-based system the frame defines the coordinates. If it moves relative to the skull, the plan points at the wrong intracranial place. Frameless delivery replaces the frame with a mask and with images that find the skull at treatment. The mask limits motion. The images locate the target. Dropping both the frame and the images leaves no localization. Frameless does not cancel Winston-Lutz. The radiation and mechanical isocenters still have to agree within 1 mm. Six standing positions are the Stanford total-skin technique.

### Q17
Stem: A Winston-Lutz test shows the radiation center 1.4 mm from the ball at mechanical isocenter. The stereotactic tolerance taught here is 1 mm or less. Lasers the same morning agreed within 1 mm. What follows?
A. The 1.4 mm result passes, because every technique uses a 2 mm isocenter tolerance
B. The lasers replace Winston-Lutz, so the 1.4 mm image is discarded
C. The test passes, because 1.4 mm is under 1 mm
D. Winston-Lutz fails the 1 mm tolerance. A laser agreement is not that test
Answer: D
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer D. Winston-Lutz images a ball at mechanical isocenter with a small treatment field from several gantry, collimator, and couch angles. For stereotactic work the radiation center and the mechanical center have to agree within 1 mm. 1.4 mm is outside that tolerance. Lasers can agree while the radiation isocenter does not. A daily laser check is not a Winston-Lutz test.

### Q18
Stem: Whole-brain radiotherapy is 30 Gy in 10 fractions with hippocampal avoidance under RTOG 0933. The hippocampus D100% is 11 Gy and the hottest point in the hippocampus is 15 Gy. Which cited constraint is missed?
A. D100% at or below 9 Gy. An 11 Gy value misses it. The 15 Gy maximum is still within 16 Gy
B. The maximum, because 15 Gy is above 16 Gy and D100% is allowed to be 11 Gy
C. Both, because RTOG 0933 prescribes 9 Gy to the whole brain
D. Neither, because D100% is allowed to equal the 30 Gy whole-brain prescription
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. RTOG 0933 keeps the entire hippocampus to 9 Gy or less, which is what D100% at or below 9 Gy means, and it keeps the hottest hippocampal point at or below 16 Gy. Eleven gray fails the D100% limit. Fifteen gray does not fail the 16 Gy maximum. The 30 Gy in 10 fractions is the whole-brain prescription outside that avoidance, not the hippocampal dose and not a 9 Gy whole-brain prescription.

### Q19
Stem: The same RTOG 0933 course, 30 Gy in 10 fractions to the whole brain, has a hippocampal maximum of 18 Gy and a hippocampal D100% of 8 Gy. Which statement matches the constraints this exam cites?
A. Both pass, because the only hippocampal limit is the 30 Gy prescription
B. D100% of 8 Gy meets the 9 Gy limit, and the 18 Gy maximum exceeds the 16 Gy limit
C. The maximum passes because 18 Gy is under 16 Gy, and the D100% fails
D. These two limits are the single-fraction plexus dose of 17.5 Gy, so 18 Gy is a chest-wall result
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. D100% at or below 9 Gy is met by 8 Gy. The maximum at or below 16 Gy is missed by 18 Gy. The whole brain is still prescribed 30 Gy in 10 fractions. That prescription is not the hippocampal maximum. 17.5 Gy is the single-fraction brachial-plexus limit from the RTOG 0915 style, and the chest-wall citation is V30 under 30 cc. Do not swap those numbers onto the hippocampus.

### Q20
Stem: The internal target volume was drawn from the 4DCT phases and already contains the full superior-inferior excursion of a lung tumor. The planner then adds that same excursion again as the planning-target margin. What is double-counted?
A. Nothing. The planning target volume is required to repeat the breathing motion on top of the internal target volume
B. The chest-wall V30, because a motion margin is a cubic-centimeter constraint
C. The breathing motion. The internal target volume is already the envelope. The planning-target margin on it is for setup residual, not a second copy of the excursion
D. Winston-Lutz. A motion margin replaces the 1 mm isocenter test
Answer: C
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer C. Once the internal target volume holds the union of the target over the breathing cycle, another full excursion in the planning target volume treats the same motion twice and sweeps extra lung into the high dose. The remaining margin is for setup. Image guidance and a Winston-Lutz result within 1 mm are what make that setup margin small. They are not replaced by the margin, and the margin is not the chest-wall constraint.

### Q21
Stem: A sheet assigns 17.5 Gy to the chest wall, assigns V30 under 30 cc to the brachial plexus, and calls both numbers RTOG 0933. Which assignment matches the citations in this course?
A. All three assignments are right, because 0933 is the lung stereotactic protocol
B. 17.5 Gy is the hippocampal maximum, and 30 cc is D100%
C. Chest-wall V30 under 30 cc is an RTOG 90-05 single-fraction brain constraint
D. Chest wall is the V30 under 30 cc citation. 17.5 Gy is the single-fraction brachial-plexus citation in the RTOG 0915 style. RTOG 0933 is hippocampal D100% at or below 9 Gy and maximum dose at or below 16 Gy, on whole-brain 30 Gy in 10 fractions
Answer: D
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer D. Keep the three citations apart. RTOG 0813 and RTOG 0915 are where this course places the conformity index, R50%, D2cm, and lung V20, plus chest-wall V30 under 30 cc and the single-fraction plexus limit of 17.5 Gy. RTOG 0933 is the hippocampal avoidance regimen, 30 Gy in 10 fractions, with D100% at or below 9 Gy and maximum dose at or below 16 Gy. ## Special Treatment Procedures Quiz

### Q1
Stem: Intraoperative electron therapy is delivered in a single fraction through a sterile cone on the tumor bed. The cord is 3 cm deep to that surface. Which statement uses the rule that the practical range in centimeters is about half the energy in MeV?
A. 12 MeV stops at about 3 cm, so the cord is beyond the practical range
B. 6 MeV has a practical range of about 3 cm and therefore reaches a cord at 3 cm; 12 MeV reaches about 6 cm and 20 MeV about 10 cm, so sparing that cord takes a lower energy or bolus
C. 20 MeV has a practical range of about 2 cm
D. 6 MV photons stop at 3 cm, so a photon beam is the one that spares the cord
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. Practical range Rp is about E/2 in centimeters of water. At 6 MeV, Rp is about 3 cm, which lands on the cord rather than stopping short of it. At 12 MeV, Rp is about 6 cm. At 20 MeV, Rp is about 10 cm, not 2 cm. If the prescription depth is only the first centimeter of the bed, the cord at 3 cm is spared by a lower energy or by bolus that uses up range before the patient, not by going up in energy. Megavoltage photons do not stop at 3 cm. Intraoperative electrons are chosen because the range can be matched to the bed and then ended.

### Q2
Stem: Which total-body accessory is matched to the correct purpose?
A. Lung block: raise skin dose; spoiler: lower lung dose; neck compensator: increase electron energy
B. Spoiler: raise skin dose; neck compensator: reduce the high dose in a thin section; lung block: reduce lung dose from low density
C. All three devices reduce the monitor units by the same transmission factor
D. The spoiler is a high-Z wedge used to tilt the midline isodose
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. The three accessories solve three different problems of a large photon field at extended distance. The spoiler restores surface dose. The compensator or bolus bags equalize path length through the neck, ankles, and other thin anatomy. The lung block (or lung compensator) offsets the extra dose lung would receive because it attenuates less than soft tissue. They are not interchangeable, and a spoiler is low-Z plastic, not a wedge.

### Q3
Stem: A total skin electron plan and a total-body photon plan both treat "the whole patient" at large distance. Which difference in target depth is the reason one uses degraded electrons and the other uses photons?
A. Total skin electrons target the epidermis and dermis and must fall off before deep organs; total-body photons target marrow and deep tissues as well as skin
B. Total-body photons target only the epidermis; total skin electrons target the mid-pelvis
C. Both techniques use 18 MeV electrons; the names refer only to the couch
D. Total skin therapy uses a proton Bragg peak at 30 cm range
Answer: A
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer A. The names describe the target, not the room. Cutaneous lymphoma in the total skin technique is a few millimeters deep, so the beam is a degraded electron with a short practical range and very low photon contamination. Total-body irradiation before transplant has to reach bone marrow throughout the body, so it is a photon technique with skin dose boosted by a spoiler and with lungs selectively reduced. Swapping the beams would either miss the marrow or overtreat the deep organs.

### Q4
Stem: Stereotactic body radiotherapy to a peripheral lung tumor uses 18 Gy times 3 fractions and a 5 mm PTV margin. A conventional lung plan uses 2 Gy times 30 and a larger margin. Why is the stereotactic margin kept tight?
A. Because 18 Gy fractions have a shallow dose gradient, so extra margin does not add normal-lung dose
B. Because the high dose per fraction and the steep falloff make every extra millimeter of lung inside the PTV biologically expensive
C. Because stereotactic body plans cannot be image guided, so the margin is tightened to save time
D. Because a 5 mm margin is the ICRU internal target volume for the heart
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. The planning advantage of stereotactic body radiotherapy is a fast dose falloff around a small target. Combined with a large dose per fraction, tissue inside the margin receives a high biologically effective dose. The margin is therefore only as large as residual motion and setup allow, which is why motion management and image guidance are part of the technique rather than a reason to skip them. A shallow gradient would be a reason for a wider margin, not a tighter one. The internal target volume is a motion volume around the CTV, not a fixed 5 mm heart margin.

### Q5
Stem: A palliative single-fraction total-body dose of 2 Gy and a myeloablative course of 12 Gy in 6 fractions are both called total-body irradiation. A planner applies lung blocks designed for the 12 Gy course, with transmission calculated to hold the lung at 10 Gy, to the 2 Gy single fraction. What is wrong with that reuse?
A. Nothing; lung transmission is independent of the prescription
B. The transmission was solved for a different prescription and a different lung limit; a block that transmits enough dose to reach 10 Gy in the high-dose course is not the block for a 2 Gy course
C. The 2 Gy course always needs a thicker block than the 12 Gy course because single fractions produce more neutrons
D. Lung blocks are used only for total skin electrons
Answer: B
Objective: III.6
Why: Answer B. In the worked example for a 12 Gy midline prescription, an unblocked lung dose of 14 Gy was brought to 10 Gy by a transmission of 10/14. That thickness and that clinical limit belong to the myeloablative regimen, where lung toxicity is the constraint. A 2 Gy low-dose total-body fraction does not have a 10 Gy lung target, and the same physical block would still transmit about 71% of an unblocked lung dose that is only slightly above 2 Gy, which is a different clinical decision. Accessories are recalculated for the prescription in front of the planner, not copied from a different protocol because the field size looks similar.

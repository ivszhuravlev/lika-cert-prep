# Module 1. Introduction

Original items. Legal and professional framework: negligence, informed consent, advance directives, AAMD documents, and JRCERT.

## Ethics, Law and Professional Standards

### Q1
Stem: A dosimetrist enters the wrong isocenter shift into the record. The therapist catches the error at the first setup and the patient is never treated with the wrong shift. In legal terms this event is:
A. Malpractice, because a professional standard was breached
B. Battery, because the patient did not consent to an incorrect setup
C. A breach of duty without negligence, because no damages occurred
D. Negligence, because the four elements of a tort are present
Answer: C
Objective: admin
Why: Negligence requires duty, breach, causation, and actual damages; here there was a breach but no harm, so the legal definition is not met even though the event must be reported and investigated internally. Malpractice is professional negligence and also requires damages. Battery is an intentional tort involving unconsented touching, which does not describe a data entry error. Option D is wrong because the damages element is missing.

### Q2
Stem: Which element is required for informed consent to be valid?
A. The patient has been told the material risks, benefits, and alternatives in language they understand
B. The patient has signed the department consent form in the presence of a witness
C. The dosimetrist has explained the plan and the expected dose to critical structures
D. The consent was obtained after the first fraction so the patient could judge tolerability
Answer: A
Objective: admin
Why: Consent is a process of disclosure to a competent patient who is free to refuse; the disclosure of risks, benefits, and alternatives including no treatment is the heart of it. A signature is documentation of the process, not the process itself, and a signed form obtained without disclosure is not valid consent. The treating physician, not the dosimetrist, is responsible for obtaining consent. Consent must be obtained before treatment begins.

### Q3
Stem: A patient with a signed living will refusing intubation is alert and oriented during simulation and asks a question about resuscitation. Which statement about the living will is correct?
A. It overrides the patient's current statements because it was signed earlier
B. It takes effect only if the patient loses the capacity to make decisions
C. It must be re-signed in the radiation oncology department to be valid
D. It names the radiation oncologist as the surrogate decision maker
Answer: B
Objective: admin
Why: An advance directive, whether a living will or a durable power of attorney for health care, speaks for the patient only when the patient can no longer speak for themselves; a competent patient's current wishes always control. It does not expire when the patient changes departments. A living will gives instructions about interventions; it is the durable power of attorney that names a surrogate, and that surrogate is chosen by the patient, not the treating physician.

### Q4
Stem: Which document would a department cite to show that a medical dosimetrist is not permitted to change a radiation prescription?
A. The MDCB examination content outline
B. The JRCERT accreditation standards for the training program
C. The AAMD Scope of Practice for the medical dosimetrist
D. The hospital radiation safety committee charter
Answer: C
Objective: admin
Why: The AAMD Scope of Practice defines what the dosimetrist does and does not do, including that prescribing and altering a prescription belong to the radiation oncologist; the AAMD Practice Standards set the expected level of performance within that scope. The MDCB outline lists examination topics, not permitted duties. JRCERT standards govern educational programs. A radiation safety committee charter addresses licensed material and protection, not planning roles.

### Q5
Stem: The JRCERT is best described as the organization that:
A. Certifies individual medical dosimetrists by examination
B. Accredits educational programs in radiation therapy and medical dosimetry
C. Accredits hospitals that deliver radiation therapy
D. Publishes the professional scope of practice for dosimetrists
Answer: B
Objective: admin
Why: The Joint Review Committee on Education in Radiologic Technology accredits programs, and graduation from a JRCERT-accredited dosimetry program is one route to MDCB eligibility. Certification of individuals is the MDCB's job. Hospital accreditation belongs to bodies such as The Joint Commission or ACR/ASTRO practice accreditation. The scope of practice is an AAMD document.

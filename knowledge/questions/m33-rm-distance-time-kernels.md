# Module 33. External beam dose calculation and algorithms

Original items. Inverse square solved for distance and for accumulated exposure, plus the kernel-invariance distinction between convolution and superposition.

## Solving Inverse Square for Distance and Time

### Q1
Stem: A linac delivers 300 cGy per minute at 100 cm from the source. A hemibody protocol allows at most 80 cGy per minute at the patient midplane. Considering inverse square only, what is the shortest source-to-midplane distance that meets the cap, to the nearest 0.1 cm?
A. 375.0 cm
B. 193.6 cm
C. 51.6 cm
D. 1406.3 cm
Answer: B
Objective: IV.1
Why: The rate must fall by 300/80 = 3.75, so the distance grows by the square root of 3.75, which is 1.9365. Distance = 100 × 1.9365 = 193.6 cm. Check: 300 × (100/193.6)² = 300 × 0.2667 = 80.0 cGy/min. Multiplying by 3.75 without the root gives 375.0 cm, which would cut the rate to about 21 cGy/min. Putting 80/300 under the root gives 51.6 cm and moves the patient closer, raising the rate. Squaring 3.75 instead of taking its root gives 1406.3 cm.

### Q2
Stem: In normal mode a unit measures 320 cGy per minute at 100 cm from the source. A total-body protocol requires a midplane dose rate of 20 cGy per minute or less. Ignoring attenuation and using inverse square only, how far from the source must the midplane be placed?
A. 1600 cm
B. 25 cm
C. 400 cm
D. 800 cm
Answer: C
Objective: IV.1
Why: The required reduction is 320/20 = 16, and the square root of 16 is 4, so the distance is 100 × 4 = 400 cm. Check: 320 × (100/400)² = 320/16 = 20 cGy/min. Leaving out the square root gives 100 × 16 = 1600 cm. Inverting the ratio under the root gives 100 × (1/4) = 25 cm, which would raise the rate to 5120 cGy/min. Treating a factor of 16 as if it needed an 8-fold distance gives 800 cm, where the rate would be only 5 cGy/min.

### Q3
Stem: A survey meter reads 60 mR per hour at 1.5 m from a patient with a temporary implant. A nurse works for 20 minutes at 3 m from the patient. Using inverse square only, what exposure does the nurse accumulate?
A. 300 mR
B. 10 mR
C. 20 mR
D. 5 mR
Answer: D
Objective: IV.1
Why: The rate at 3 m is 60 × (1.5/3)² = 60 × 0.25 = 15 mR/h. Twenty minutes is 20/60 = 0.333 h, so the exposure is 15 × 0.333 = 5.0 mR. Check: 15 mR in a full hour means 5 mR in a third of an hour. Multiplying the per-hour rate by 20 minutes without converting gives 300 mR. Using the unsquared distance ratio, 60 × 0.5 × 0.333, gives 10 mR. Forgetting the move from 1.5 m to 3 m, 60 × 0.333, gives 20 mR.

### Q4
Stem: The exposure rate at 2 m from a brachytherapy patient is 12 mR per hour. A visitor is limited to 2 mR for the visit and will stand at 4 m. Using inverse square only, how long may the visit last?
A. 40 minutes
B. 10 minutes
C. 20 minutes
D. 2.5 minutes
Answer: A
Objective: IV.1
Why: At 4 m the rate is 12 × (2/4)² = 12 × 0.25 = 3 mR/h. The allowed time is 2 mR divided by 3 mR/h = 0.667 h = 40 minutes. Check: 3 mR/h × 0.667 h = 2.0 mR. Using the 2 m rate without moving to 4 m gives 2/12 h = 10 minutes. Halving the rate instead of quartering it gives 6 mR/h and 20 minutes. Inverting the distance ratio gives 48 mR/h and only 2.5 minutes.

## Spatially Invariant Kernels

### Q1
Stem: A vendor describes its photon dose engine as applying the same dose-spread array at every voxel and handling heterogeneities only by changing the path length to each voxel. Which statement best classifies that engine?
A. Convolution with a spatially invariant kernel, so lateral scatter in lung is modeled as in water
B. Superposition, because the path-length change rescales the kernel by density
C. Monte Carlo, because dose-spread arrays are generated by particle histories
D. A grid-based Boltzmann solver, because it is deterministic
Answer: A
Objective: IV.1
Why: One kernel for every point is the definition of a spatially invariant kernel, and that is a convolution; a path-length change only moves where energy is released, it does not rescale the kernel by local density. Superposition is the model in which the kernel is scaled by the density between the interaction site and the dose point, which this description explicitly does not do. Kernels are precomputed by Monte Carlo, but using a stored kernel does not make the engine a Monte Carlo transport calculation. A Boltzmann solver transports fluence through each voxel's material and uses no stored dose-spread array.

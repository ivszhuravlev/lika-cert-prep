## Mass Defect and Binding Energy

### Q1
Stem: The measured mass of a stable nucleus is compared with the summed masses of its free protons and neutrons. The nucleus is:
A. Heavier, because the strong force adds mass
B. Lighter, and the difference is the binding energy
C. Exactly equal, because mass is conserved
D. Lighter only in radioactive nuclei
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: The bound nucleus is lighter than its separated parts. The missing mass, the mass defect, left as energy when the nucleons bound together and equals the binding energy through E = mc squared. Mass alone is not conserved; mass-energy is. Every bound nucleus, stable or not, shows a defect. The strong force binds; it does not add mass.

### Q2
Stem: A helium-4 nucleus has a mass defect of 0.0304 atomic mass units. One unit is equivalent to 931.5 MeV. What is the binding energy per nucleon?
A. 28.3 MeV
B. 14.2 MeV
C. 7.1 MeV
D. 0.0304 MeV
Answer: C
Objective: I.4
Why: Total binding energy is 0.0304 times 931.5, which is 28.3 MeV. Helium-4 has four nucleons, so 28.3 divided by 4 is 7.1 MeV per nucleon. Check: 7.1 times 4 is 28.4 MeV. 28.3 MeV is the total, not per nucleon. 14.2 MeV divides by the two protons only. 0.0304 MeV restates the mass defect without converting.

### Q3
Stem: Which force holds protons and neutrons together inside the nucleus against the electrostatic repulsion of the protons?
A. The electromagnetic force
B. Gravity
C. The weak nuclear force
D. The strong nuclear force
Answer: D
Objective: I.1
Why: The strong force acts between nucleons over about a femtometer and overwhelms proton-proton repulsion at that range. The electromagnetic force is what the nucleus has to overcome; it binds orbital electrons, not nucleons. Gravity is negligible at nuclear scale. The weak force governs beta decay and does not bind the nucleus.

### Q4
Stem: Compared with a proton, the rest mass of an electron is approximately:
A. 1/1836 of the proton mass
B. 1/2 of the proton mass
C. Equal to the proton mass
D. 1/511 of the proton mass
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: The electron mass is 9.11 times ten to the minus 31 kg and the proton is 1.673 times ten to the minus 27 kg, a ratio of about 1 to 1836. In rest-energy terms that is 0.511 MeV versus 938 MeV. The number 511 is the electron rest energy in keV, not a mass ratio. Protons and neutrons are nearly equal to each other, but both are far heavier than the electron.

### Q5
Stem: One electron-volt expressed in joules is closest to:
A. 1.66 times ten to the minus 27 J
B. 1.6 times ten to the minus 19 J
C. 6.63 times ten to the minus 34 J
D. 9.11 times ten to the minus 31 J
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: An electron-volt is the energy an elementary charge gains across one volt: 1.602 times ten to the minus 19 coulombs times one volt equals 1.602 times ten to the minus 19 joules. 1.66 times ten to the minus 27 is the kilogram value of one atomic mass unit. 6.63 times ten to the minus 34 is Planck's constant in joule-seconds. 9.11 times ten to the minus 31 is the electron mass in kilograms.

## Avogadro, Planck and the Electromagnetic Spectrum

### Q1
Stem: Avogadro's number is 6.02 times ten to the 23. How many atoms are in one gram of carbon-12?
A. 6.0 times ten to the 23 atoms
B. 5.0 times ten to the 22 atoms
C. 7.2 times ten to the 24 atoms
D. 3.0 times ten to the 23 atoms
Answer: B
Objective: I.1
Why: One gram-atomic mass of carbon-12 is 12 grams and holds Avogadro's number of atoms, so one gram holds 6.02 times ten to the 23 divided by 12, which is 5.0 times ten to the 22. Check: 5.0 times ten to the 22 times 12 returns 6.0 times ten to the 23. The first option is the whole mole. The third option multiplies by 12 instead of dividing. The last option is the electron count per gram (atoms per gram times Z of 6).

### Q2
Stem: Water has a molecular mass of 18 and ten electrons per molecule. Using Avogadro's number of 6.02 times ten to the 23, how many electrons are in one gram of water?
A. 3.3 times ten to the 23 electrons
B. 3.3 times ten to the 22 electrons
C. 6.0 times ten to the 24 electrons
D. 1.1 times ten to the 24 electrons
Answer: A
Objective: I.1
Why: Molecules per gram equal 6.02 times ten to the 23 divided by 18, which is 3.34 times ten to the 22. Ten electrons per molecule gives 3.34 times ten to the 23 electrons per gram. Check: 3.34 times ten to the 23 times 18 divided by 10 returns 6.0 times ten to the 23. The second option forgets the ten electrons. The third multiplies by ten without dividing by 18. The fourth uses 18 electrons per molecule instead of ten.

### Q3
Stem: Which list places electromagnetic radiation in order of increasing photon energy?
A. Gamma rays, x-rays, ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwaves, radio
B. Radio, infrared, microwaves, visible, x-rays, ultraviolet, gamma rays
C. Radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, x-rays, gamma rays
D. Visible, ultraviolet, infrared, microwaves, x-rays, gamma rays, radio
Answer: C
Objective: I.3
Why: Energy rises with frequency and falls with wavelength, so the sequence runs radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, x-ray, gamma. The first option is the correct order reversed, which is increasing wavelength. The second swaps infrared and microwaves and swaps ultraviolet and x-rays. The last is scrambled. X-rays and gamma rays overlap in energy and are separated by origin, but gamma rays are conventionally listed last.

### Q4
Stem: A linac accelerating structure is driven at 3 GHz. Planck's constant is 4.14 times ten to the minus 15 electron-volt seconds. What is the energy of one RF photon?
A. 12 eV
B. 12 keV
C. 1.2 MeV
D. 12 micro-electron-volts
Answer: D
Objective: I.3
Why: Energy equals Planck's constant times frequency: 4.14 times ten to the minus 15 times 3 times ten to the 9 equals 1.24 times ten to the minus 5 eV, about 12 micro-electron-volts. Check: 1.24 times ten to the minus 5 divided by 4.14 times ten to the minus 15 returns 3 times ten to the 9 Hz. That is far below the roughly 10 eV needed to ionize tissue, so microwaves are a heating hazard only. 12 eV would be near-ultraviolet. 12 keV is a diagnostic x-ray. 1.2 MeV is a Co-60 gamma ray.

### Q5
Stem: A gamma ray has an energy of 1.24 MeV. Using the relation that energy in keV equals 12.4 divided by wavelength in angstroms, what is its wavelength?
A. 0.010 angstrom
B. 0.10 angstrom
C. 1.0 angstrom
D. 10 angstroms
Answer: A
Objective: I.3
Why: 1.24 MeV is 1240 keV. Wavelength equals 12.4 divided by 1240, which is 0.010 angstrom, or one picometer. Check: 12.4 divided by 0.010 returns 1240 keV. 0.10 angstrom corresponds to 124 keV, 1.0 angstrom to 12.4 keV, and 10 angstroms to 1.24 keV, each a factor of ten lower in energy. The mistake behind the wrong answers is treating 1.24 as keV rather than MeV, or sliding the decimal.

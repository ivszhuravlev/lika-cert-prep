# Module 44. Practice Test

Original items. Exam-format drills in the MDCB "which statements are correct" combination style and matching style, built only from facts already stated in the theory notes of modules 2–43.

## Combination items

### Q1
Stem: Which statements about photon interactions are correct? I. Photoelectric absorption varies about as Z cubed divided by energy cubed. II. The Compton probability per electron is independent of Z. III. Pair production has a threshold of 1.022 MeV and is the dominant interaction in soft tissue at 6 MV.
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: A
Objective: I.4
Why: Statement I is true: the exam form of the photoelectric dependence is Z cubed over E cubed. Statement II is true: Compton depends on electron density, not Z, which is why megavoltage dose tracks density. Statement III is false: the threshold is 1.022 MeV, but Compton still dominates in tissue at 6 to 18 MV; a threshold is permission to occur, not dominance. Only I and II are correct.

### Q2
Stem: Which statements about cobalt-60 teletherapy are correct? I. The source emits gammas of 1.17 and 1.33 MeV. II. The half-life is 5.26 years, so output falls about 1 percent per month. III. The depth of maximum dose in water is about 0.5 cm.
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. I, II and III
D. II and III only
Answer: C
Objective: I.7
Why: All three are true. The two gamma lines average 1.25 MeV. A 5.26-year half-life gives a decay constant of about 0.011 per month, so output drops about 1.09 percent monthly and treatment time rises. Buildup for a 1.25 MeV beam is shallow, about 0.5 cm, so skin sparing is modest compared with 6 MV.

### Q3
Stem: Which statements about linear accelerator components are correct? I. The klystron or magnetron supplies radiofrequency power and sits outside the treatment beam. II. In electron mode the flattening filter is removed and a scattering foil is inserted. III. The x-ray target stays in the beam for electron treatments.
A. I and III only
B. II and III only
C. I, II and III
D. I and II only
Answer: D
Objective: I.5
Why: Statement I is true: the RF source feeds the waveguide and is never in the beam path. Statement II is true: electrons are spread by a thin foil, not flattened by the thick cone. Statement III is false: the target is retracted for electron mode; leaving it in would make bremsstrahlung x-rays. The answer is I and II only.

### Q4
Stem: Which statements about x-ray production in a tube are correct? I. Raising kVp changes beam quantity only. II. Added filtration raises the average energy and the half-value layer. III. Tungsten K characteristic x-rays appear only when the tube potential exceeds about 70 kV.
A. I and II only
B. II and III only
C. I and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: B
Objective: I.4
Why: Statement I is false: kVp changes both quality and quantity, and it is mA that changes quantity only. Statement II is true: filtration removes low-energy photons, so the remaining beam is harder with a higher HVL. Statement III is true: the tungsten K-shell binding energy is about 69.5 keV, so a 60 kV tube produces no K lines. II and III are correct.

### Q5
Stem: Which statements about kerma and absorbed dose in a megavoltage beam are correct? I. Kerma is highest at the surface and falls with depth. II. Absorbed dose equals collision kerma under charged-particle equilibrium. III. Dose builds up to dmax because electrons set in motion upstream have not yet arrived at the surface.
A. I and II only
B. I, II and III
C. II and III only
D. I and III only
Answer: B
Objective: I.6
Why: All three are true. Photon fluence, and so kerma, is greatest at the surface and attenuates with depth. Dose tracks collision kerma only where the electrons leaving a small mass are replaced by electrons arriving, which is charged-particle equilibrium. Buildup is the arrival of forward-directed electrons, not attenuation of the primary photons; attenuation would make the surface the hottest point.

### Q6
Stem: Which statements about depth-dose quantities are correct? I. Percent depth dose increases with energy, field size and SSD. II. Tissue-maximum ratio does not depend on SSD. III. The Mayneord F factor corrects a PDD for both inverse square and scatter when SSD changes.
A. II and III only
B. I and III only
C. I and II only
D. I, II and III
Answer: C
Objective: I.6
Why: Statement I is true: more energy penetrates, more field adds phantom scatter, and a longer SSD flattens the inverse-square fall-off inside the PDD. Statement II is true: TMR is defined at a fixed source-to-point distance so inverse square cancels. Statement III is false: Mayneord F is an inverse-square correction only; scatter still belongs to the original SSD. I and II only.

### Q7
Stem: Which statements about radiation units are correct? I. One millicurie equals 37 gigabecquerels. II. One roentgen equals 2.58 times ten to the minus four coulombs per kilogram. III. One centisievert equals one rem.
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. I, II and III
D. II and III only
Answer: D
Objective: I.1
Why: Statement I is false: 1 Ci is 37 GBq, so 1 mCi is 37 MBq, a factor of a thousand smaller. Statement II is true: that is the SI definition of the roentgen in air. Statement III is true: 1 Sv is 100 rem, so 1 cSv is 1 rem. II and III only.

### Q8
Stem: Which statements about radiation weighting factors are correct? I. Photons and electrons have a weighting factor of 1. II. Alpha particles have a weighting factor of 20. III. Neutrons have a weighting factor of 1 at all energies.
A. I and II only
B. II and III only
C. I and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: A
Objective: I.3
Why: Statements I and II are true: equivalent dose in sievert equals absorbed dose in gray for photons and electrons, and alphas are weighted 20. Statement III is false: the neutron factor depends on energy and peaks near 20 around 1 MeV; the ICRP 60 steps run from 5 to 20. Never assign neutrons a flat factor of 1. I and II only.

### Q9
Stem: Which statements about charged-particle beams are correct? I. Clinical electron beams show a sharp Bragg peak. II. Protons have a finite range with a Bragg peak and a clinical RBE of 1.1. III. Electron surface dose rises as the electron energy rises.
A. I and II only
B. I, II and III
C. II and III only
D. I and III only
Answer: C
Objective: I.3
Why: Statement I is false: multiple scattering washes out any peak in a clinical electron beam, which rises to a shallow dmax and falls to a bremsstrahlung tail. Statement II is true: range is set by energy, LET rises in the peak, and planning uses RBE 1.1. Statement III is true: an 18 MeV beam is already high at the skin while 6 MeV still shows buildup, the opposite of megavoltage photons. II and III only.

### Q10
Stem: Which statements about permanent-seed nuclides are correct? I. I-125 has a half-life of 59.4 days and emits photons of about 28 keV. II. Pd-103 has a half-life of 17 days. III. Cs-131 is the 30-year source with a 662 keV gamma.
A. I and III only
B. I and II only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: B
Objective: I.7
Why: Statements I and II are true; both are electron-capture, low-energy photon seeds, and the shorter Pd-103 mean life means it starts about 3.5 times hotter for the same total dose. Statement III is false: the 30-year, 662 keV source is Cs-137; Cs-131 is a 9.7-day permanent seed with photons near 30 keV. I and II only.

### Q11
Stem: Which statements about the Hounsfield scale are correct? I. Water is 0 HU and air is minus 1000 HU by definition. II. A voxel at plus 1000 HU attenuates CT photons twice as much as water. III. The planning system converts HU to density with a single straight line through bone, equal to HU divided by 1000.
A. I and III only
B. II and III only
C. I, II and III
D. I and II only
Answer: D
Objective: I.9
Why: Statement I is true: those two anchors define the scale, and minus 1024 is only a storage floor. Statement II is true: relative attenuation is 1 plus HU over 1000, so plus 1000 is a ratio of 2. Statement III is false: the CT-to-density curve is measured in a phantom and is typically bilinear, with a different slope above water because photoelectric absorption inflates bone attenuation at kV energies. I and II only.

### Q12
Stem: Which statements about imaging modalities in planning are correct? I. CT is the modality that supplies electron density for the dose calculation. II. On T1-weighted MRI fat is bright; on T2-weighted MRI fluid is bright. III. PET detects two coincident 511 keV annihilation photons.
A. I, II and III
B. I and II only
C. II and III only
D. I and III only
Answer: A
Objective: II.3
Why: All three are true. Only CT numbers pass through a commissioned density table; MRI pixel values and PET uptake must never be sent through it. T1 shows fat bright and fluid relatively dark, T2 shows fluid bright. PET coincidence imaging registers the two 0.511 MeV photons from positron annihilation, and SUV is an uptake number, not a density or a dose.

### Q13
Stem: Which statements about four-dimensional CT are correct? I. The ITV is drawn from the maximum-intensity projection or the union of the phases. II. Dose is calculated on the MIP. III. Irregular breathing can sort anatomy into the wrong phase and produce duplicated diaphragms.
A. I and II only
B. I and III only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: B
Objective: II.6
Why: Statement I is true: the MIP shows every voxel the tumor passed through and is the contouring set for a solid lung target. Statement II is false: dose is calculated on the average CT or a chosen phase, because the MIP assigns tumor density everywhere the tumor moved. Statement III is true: sorting artifacts must be recognized and not contoured as tumor. I and III only.

### Q14
Stem: Which statements about image registration are correct? I. A rigid registration has six degrees of freedom: three translations and three rotations. II. Mutual information is the usual similarity measure for CT-to-MRI fusion. III. Cortical bone is bright on both CT and MRI, so pixel values can be compared directly.
A. II and III only
B. I and III only
C. I and II only
D. I, II and III
Answer: C
Objective: II.5
Why: Statement I is true: left-right, superior-inferior and anterior-posterior shifts plus pitch, roll and yaw. Statement II is true: mutual information does not need the intensities to match, which is what cross-modality fusion requires. Statement III is false: cortical bone is bright on CT and dark on MRI, which is exactly why a direct intensity comparison fails. I and II only.

### Q15
Stem: Which statements about image guidance are correct? I. Kilovoltage cone-beam CT shows soft tissue, bladder filling and rectal gas. II. Surface guidance alone is a reliable check of prostate position. III. Prostate position follows rectal and bladder filling.
A. I and III only
B. I and II only
C. II and III only
D. I, II and III
Answer: A
Objective: II.7
Why: Statement I is true: a volumetric kV image on the linac shows the soft-tissue anatomy a planar kV pair or MV portal cannot. ### Q16

### Q18

### Q19

### Q21
Stem: Which statements about dose homogeneity goals are correct? I. The classic ICRU 50 target band is minus 5 to plus 7 percent of the prescription. II. ### Q22
Stem: Which statements about wedges are correct? I. The wedge angle is the tilt of an isodose line, often near 10 cm depth, not the slope of the metal. II. Dynamic and enhanced dynamic wedges need no wedge factor in the monitor-unit calculation. III. For a symmetric wedged pair, wedge angle equals 90 degrees minus half the hinge angle.
A. I and II only
B. II and III only
C. I, II and III
D. I and III only
Answer: D
Objective: III.2
Why: Statement I is true by definition of wedge angle. Statement II is false: a jaw-motion wedge still attenuates the time-integrated fluence and still needs a measured factor in the MU. ### Q23
Stem: Which statements about wedge errors are correct? I. If the wedge is in the beam and its factor was left out of the MU, the patient is underdosed by about that factor. II. If the factor was used in the MU and the wedge was never inserted, the patient is overdosed. III. A physical wedge changes beam quality slightly.
A. I and II only
B. I, II and III
C. I and III only
D. II and III only
Answer: B
Objective: III.2
Why: All three are true. A wedge factor of 0.65 omitted from the MU delivers 65 percent of the prescription; the same factor applied while the wedge is absent delivers about 1.54 times the prescription. Metal in the beam hardens the spectrum a little, which is why physical wedges need measured factors.

### Q24
Stem: Which statements about beam modifiers are correct? I. Bolus pulls dmax toward the surface and raises skin dose. II. A compensator replaces missing tissue and sits in the beam away from the skin. III. MLC leaves transmit no dose, so a region under the leaves receives zero.
A. I and III only
B. II and III only
C. I and II only
D. I, II and III
Answer: C
Objective: III.2
Why: Statements I and II are true: bolus is tissue-equivalent material on the skin, a compensator is a shaped attenuator in the head that keeps skin sparing. ### Q25
Stem: Which statements about raising megavoltage photon energy are correct? I. The depth of maximum dose moves deeper and skin sparing increases. II. Exit dose increases. III. Above about 10 MV the accelerator head produces photoneutrons.

D. I and III only

### Q26
Stem: Which statements about electron planning are correct? I. The 80 percent depth is about E divided by 2.8 and the practical range about E divided by 2, in centimeters for E in MeV. II. B. II and III only
C. I, II and III
D. I and II only

### Q27

### Q31

### Q32

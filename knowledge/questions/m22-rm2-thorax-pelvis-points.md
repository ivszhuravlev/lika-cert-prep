# Module 22. Cross-Sectional Anatomy

Original items. Round 2 from the RM Review gap list: surface position of the cardiac apex, the chambers that form the heart borders, and rectal dimensions.

## Thoracic and Pelvic Surface Points

### Q1
Stem: Where is the cardiac apex located on the surface of an adult chest, and which chamber forms it?
A. Right fourth intercostal space at the sternal border; right ventricle
B. Left second intercostal space at the midclavicular line; pulmonary trunk
C. Left fifth intercostal space at about the midclavicular line; left ventricle
D. Left eighth intercostal space at the anterior axillary line; left atrium
Answer: C
Objective: III.4
Why: The apex is the tip of the left ventricle and lies in the left fifth intercostal space at about the midclavicular line, roughly 8 to 9 cm from the midline, which is where the apex beat is felt. The right ventricle is anterior behind the sternum and does not reach the apex. The second intercostal space on the left is the pulmonary area for auscultation, not the apex, and the left atrium is posterior and reaches no surface point.

### Q2
Stem: On a frontal chest radiograph or coronal CT reconstruction, which chamber forms the right heart border, and which great vessel continues that border upward?
A. Right atrium, continued upward by the superior vena cava
B. Right ventricle, continued upward by the pulmonary trunk
C. Left atrium, continued upward by the azygos vein
D. Left ventricle, continued upward by the aortic knob
Answer: A
Objective: III.4
Why: The right heart border is the right atrium, with the superior vena cava carrying the border up toward the mediastinum and the inferior vena cava sometimes visible at the lower corner. The right ventricle is anterior and forms the inferior border on the diaphragm, not the lateral edge. The left atrium is posterior and contributes to neither border. The left ventricle and the aortic knob are on the left border.

### Q3
Stem: Approximately how long is the adult rectum, and between which landmarks is that length measured?
A. About 5 cm, from the anal verge to the dentate line
B. About 25 cm, from the sigmoid colon to the anal verge
C. About 3 to 4 cm, from the puborectalis sling to the anal verge
D. About 12 to 15 cm, from the rectosigmoid junction to the anorectal junction at the puborectalis sling
Answer: D
Objective: III.4
Why: The rectum runs about 12 to 15 cm from the rectosigmoid junction, at about the third sacral segment, down the sacral curve to the anorectal junction at the puborectalis sling. The anal canal is the separate 3 to 4 cm segment below that down to the anal verge. A 5 cm figure is roughly the low-rectal band used to describe tumor height, not the organ length, and 25 cm overshoots by including sigmoid.

### Q4
Stem: A rectal cancer has its lower edge 12 cm from the anal verge. How is the tumor classified by height, and roughly where does the rectum end above it?
A. Low rectal; the rectum ends about 2 cm above the tumor at the pelvic floor
B. High rectal; the rectosigmoid junction lies about 3 cm above the tumor edge at about the third sacral level
C. Mid rectal; the rectum continues another 15 cm upward
D. Sigmoid; a lesion 12 cm from the verge is above the rectum
Answer: B
Objective: III.4
Why: Tumor height is the distance of the lower edge from the anal verge: low under about 5 cm, mid 5 to 10 cm, high 10 to 15 cm, so 12 cm is a high rectal cancer. With the anal canal about 3 to 4 cm and the rectum 12 to 15 cm, the rectosigmoid junction sits about 15 to 18 cm from the verge, a few centimeters above this lesion at about the third sacral segment. A lesion at 12 cm is still rectum, not sigmoid, and the rectum does not extend another 15 cm.

# What “done” means

This bank is the study product, not a sample. A person who works every note and every question should be able to sit the CMD exam.

Public comments on DoseGrid, from candidates who passed, point at the same three things: a walkthrough that shows the arithmetic, a quiz with a full solution, and a full practice exam. Raphex is praised for the length of the explanation. The exam itself is described as fair, current, and close to clinic. It is not a vocabulary test.

## Notes

Every calculation lesson is a worked tutorial:

1. The data a stem would give.
2. The formula, with each symbol defined.
3. The numbers substituted.
4. The arithmetic, checked.
5. What the result means.
6. The mistake that produces each common wrong answer.

Concept lessons still name the exam trap, the number the board expects, and the source. They do not stop at a definition.

## Questions

- Four options. The wrong ones are the errors a candidate actually makes: half-life swapped with mean life, PDD used in an SAD setup, Khan’s electron rule marked when the stem asked for Bentel, a QUANTEC limit applied to the wrong organ.
- The rationale shows the arithmetic. “B, because that is the formula” is not a solution.
- Each formula appears as several different number sets, not one example.
- Minimums: 5 items on a concept lesson, 8 on a quiz or tutorial, and at least 4 of those 8 numeric. Practice Exam 1 and Practice Exam 2 are 40 items each, mixed in the published domain weights.
- Items are original. Retired MDCB items and any commercial quiz stay out of the repo.

## Before a module is pushed

- Every lesson title from `plan/dosegrid-public-curriculum.md` is a heading.
- Every numeric answer has been recomputed.
- The MDCB objective id is on the item.
- Thin sections get rewritten before the commit, not after.
